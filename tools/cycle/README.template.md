# TPDB v0.48: the heads, faces and hair drawn again from a study of two characters, the animals and their limbs redrawn, read a forty-fifth time

v0.47 ended with 63 of the 64 templates through the gate. Sam's brief for v0.48: "go for v0.48 now. continue refining the character art, focusing on a couple of individual characters first in blender and really making them look good, then also fixing things like the quality of the animals and their limbs. I am not a fan of the squashed oval heads, and the faces need fixing. feel free to update the art style to make it easier to make the characters look better, and make sure to check for quality as you are working. I have blender installed so feel free to open that and do work there." The work list came from two places:
- that brief: the heads, the faces, the animals and their limbs, and leave to change the art style;
- v0.47's list (`vision/flaws_v047.md`): the one template that failed its gate, `about_to_trip` (R1), on a walker read as going the other way; which way a side-view figure faces or goes (O1); and the figures over the pond (O2), carried a third time.

The study took two characters, the woman in the yellow dress and the man in the red shirt. Each was built alone in Blender with one change at a time and rendered at twice the release's size, as a sheet of heads with a row at scene size under it. What v0.47's drawing showed on the first sheet: a head wider than tall, a face small in the lower middle of that oval, a fringe straight across the brow with a square corner at each temple, a smile drawn as an angular red shape with a dark edge, and a nose ringed like a letter U.

The work is in the cast (builder 0.48.0) and in two placement rules (revision 45). Each change is behind a switch whose letter O gives v0.47's drawing, so the old and the new could go to a reviewer as a pair. Five blind pair benches, a direction bench and four rounds of cut-outs (`vision/isolation_v048.md`) took these:
- **The head** is an egg, taller than wide, narrowing below the cheeks to a chin that sits a little forward, so there is a jaw from the front and a chin ahead of the neck from the side. One map places the head, its hair, its hats and every feature of the face.
- **The face:** the eyes a little smaller and just under the middle of the head, the brows longer with a clear gap over the eyes and finer on the women and the children, the nose and the mouth lower, the mouth one plain line with no dark edge under it.
- **The hair** is cut along a curve: a parting, a temple that rounds down, a short sideburn, the ear clear on a short cut, a fuller crown. The two women with long hair are parted differently. The beard is a dark chin beard under a short thin moustache.
- **A side view has a face of its own.** As a draughtsman does in a profile, the near eye and its brow are set further round the cheek, the mouth and the moustache likewise, the glasses show their near rim and its arm, and nothing of the far eye is drawn. The head of a side view turns 22 degrees toward the viewer, for v0.47's 48.
- **The art style: one flat tone.** v0.46 and v0.47 shaded each figure with a second, darker tone laid by the surface's normal, and it left a small island wherever a surface folds away, at a trouser's crotch, on a child's shorts, under a jaw. A figure is now flat colour inside its line.
- **The body:** a tee hemmed at the hips and not at the waist, standing feet turned out, the chin a little lower and a child's neck fuller, the women's bare arms slimmer, glasses of a lighter wire with an arm to the ear, smaller ears, a larger bun.
- **Every animal's body and limbs.** The dogs: one body from a rounded rump through a narrow loin and a deep chest into a thick neck; a shoulder, an elbow, a wrist and a paw, and a hip, a stifle, a hock and a paw, each on a bone of its own; a smaller skull with a muzzle that tapers from it; a tapered, curved tail; a collar that is a band of the neck. A sitting dog sits on its haunches with the hind legs folded. The cats: a longer, supple body, a broad skull, large three-sided ears, a long tail and a bright iris with a slit pupil. The goat: a deeper barrel, slim legs on split hooves, a wedge face. The birds and the duck lose the line where a head or a neck passes into the body.
- **The walker about to trip** has two short motion lines behind the back, as the runner has had three since revision 17, and the obstacle stands half a step from the leading toe where it stood a full step off. A ball is no longer one of the obstacles.
- **Placement revision 45, three rules:** that one; a person in front of the pond stands far enough down the picture that the legs are clear of the water; and no animal stands over the garden's hose reel, a rule that came of my own look at the set, where a begging dog sat up with its head on the reel's drum.
- **Put to reviewers or readers and not taken:** a side view's head turned 15 degrees with the front view's face on it ("no eye is visible, so the face looks empty and could be read as the back of the head"); three earlier beards, one of them a soft tone with no line ("reads more like a shadow or dirt than a beard"); a walk drawn as the step itself with the leading foot lifted, which five readers of six took for standing; and the dogs' first new legs, "very long and thin for the body, giving a stilt-like, spidery look".
- **Not changed:** the rest of v0.47's list. The hose's swelling (R2), the lost balloon (R3), the upward water (R4), R5 and R6, the seesaw's riders (O3) and O5 to O9 were not worked and are carried to `vision/flaws_v048.md`.

The set was regenerated and screened a forty-fifth time. Every person and every animal is drawn anew, so no picture is identical to v0.47's.

__INTRO_RESULT__

Everything is built by code and reproducible: the cast is modeled and posed in Blender (Blender 5.2.3 LTS), and the scenes are composed in SVG by the generator in `scenegen/`. From v0.24 the whole cycle also runs on Windows with an installed Blender (`RUNBOOK.md`, Setup).

Source and release history: https://github.com/FoilMiser/Cookie-Theft-Alternative-Scene-Generator- (each release is one commit; `RUNBOOK.md` there says how a release is made).

## The isolation bench

The bench of this release judged drawings more than events, so most of it is pairs: one figure of the library in v0.47's drawing beside the same figure in a trial, as "a" and "b" with the new one drawn at random, and a reviewer who says which is the better drawn and lists the faults of each. Every reviewer and reader was a fresh subagent given its prompt and its pictures and nothing else. The record is `vision/isolation_v048.md`:
- **Three pair benches of the people, sixteen figures each, four reviewers each.** The new drawing was the better in 10, 9 and 12 pairs of 16, v0.47's in 3, 6 and 3.
  - Bench 1, the head, face and hair: "The face reads clearly as a smiling woman with a simple nose and mouth, and the hair falls naturally, whereas a's looped nose and open mouth look odd and the straight fringe is helmet-like; the body is otherwise the same." The three for v0.47 were at the neck, and the reviewers named three things of both drawings alike, which were mended: "the shirt is very short and the trouser waist very high, so the upper body looks short against long legs", "a faint lighter triangular mark on the far thigh, like a stray patch" (the shadow tone), and a moustache "very large and curled, sitting over an open red mouth; slightly comic".
  - Bench 2 lost ground, 9 for 6. The direction bench (below) had turned a side view's head to 15 degrees with the front view's face still on it, and at a reviewer's size that face was nearly gone: "the single eye sits on the very front edge of the face outline, partly cut off". The mend is the side view's own face.
  - Bench 3, with that face: "the profile head looking down at the foot fits the kick and the face is placed consistently on the head; a's face looks pasted onto the side of the head". The three for v0.47 were side views still, a pose that turns the head itself and the glasses, "the glasses hang at the very edge of the head and look partly off the face". Both were mended after it, with the ears and the older woman's bun, and those last mends had no pair bench of their own.
- **The direction bench: eight moving figures, each drawn three ways, at 0.41 of full size, two readers a picture.** `flaws_v047.md` asked for it: which way is the figure going? All 48 answers have the way it goes, in v0.47's drawing too. What differs is how sure the reader was: "sure" in 4 of 16 for v0.47's drawing, 6 of 16 for the new head at v0.47's 48 degrees and 11 of 16 at 15 degrees. Of the turned heads: "the face is close to three-quarter so it is slightly less clear". A figure alone is an easier reading than the screen's, so the bench shows a direction and not a cure.
- **Two pair benches of the animals.** The first drawing was the better in 17 pairs of 20: "it reads clearly as a sitting dog with a proper haunch, hind leg and separate tail, whereas a has a ramp-shaped body with no hind quarters and an over-long neck". The three for v0.47 were all the tan dog standing or walking, its legs "very long and thin for the body, giving a stilt-like, spidery look". With shorter, thicker legs and a shorter muzzle the dogs and cats took 14 pairs of 15, those three among them. The one that went to v0.47 is the dog on the hose, which now errs the other way: "all four legs are thick, straight columns, rather heavy and stumpy for the body; the hind leg has almost no hock bend". It was left there.
- **Four rounds of single events cut out of scenes.**
  - Round 1, seventeen cut-outs: the events with no dog in them read as built, and three remarks on the cats went back to the drawing: "The cat's back legs are long and spindly and the body is long and thin; the pose reads as stalking, but proportions are a bit stretched." The walker about to trip, with v0.47's stride and gap: the way she goes read in three cut-outs of four, and in the fourth "she is actually walking to the right, away from it, which makes the relation unclear" of a walker who faces left.
  - Round 2, a walk that was not taken: the stride drawn as the step itself, the leading foot lifted with its toe up. Five readers of six had the walker standing: "The pose reads two ways: walking forward while reading, or standing still with legs apart and leaning slightly forward." The long stride is what says walking, and it was kept.
  - Round 3, the same six seeds with the long stride, two motion lines and the obstacle half a step off: all six readers have the walker going the way he goes, and all six have the trip: "directly in his path, so he seems about to trip over or bump into it because he is not watching where he goes". What the lines cost: "The speed lines suggest she is moving faster, maybe almost jogging."
  - Round 4, eighteen cut-outs of the animals' events with the final cast: sixteen read as built, among them the begging dog, which "sits up on its hind legs on the grass next to the bench, looking up toward her, as if begging for the ice cream". The stopped hose was hedged as it was in v0.47. The sleeping dog, small and far back, was not read: "A small white animal stands or crouches in the middle of a green lawn, seen from the front." And in one of the two dog walks the walker was again taken to go the other way: "The woman and dog walk in opposite directions (she faces right, the dog faces left), so the leash is pulled between them". Nothing was changed after this round.

**What the bench leaves open.** A walker who faces left was taken to go right in one cut-out of round 1 and one of round 4, as in v0.47's screen; the trip has its motion lines now and the other walkers have none. The side view's face is a draughtsman's cheat, judged in enlarged pairs and in cut-outs before this screen. The dog on the hose lost its pair.

**The blind rule.** From pair bench 2 on a reader was given the path of its prompt file and told to open nothing but that file and the pictures it names. The maps that say which picture is which drawing were kept outside a round's folder while it was read.

## Placement revision 45

Every record stores the placement revision it was made with. v0.48 pictures use revision 45. Its three rules, each behind a switch whose letter O draws revision 44:
- **The walker about to trip** (`TRIP48` B, `scenegen/templates.py`): two short motion lines trail from the walker's back, at chest and hip height, and stand clear of every other thing; the obstacle is 22 to 44 px ahead of the leading toe at full size, where revision 21 had set it a full step off, 45 to 85 px; and a ball is no longer an obstacle, since both bench readers of a ball offered a kick beside the trip. With `TRIP47`'s rule that nothing upright stands behind or ahead of the walker, the template needs a clear stretch of ground, and in the crowded tier-4 street it still finds none: the forced set that holds it placed on 0 seeds of 8.
- **A person in front of the pond** (`POND48` B, `scenegen/scene.py`): the hips, half the figure's height above the feet, lie below the pond's near edge at the figure's own place, so the legs are clear of the water. Revisions 10 and 19 had kept only the feet off the rim, and a grown figure's legs are twice that. v0.47's readers had it in five pictures: "His legs overlap the pond outline, so he could be read as standing at the water's edge or just in front of it."
- **No animal over the hose reel** (`REEL48` B, `scenegen/scene.py`): an animal on the ground has none of the garden's hose reel, or of the hose's end that hangs from it, inside its box. It was found on my own look at the set, before any reader had it: in a tier-2 isomorph the begging dog sat up in front of the reel, its head on the drum, so the drum was a ring behind the head and the hose's end a thing coming from the neck. That picture no longer builds and was taken out; the other twenty-one records of the set rebuild to the pixel with the rule. The calibration was measured before the rule and was left.

A v0.47 record rebuilds with the v0.47 package, and a v0.48 record with v0.48's.

The cast draws every person and every animal again (builder 0.48.0), behind switches read when it is built: `TPDB_HEAD48`, `TPDB_FACE48`, `TPDB_HAIR48`, `TPDB_PROF48`, `TPDB_FLAT48`, `TPDB_TORSO48`, `TPDB_WALK48` and `TPDB_ANIM48` at B, `TPDB_STANCE48` at 16 (degrees) and `TPDB_PROFILE` at -22, which are the code's defaults. The letter O gives v0.47's drawing; with `TPDB_ANIM48` and `TPDB_FLAT48` at O all 60 of v0.47's gallery animals rebuild to the pixel. A pose's render config has one new field, `profile_face` (a side view of that pose is drawn with the side view's own face), and a person's config carries the face states for it, `expression_states_profile`. The renderer takes its scale from `TPDB_PPM` (400 px per metre; the study's sheets were rendered at 800).

## Key words, frequency and age of acquisition


Unchanged from v0.20: every item record carries its key word's Zipf frequency (f, for things and actions only) and its
age of acquisition (a, from the bundled excerpt of Kuperman, Stadthagen-Gonzalez and Brysbaert, 2012) at weight 0;
`scenegen/norms/aoa_kuperman2012.tsv` keeps the citation and source in its header and every record stores its sha256.
The licence note and what the pilot should test are in the v0.20 README section of the same name (the CC BY-NC-ND 3.0
question about shipping an excerpt; the model with AoA against the model without, pre-registered; AoA kept varying apart
from frequency when choosing the pilot's pictures). Before the pilot, check the bundled rows against the original file
(`python -m scenegen.difficulty --check-aoa <the original .xlsx or .csv>`).

```bash
python -m venv .venv && source tools/env.sh      # from Git Bash on Windows too
```

The release is one zip, `tpdb_v0.48.zip`, about __ZIP_MB__ MB. Google Drive does not virus-scan a file over 100 MB, so it asks to confirm the download ("can't scan this file for viruses"); the file downloads as normal after that. `package_release.py` checks, file by file, that the zip holds every file of the release. Without `--single` it writes the same release as zip parts under 50 MB each, with the README and the set's pictures beside them, for browsing in Drive.

Exact rebuilds hold within a release: a v0.47 record rebuilds with the v0.47 package. They also depend on Pillow, pinned in `requirements.txt`: Pillow 13 resamples RGBA differently from 12, and every embedded asset changes. A different cairo moves a few pixels by one level and leaves the SVG identical. Renders from an installed Blender differ from the Linux module's only in sub-pixel anti-aliasing on the outlines, with identical anchors and geometry, so the library mixes the two.
