# Review of pair_4: seated lower body, row a against row b

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the figures lean back. The seat is a low, rounded bulge that hangs at or below knee height, the thighs are short and tilt up toward the knee, and the lower legs slant well forward, so the feet sit far ahead of the knees. Together this reads as sliding off a seat or half-squatting against a wall. In row b the torso is upright, the seat and thighs form a flat, level shelf at knee height, and the lower legs drop almost straight down with the feet just ahead of the knees. That reads at once as sitting on an ordinary chair, in the large drawings and in the small ones.

## 2. Figure by figure

- **Child in shorts: b.** In b the shorts make a level lap and the shins drop to the floor like a child on a chair; in a the shorts sag into a round lump below the knees and the shins slant forward as if he is slipping off.
- **Girl in dress: b.** In b the dress makes a flat lap with the knees at seat height; in a the dress bulges down behind into a rounded bottom and her body tilts back, so she looks like she is reclining on nothing.
- **Woman reading: b.** In b the dress hem makes a level lap and the legs come down under the knee; in a the very long, steeply slanted shins put her feet far forward, like someone perched on a tall stool or leaning against a wall.
- **Older woman in skirt: b.** In b the skirt sits level and the shins are close to vertical, a calm and believable chair pose; in a the shin is much longer than the visible thigh and slants far forward, so the leg looks stretched and the pose looks unstable.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All four: the torso leans back and nothing under the body would hold that pose; with no seat drawn, the figures look as if they are falling backward or sliding forward.
- Child, girl: the seat is a rounded bulge that hangs lower than the knees, so the thighs run uphill to the knee. That reads as a sagging bottom, not a lap.
- Woman reading, older woman: the shin is much longer than the visible thigh and slants strongly forward. The legs look over-long and the feet end up well in front of the body.
- All four: the near and far shoes sit almost on top of each other, offset only slightly, so they read as one thick double shoe rather than two feet.
- All four: the sharp turn from the slanted shin to the flat shoe makes a stiff, bent-back ankle.
- All four: the inner line of the far leg runs inside the near shin as a thin second contour, which looks like a crease or a split leg.
- In the small drawings, every figure reads as leaning back in mid-air, not sitting.

### Row b
- Child: there is a lighter orange band between the shirt and the shorts that looks like a separate strip or a gap, not part of the shorts.
- Child, woman reading: the underside of the seat is very flat and squared, so the shorts and dress look like a box or cushion, which is a little stiff.
- Older woman: the skirt is a heavy, wide slab compared with the thin shins, so the lower body looks top-heavy.
- All four: the thighs look thick and short next to the shins. This is acceptable for a cartoon but slightly chunky.
- All four: the near and far shoes are staggered and read as two feet, but the far shoe is cut off rather abruptly behind the near one.
- Nothing else looks clearly wrong. The hands rest on the knees properly in every figure.

## 4. Choice

**Row b.** The level lap, upright back and near-vertical shins give a clear, friendly "sitting on a chair" pose that still reads at picture size. Row a reads as leaning or sliding at both sizes. Confidence: **sure**.
