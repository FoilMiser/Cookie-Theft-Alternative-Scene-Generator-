# Review of pair_7: seated lower body, row a against row b

## 1. How the rows differ

In the two front-view swing figures the rows look almost the same: short foreshortened thighs under the shorts or skirt, round knees, and straight lower legs hanging down to the feet. Row b's girl has a wider, flatter skirt over her lap, and row b's child has a darker orange band between the shirt and the shorts. The big difference is in the two side-view slide figures. In row a the thighs slope steeply down and forward from a torso that leans back, so the leg reads as one long diagonal. In row b the torso is upright, the thighs are level with a flat underside, and the lower legs angle down from a clear knee, so the figure reads as sitting.

## 2. Per figure

1. **Child on swing, front:** a. The legs are drawn the same in both rows, but row b adds a darker orange stripe under the shirt that looks like a stray band.
2. **Girl on swing, front:** no difference. Thighs, knees, lower legs and feet match. Row b's skirt is wider at the hips and row a's is slimmer, and neither one is clearly better.
3. **Child on slide, side:** b. The level thigh and the bent knee in row b show a child sitting on the slide; row a's steeply sloping thigh makes the child look like they are falling or jumping backwards.
4. **Girl on slide, side:** b. In row b the skirt makes a level lap with a flat seat line and a clear knee; in row a the thigh and shin form nearly one straight slope and there is no clear lap.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Child on slide (side): the thigh slopes down at a steep angle, so there is no level lap and no clear seat under the hips. The bend at the knee is shallow and the leg reads as one long diagonal. With the torso leaning back, the pose looks like a fall or a jump, not sitting.
- Girl on slide (side): the same problem. The pink skirt runs diagonally down to the knee and then the shin carries on at almost the same angle. The hip has no flat underside, so nothing tells you she is sitting on anything.
- Child on swing (front): the thighs are so short that the shorts are only a thin band with the knee caps inside it, and the lower legs look very long under them. This is slight.
- Girl on swing (front): the slim skirt with a curved hem and the long straight lower legs make her read a little like a standing figure. This is slight.

### Row b
- Child on swing (front): there is a darker orange band between the yellow shirt and the orange shorts. It looks like a stray stripe or a colour seam.
- Child on slide (side): the same darker orange band shows at the bottom of the shirt. The shorts are a large, very square block, which looks stiff.
- Girl on swing (front): the skirt flares out wider than the torso at the hips, which makes the lower body look a little heavy.
- Girl on slide (side): the skirt over the thigh is a flat rectangular slab with a hard flat bottom edge, which looks stiff. The lower legs are long next to the short thigh.
- Both slide figures: the feet are at the same far-forward spot as in row a, so the shins are long and stretched forward.

### Anything else
- In both rows the slide figures reach far forward with both arms, so in row a the whole body becomes one curve leaning back. This makes row a's pose read even less like sitting.
- At picture size, row a's slide figures look like they are leaping. Row b's look like children sitting.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. On the two front figures the rows are about even, and row b's orange band is a small flaw that could be fixed. On the two side figures, row b's level thighs and clear knees read as sitting at a glance, even at picture size. Row a's sloping legs do not read that way.
