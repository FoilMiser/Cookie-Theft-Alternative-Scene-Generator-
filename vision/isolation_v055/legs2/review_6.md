# Review of pair_6 (seated lower body)

## 1. How the rows differ

In the front-view swing figures, row a draws the lap as a short band with the knees shown as rounded caps poking up in front of the shorts or skirt hem. Row b draws a taller, boxier lap where the shorts or skirt fall straight across and the knees are only faint lines inside the cloth. In the side-view slide figures, row a has an upright torso, a flat squared-off seat and nearly horizontal thighs, so the body is a stiff L. Row b has a rounded seat, a torso leaning slightly back and thighs that slope down toward the knee, so the body is a smooth curve.

## 2. Figure by figure

- Child on swing, front: **a**. The knee caps rising in front of the orange shorts make it clear that the thighs come toward the viewer and the child is sitting. In b the shorts are a flat box with the legs hanging out underneath.
- Girl on swing, front: **a**. The tan knees showing over the pink hem read as a seated lap. In b the skirt hangs straight across the legs, which looks closer to a standing girl in a short dress.
- Child on slide, side: **b**. The rounded seat, backward lean and downward-sloping thighs look like a child sliding. Row a's flat-bottomed, upright L-shape looks stiff, like a sit-up hold.
- Girl on slide, side: **b**. Same reason: the dress curves round the seat and the line from hip to feet flows. In a the dress makes a hard rectangle with a sharp corner at the hip.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Child on slide: the seat is a flat, square back edge with no rounding of the buttocks. The torso sits at a near right angle to horizontal thighs, which looks rigid. The knee is an abrupt corner where the shorts end and the lower leg starts.
- Girl on slide: the dress over the thighs is a stiff horizontal block with a sharp hip corner. The lower legs start abruptly at the hem, so the knee is hard to read.
- Child on swing: the thighs are very short. The knee caps sit slightly above the shorts' lower edge, so they look a little pasted on.
- Girl on swing: the hem line runs behind the knees and is partly hidden, so the edge of the lap is slightly unclear.
- Feet (all four): fine. The swing feet sit squarely under the shins, and the slide shoes are stacked at the end of the legs. They are the same in both rows.

### Row b
- Child on swing: the shorts are a wide, deep box, and the knees show only as faint inner lines. The legs drop straight out of the bottom, so the seated pose is weaker.
- Girl on swing: the skirt falls straight down over the thighs and hides the knees. Her pose reads ambiguously, nearly standing.
- Child on slide: there is a short stretch of bare thigh between the shorts' hem and the knee, and it joins a bit unevenly. The knee bend is soft and slightly vague.
- Girl on slide: the hem ends just past the knee with a small step in the outline where the lower legs come out. Otherwise it is clean.
- Feet: no difference from row a.

### Other observations
- In both rows the two shins of each slide figure are drawn as nearly identical parallel shapes, so near and far legs are hard to tell apart.
- At picture size, the front figures look almost identical between the rows. The slide figures show the difference clearly: a is an L, b is a curve.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. The side-view slide figures are clearly friendlier and more natural in b, and that difference is visible at picture size. Row a's advantage on the front-view swing figures is small and hard to see at picture size.
