# Review of pair 9 (seated lower body, seesaw riders)

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the two readers sit very low. A large rounded seat sits near the level of the feet, the thighs climb steeply up to knees tucked under the book hands, and the shins slant down and forward to the feet. The two children have nearly level thighs and shins folded back under them. In row b the readers sit as if on a chair: the thighs run level from the hip to the knee, the shins drop straight down and the feet are under the knees. The two children have long, steep thighs and near-vertical shins, so the whole figure is stretched tall.

## 2. Per figure

- **Bearded man reading: b.** The level thigh, the knee under the hands and the vertical shin read at once as sitting. Row a's big seat bulge with steep thighs looks more like crouching or sitting on the floor.
- **Older man reading: b.** Same reason. The bend at the hip and knee is clear in b. In a, the dark trouser blob and the long diagonal leg read as a lump on the ground.
- **Girl, legs hanging: a.** The dress runs nearly level from the hip to the knee and the shins fold down and back, so she clearly sits on something. In b the thigh is steep and the body is drawn long, so she looks like she is standing or jumping.
- **Child, legs hanging: a.** The shorts are almost level with a clear knee bend and dangling shins. In b the steep shorts and straight-down shins make the child look like they are dropping or hopping, not seated.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Bearded man: the seat is an oversized rounded bulge that sits almost as low as the feet and juts out behind the back. It reads as kneeling or squatting on the ground.
- Bearded man: the thighs climb steeply into the hands, so no knee shows. The leg looks like one long diagonal from the seat to the feet.
- Bearded man: the two shins and feet are stacked almost on top of each other at the far right, so the legs look stretched out rather than bent.
- Older man: the same oversized dark seat lump at the bottom left, with no clear hip joint under the vest.
- Older man: no knee shows; it is hidden under the hands and the long diagonal shin runs far forward to the feet.
- Girl: the shins bend back under the seat at a sharp angle, and the feet point down and back, which looks a little cramped.
- Girl: the two knees overlap, so it is hard to tell the near leg from the far one.
- Child: the shins fold back under the body and the feet point back and down. The far leg is mostly hidden behind the near one.

### Row b
- Bearded man: the thigh is short and the knee sits right under the forearm and the book, so the knee is partly covered.
- Bearded man: the seat bulge under the hip is small but floats below the thigh line, and with no seat drawn it hangs in air.
- Older man: the trouser seat is a dark lump that bulges below the vest. The shins are very straight and parallel, like a chair pose.
- Girl: the thigh is steep and long, so the sitting bend is weak. The figure looks taller than in a and close to standing or jumping.
- Girl: the dress hem reaches far down the thigh, which lengthens the body. The feet hang straight down and are splayed slightly apart.
- Child: the shorts slope steeply and the lower body is long, so the child looks like they are hopping down rather than sitting.
- Child: the feet are large and point down at an angle, which adds to the falling look.

### Other things that look wrong
- In both rows the girl and the child hold their hands up to their mouths with a raised flat palm. That is the same in both rows, but it is an odd gesture for someone holding on to a seesaw, because nothing is being gripped.
- The small versions keep the same reading: at picture size row a's men look like they are squatting on the ground and row b's children look like they are standing.

## 4. Choice

**Row b, toss-up.** Row b fixes the two readers, whose row a pose with the big low seat is the strongest fault on the sheet. But row a draws both hanging-leg children better, so the split is two figures each.
