# Review of pair_4.png (seated lower body, rows a and b)

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the front-view figures draw the knees as rounded outlines inside the shorts or skirt, with a flat band along the bottom of the clothing that the lower legs hang out from under; the side-view figures have a long, nearly level thigh, a square corner at the back of the seat and only a slight bend at the knee. In row b the front-view knees poke out just below the hem as round caps and the lower legs start straight from them, and the side-view figures lean further back, with a rounded seat, a shorter thigh that slopes down, and the knee placed at the hem.

## 2. Figure by figure (left to right)

- **Child on swing, front:** b. The knees come out cleanly at the hem of the shorts and the shins hang straight from them, while row a's knee outlines inside the shorts and the flat band under them look like a box with legs fixed below it.
- **Girl on swing, front:** b. The skirt hem lies over the knees and the legs fall from there, while row a draws knee shapes through the skirt with a second flat ledge under the hem, so the lap looks layered.
- **Child on slide, side:** b. The rounded seat and the visible knee bend at the shorts hem read as a seated child, while row a's square back corner and almost straight leg look stiff.
- **Girl on slide, side:** no difference. Row a has a clearer level thigh but a hard square seat corner and a knee hidden under the dress; row b has a softer seat but a short thigh, a slanted hem and very long shins. Neither is clearly better.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Child on swing, front: the knees are drawn as outlines inside the shorts, and the thigh shape doesn't continue past them.
- Child on swing, front: there is a flat shaded band under the shorts, and the shins come out from behind it as if fixed under a shelf rather than growing from the knees.
- Girl on swing, front: the knee outlines show through the skirt, and a second flat ledge sits under the hem, so the lap reads as two stacked layers.
- Girl on swing, front: the shins start under the ledge and are set apart from the knee shapes above them.
- Child on slide, side: the back of the seat is a sharp square corner, not a rounded bottom.
- Child on slide, side: the thigh is long and almost level, and the knee bend is so small that the leg looks nearly straight and stiff.
- Child on slide, side: the knee itself is not marked; the shorts just end and the shin goes on.
- Girl on slide, side: the same square seat corner and long, level, blocky thigh, and the knee is hidden under the dress with no bend shown.
- Small size: in both front figures the lap looks like a wide flat block.

### Row b
- Child on swing, front: the knee caps that bulge under the hem are a little large and round.
- Child on swing, front: the shins are long next to the very short visible thigh.
- Girl on swing, front: the skirt hem is a plain straight edge, and the knees only show as small bumps under it.
- Child on slide, side: the thigh is short and wide, mostly a blob of shorts.
- Child on slide, side: the shin is long next to the thigh, so the leg looks out of proportion.
- Child on slide, side: the torso leans back a lot, which reads as nearly lying down or falling backward.
- Girl on slide, side: the dress hem cuts across on a slant, and the thigh is short.
- Girl on slide, side: the shins are very long, and no knee bend shows at the hem.
- Girl on slide, side: the strong lean back also makes her look like she is tipping over.
- Both side figures at small size: they read as a body tilted back with long legs, a little less like sitting than row a.

## 4. Choice

Row b, fairly sure. Its front figures are clearly cleaner, with the knees and shins following on naturally from the clothing and no flat ledge. Its side figures have softer, more cartoon-friendly seats, but their short thighs, long shins and strong lean back are real weaknesses.
