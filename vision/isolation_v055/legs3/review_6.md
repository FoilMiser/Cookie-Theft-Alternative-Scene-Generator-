# Review of pair_6 (seated lower body, rows a and b)

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the hips are rounded and narrow, with no flat underside. The thighs slope steeply down and forward from the hip, and the shins angle further forward, so the feet end up well in front of the knees and the figures look as if they are perched or sliding off. In row b each figure has a broad, flat-bottomed seat shape (a horizontal shelf of dress, skirt or shorts), and the thighs run roughly level toward the viewer. The shins drop close to vertical, so the feet sit below or just ahead of the knees, and the figure reads as sitting on something.

## 2. Per figure (left to right)

1. Woman in yellow dress, reading: **b**. Her flat dress hem and level thighs show a clear seat, while in row a she looks like she is leaning back on nothing with her legs reaching forward.
2. Older woman in skirt, ice cream: **b**. The flat skirt base and level thighs make her clearly seated; row a has a small rounded bottom, sloping thighs and feet far forward, so she looks like she is half standing.
3. Child in shorts, ice cream: **b**. Row b gives the shorts a flat seat and puts the feet under the knees; in row a the thighs droop and the feet are pushed forward, so the pose looks slumped.
4. Girl in pink dress, drink: **b**. Row a is the weakest drawing in the set: her torso tilts back hard over a tiny rounded hip and the legs dangle forward, so she looks like she is falling backward. Row b at least plants her on a flat seat.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All four: the bottom is rounded with no flat seat surface, so nothing shows that the figure is resting on a seat; at small size they look like they are hovering.
- All four: the thighs slope steeply downward, so the knees sit well below the hips and the lap does not read as a lap.
- All four: the shins angle forward and the feet land far in front of the knees, which reads as sliding forward or bracing, not sitting.
- Woman in yellow: the dress drapes over the thighs to a point near the knees, giving a narrow wedge for the whole lower body; the torso leans back over it.
- Older woman: she has only a small sliver of skirt under the hip; the thighs are thin and slanted, so she looks like she is leaning on a stool edge.
- Child: the shorts bunch into lumpy shapes at the knees, the knees look knobbly, and the pose is slumped.
- Girl in pink: her torso leans far back over a very small hip; the legs are long, thin and hang forward. The overall impression is of falling backward. This is the clearest fault in the image.

### Row b
- All four: the seat is drawn as a hard, flat-bottomed block with squared corners that sticks out behind the figure. It looks like a box or cushion strapped to the hips more than a body on a chair, and with no seat drawn it looks odd.
- All four: the thighs are strongly foreshortened and short, while the shins are long, so the legs look stretched below the knee (most clearly in the two adult women).
- Woman in yellow: the dress hem makes a wide rectangular shelf that is wider than her shoulders on the left side.
- Older woman: the skirt reads as a separate slate-blue box, with a visible step between it and the cardigan.
- Child: the thighs are very short; the knees and shins look bigger than the thighs, and the feet are splayed wide apart.
- Girl in pink: the dress base is a flat slab with a sharp corner at the back; her torso still leans back somewhat, though much less than in row a.
- All four: the feet turn outward and spread apart a little, which is acceptable but slightly duck-footed.

### Other things that look wrong (both rows)
- The girl in pink leans back in both rows more than the other figures, and her arm holding the cup is short and stiff.
- The older woman's lower legs are very long and thin compared with her upper body in both rows.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. It reads clearly as "sitting" at both large and small size, which matters most for a friendly, clear cartoon. Its blocky seat shape is a fault, but a smaller one than row a's look of hovering or sliding, especially for the girl in pink.
