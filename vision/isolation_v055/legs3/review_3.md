# Review of pair_3: seated lower body, row a vs row b

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the figures sink low at the hips and lean back. The seat of the body is a rounded bulge hanging below the hip line. The thighs rise toward high knees, and the lower legs slant well forward, so the feet are planted far in front of the knees. In row b the seat of the body has a flat bottom and the thighs run level. The knees are bent at about a right angle, and the lower legs drop almost straight down to feet just in front of the knees. The torso is upright, which reads as sitting on an ordinary chair.

## 2. Per figure, left to right

1. Child in shorts: **b**. The level thighs and near-vertical shins read at once as sitting on a chair, while in row a the shorts sag into a low lump and the child looks as if it is sliding off something or squatting.
2. Girl in a dress: **b**. The flat-bottomed dress over level thighs gives a clear seated shape, while in row a the dress slopes down and back from the knees and the body tips backward with no visible support.
3. Woman reading: **b**. The level lap gives the book-reading pose a stable base, while in row a the dress hangs like a flat sheet below the hips and the long forward-slanting shins make her look as if she is reclining.
4. Older woman in a skirt: **b**. Her seated posture is clearly upright and settled, while in row a the skirt sags below the hip and the shins splay far forward, which looks stiff and off balance.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All four: the torso leans back and the hips sit low behind the knees. With no seat drawn, the figures look as if they would tip over backward or are sliding off a seat.
- All four: the knees sit high, about level with the hips or above, so the thighs slope upward. It looks more like squatting on a low stool, or sitting on the floor with the knees up, than sitting on a chair.
- All four: the lower legs are long and slant strongly forward. The feet land well ahead of the knees, which makes the legs look stiff and propped out.
- Child: the shorts form a rounded sack whose lowest point is under the hip, below the line of the thigh. It reads as a sagging bottom, not a seat resting on something.
- Girl: the bottom edge of the dress runs diagonally down and back from the knee to a low rounded rump, so the lap and the seat of the body run together into one tilted blob.
- Woman reading: the dress hangs as a flat panel with a straight vertical hem at the knee and no sign of a lap under the book. The thigh does not read under the cloth.
- Older woman: the skirt bulges below the hip, and the thigh and knee show only as thin lines. The knee where her hands rest looks bony and high.
- At picture size, all four read as crouching or reclining, not clearly as seated.

### Row b
- Child and girl: the seat of the body is a hard-cornered rectangle, with a flat bottom and squared back corners. It looks box-like and a little stiff.
- Child: the shorts box is deep and wide compared with the short stretch of thigh that shows beyond it, so the hips look bulky.
- Woman reading: the dress forms a large flat-bottomed block whose back corner sticks out behind the torso. The hem at the knee is cut off squarely.
- Older woman: the grey skirt is a puffy, rounded, wide shape that looks like a cushion or pouf she is sitting on, rather than a skirt over her thighs.
- All four: the far leg is set a little forward and lower than the near leg, so the two shoes overlap in a stepped pair. This is fine, but the far shin shows as a thin sliver beside the near one.
- All four: the lower legs are still slightly long for the thigh length, though much less than in row a.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, sure. It gives each figure a level lap, right-angle knees and feet under the knees, which reads at once as "sitting on a chair", even at picture size. Its faults (boxy hips, a cushion-like skirt on the older woman) are smaller and easier to fix than row a's tipped-back, off-balance posture.
