# Review of pair_12 (seated lower body, rows a and b)

## 1. How the rows differ

Row a draws a boxy L-shaped sit: a flat-bottomed seat, level thighs, a near right-angle knee, lower legs that drop straight down, and feet flat on the floor under the knees. Row b uses one smooth curve: the bottom is rounded, the thighs slope down from hip to knee, the lower legs slant forward, and the feet sit well in front of the knees. Row a reads as sitting on a chair; row b reads as perching on an edge or sliding forward.

## 2. Per figure (left to right)

1. Man reading: **a**. The level thigh and flat bottom show plainly that he sits on a seat. In b the down-sloping thigh and forward-thrust feet make him look like he is slipping off something, and at picture size he seems to hang in the air.
2. Bearded man: **a**. The hands resting on a level knee look settled. In b the thigh slopes down so steeply that the pose looks like a half-squat, not a sit.
3. Worker in vest: **a**. Same as figure 1: in a the lap that holds the book is level and stable. In b the lap drops away under the book, and the small version looks like it is floating.
4. Older man: **a**. Row a is clean. In b a dark ring of trouser or belt sticks out around the waist below the vest like a tyre, and the sloped thigh again reads as sliding.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All figures: the joints are stiff and mechanical. The hip and the knee both bend at close to 90 degrees, so the figures look like posed mannequins and not like relaxed people.
- All figures: the seat underside is flat and squared off. It looks like a block, with little sense of a rounded bottom.
- All figures: the lower legs widen slightly toward the ankle, which gives a mild flared-trouser look.
- Figures 1 and 3: the two shoes are offset, and the far shoe pokes out slightly in front of the near one. This reads a little oddly, as a stacked double foot.
- Figure 4: the trouser seat sticks out a little behind the vest's lower edge, a small bump at the back of the hip.

### Row b
- All figures: the thighs slope down steeply from hip to knee. With no seat drawn, nothing looks like it could support them. A flat seat would touch only the back of the bottom, so the figure would look like it is slipping off.
- All figures: the bottom is rounded with no flat contact area, so the sit has no clear point of support.
- All figures: the lower legs slant forward and the feet sit far in front of the knees. This is a lounging pose and makes the figure's footprint longer.
- All figures: each pair of feet is drawn as one merged shoe shape with a thick sole, and the two feet are hard to tell apart.
- At picture size (all four), the figures read as hovering or doing a wall-sit more than sitting.
- Figure 4: a dark flared rim around the waist, under the vest, sticks out front and back like a skirt or tyre. This is the clearest drawing error in either row.
- Figure 2: the thigh-to-shin line is close to one continuous diagonal, so the knee bend is weak.

### Other
- Nothing outside the lower body looks wrong in either row. The heads, arms, books and hands match between the rows.

## 4. Choice

**Row a**, fairly sure. Row b's curves are softer and more organic, but they make the sit unclear and look precarious. Row a shows plainly that each person is seated, which matters most for clear pictures. Its stiffness could be eased by rounding the bottom and knee a little, without giving up the level thigh.
