# Review of pair_4 (seated lower body, side view)

## 1. How the rows differ

Row a draws a chair-height sit: a flat-bottomed seat, thighs level, knees at about a right angle, shins close to upright and the feet set under the knees. Row b gives the seat a rounded, tipped-back underside sitting lower than the knees, so the thighs slope up to the knee, the shins lean well forward and the feet land far in front of the knees. In row b the figures look slumped or slid forward, as if on a low lounger or hovering with nothing beneath them.

## 2. Per figure

1. Child in shorts: **a**. The level thigh and upright shin read straight away as sitting on a chair, while in b the rounded shorts and forward-thrust shins make him look as if he is tipping backwards.
2. Girl in dress: **a**. The flat dress bottom and square knee give a clear sit, while in b the dress curves up into a raised knee and the long, slanted shin looks slouched.
3. Woman reading: **a**. Her upright, attentive reading pose suits a's square sit; in b the backward-leaning torso, sloped lap and far-forward feet look like lounging and leave her seat with no visible support.
4. Older woman in skirt: **a**, but narrowly. a's skirt bulges like a cushion, yet the sit is still clear; in b the long bare thigh slopes up and the feet sit far forward, so a short, upright older figure looks sprawled.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All figures: the seat is a hard-edged flat box, so the bottom looks like a block rather than a body pressed on a seat (strongest in figure 2, pink dress, and figure 3, yellow dress).
- Figure 1 (child): the shins slant slightly back, so the feet sit a little behind the knee; the two shins overlap almost completely and look thin next to the bulky shorts.
- Figure 2 (girl): the shins also tilt back slightly, and the near and far legs merge into one shape with a double line at the knee.
- Figure 3 (reader): the dress hem ends in a straight vertical cut at the knee, and the knee bump under it is awkward; the shins taper sharply to small ankles.
- Figure 4 (older woman): the skirt balloons into a rounded, puffy mass that reads like a cushion or pouf she sits on rather than a skirt; the near and far legs are drawn with crossing outlines that are hard to separate at the knee.
- Feet in all four: the two shoes are stacked with a small offset, which is fine but a little stiff.

### Row b
- All figures: the bottom is a rounded, tilted shape with no flat underside, so nothing shows the weight resting on a seat; at picture size the figures look as if they float or are falling backwards.
- All figures: the knees sit higher than the hips and the shins lean far forward, with the feet well ahead of the knees, giving a slumped, slid-forward sit.
- Figure 1 (child): the shorts are a round bulb, and the hands reach forward to the raised knees, so the arms look stretched.
- Figure 2 (girl): the dress sweeps up in a curve to the knee and the shin is very long and slanted; the legs look longer than a child's should.
- Figure 3 (reader): the torso leans back while she reads, which together with the sloped lap looks like reclining; there is a small stray tick mark on the dress near the lower front.
- Figure 4 (older woman): a long stretch of bare thigh between the skirt and the knee looks odd for a skirt; the shins are long and splayed forward, which does not suit an older figure sitting upright with hands on knees.
- Feet in all four: the shoes are pushed far forward and look large relative to the slanted shins.

## 4. Choice

**Row a**, fairly sure. Its square, chair-height sit reads clearly at picture size and fits a friendly cartoon style. Row b's rounded, sloping seat and forward-thrust legs look slumped and unsupported. Row a's main weakness is the boxy seat (and figure 4's cushion-like skirt), which could be softened without changing the pose.
