# Review: pair_1 (paint roller, rows a and b)

## 1. How the rows differ

All eight figures hold a paint roller on a handle. In row a the roller is held low and level at waist height with both hands close together near the belt, the pink roller lying sideways out in front at hip level. In row b the handle is held across the chest and angled up and forward, so a blue roller stands upright at head height, as if pressed against a wall.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in purple top**
- a: stands straight, both hands at the hip, holding a short handle level; pink roller sticks out sideways at waist height.
- b: stands straight, hands at chest and shoulder, handle angled up; blue roller upright beside her head.
- Better: **b**. The raised handle and upright roller read at once as painting a wall; row a's roller just hangs in the air at the hip.

**Man in red shirt**
- a: slightly hunched, hands at the hip on a level handle; pink roller sideways at waist height.
- b: upright, one hand at the chest and one forward, handle angled up; blue roller beside his head.
- Better: **b**. Same reason: the action reads as reaching up to paint, and the arms look purposeful rather than slack.

**Older man in cap**
- a: stooped slightly, hands at the hip on a level handle; pink roller sideways at waist height.
- b: upright, front hand high on the handle, handle angled up; blue roller at head height.
- Better: **b**. The pose reads as active painting; in row a the hands and handle get lost against the dark vest and trousers.

**Older woman in cardigan**
- a: upright, hands together at waist on a level handle; pink roller sideways at hip height.
- b: upright, arm reaching forward and up to the handle; blue roller beside her head.
- Better: **b**. The reach is clear and the roller sits where a wall would be; row a's grip is tucked against the cardigan and hard to see.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- All four: the roller is held level at waist height pointing sideways, which is not how anyone paints; it reads more like pushing a rolling pin, a tray or a small cart than painting.
- All four: the handle is very short and mostly hidden by the hands; the frame wraps under the roller like a tray, so at the small size the tool looks like a pink box on a bar.
- All four: the two hands sit close together low at the hip, one under the other, and look limp; the rear hand is partly hidden and its arm is unclear.
- Older man in cap: the hands and dark handle merge into the brown vest and black trousers; the grip is hard to make out at both sizes.
- Older woman in cardigan: the hands are tucked against the teal cardigan and the handle disappears into it.
- Man in red shirt and older man: slight forward stoop makes them look tired rather than working.

**Row b**
- All four: the roller stands upright (axis vertical) at the end of an angled bracket, so at the small size it can read as a blue can, bottle or flask hanging on a hook rather than a roller.
- All four: the roller floats beside the head with nothing behind it; without a wall drawn the target is implied only.
- Older man in cap: the rear arm (cream sleeve) hangs down beside the vest while the front hand holds the pole, and a second hand near the chest is hard to find; the grip looks partly one-handed.
- Man in red shirt: the lower hand at the chest overlaps the shirt and the handle and is a little muddled at the small size.
- Older woman in cardigan: her reaching arm crosses the teal body and the grip near the chest is hard to separate from the cardigan.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. The raised, angled handle and roller at head height read clearly as painting a wall at both sizes, and the poses look active and friendly; row a's waist-level sideways roller reads as some other tool. Row b would be stronger still if the roller were turned to lie level (axis horizontal) so it cannot be mistaken for a can.
