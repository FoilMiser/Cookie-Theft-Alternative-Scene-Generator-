# Review of pair 9 (grey cat, ginger cat)

## 1. How the rows differ

The heads, colours and tails are almost the same in both rows; the difference is the body and legs. Row a draws a low, creeping cat: the back slopes down from high hips to the head, one hind leg is pushed far back and one front leg reaches far forward. Row b draws a cat standing or walking with a level back and all four legs set more or less under the body, with bent, bulky legs.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Grey cat (left)**
- Row a: crouched low with high hips, hind leg stretched back, front leg reaching forward, as if creeping or stalking.
- Row b: level back, four legs under the body, bent at the knees, standing or mid-step.
- Better: **a**. The pose is clear and reads at once as a creeping cat at both sizes, and the leg outlines are clean; row b's legs are lumpy and carry stray marks.

**Ginger cat (right)**
- Row a: same creeping pose as the grey cat: hips high, hind leg back, front leg reaching.
- Row b: same level-backed standing pose as the grey cat, legs bent under the body.
- Better: **a**. Same reasons: a clear pose with a readable silhouette, while in b the legs bunch into a row of four similar bent shapes with small stray strokes on them.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- Grey cat: the reaching front leg is very long and flat, close to a stiff noodle, and the elbow joint is unclear.
- Grey cat: the white chest patch shows up as a sharp wedge between the front legs, so it looks like a gap more than fur.
- Grey cat: the far front leg (darker) is mostly hidden, so at the small size the cat reads as having three legs.
- Ginger cat: the same long, stiff reaching front leg and the same chest wedge as the grey cat.
- Ginger cat: the pale chest patch is close to the background colour, so at the small size it reads as a hole in the body.
- Both: the eye sits right at the front edge of the head with the nose and whiskers squeezed against it, so the face is cramped and hard to read when small.

**Row b**
- Grey cat: small stray strokes or notches on the hind leg and front leg near the knees look like drawing errors.
- Grey cat: the legs are thick and rubbery with odd bends at the ankles; the hind leg in particular looks swollen.
- Grey cat: at the small size the four feet sit in an even row and blur together, and the pose reads as a stiff stand more than any action.
- Ginger cat: the same stray strokes on the legs and the same thick, rubbery legs as the grey cat.
- Ginger cat: the darker far legs merge with the near legs at the small size, so the feet form one lumpy band.
- Both: the same cramped face at the front edge of the head as in row a.

## 4. Choice

Row **a**, fairly sure. Its pose is distinct and clear at both sizes, and its lines are clean. Row b looks calmer, but its legs are lumpy, carry stray marks and blur together when small.
