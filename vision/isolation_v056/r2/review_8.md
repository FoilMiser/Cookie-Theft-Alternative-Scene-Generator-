# Review of pair_8

## 1. How the rows differ

Row a draws all four figures kneeling on both knees with their shins flat on the ground behind them. They lean forward and hold a small pink-flowered seedling by the stem, with a brown root ball hanging under the hand. Row b draws them in a half-kneel, with the back knee down, the front knee up and the front foot flat. They bend low and push a small grey trowel point-down toward the ground with both arms.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in purple top**
- a: kneeling on both knees, leaning forward, holding out a seedling with its root ball.
- b: half-kneeling, bent low, pushing a trowel down with straight arms.
- Better: **b**. The half-kneel has a clear front foot and back knee and reads at once as working the ground. Row a's two flat shins merge into one long teal shape.

**Man in red shirt**
- a: kneeling on both knees, leaning forward, holding out a seedling.
- b: half-kneeling, bent forward, pushing a trowel down.
- Better: **b**. The legs separate cleanly and the pose looks active. In row a the hip-to-knee block is stiff and the far leg is mostly lost behind the near one.

**Older man in cap**
- a: kneeling on both knees, leaning forward, holding out a seedling.
- b: half-kneeling, bent forward, pushing a trowel down.
- Better: **b**. The dark trousers keep their shape better as two separate legs in the half-kneel. In row a they turn into one dark mass at the small size.

**Boy in red cap**
- a: kneeling on both knees, leaning forward, holding out a seedling.
- b: half-kneeling, bent low, pushing a trowel down.
- Better: **no difference**. Both read clearly. Row a is calmer and its seedling is more visible, but row b's pose has more life, and the two balance out.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All figures: the two shins lie flat and overlap, so the legs read as one long block ending in two shoes. This is hardest to make out for the man in red and the older man.
- All figures: the thigh rises almost straight up from the knee, which makes the kneel look stiff and wooden.
- All figures: at the small size the root ball is a lone brown dot under the hand. It could be read as a ball or a stone rather than part of the plant.
- Older man: a dark waistband sticks out at the back of the hip.
- Boy: the bottom edge of the hoodie is ragged and a hood string hangs loose. Both are a little untidy.

### Row b
- All figures: the arms are very long and straight, reaching down to ground level. They look stretched.
- All figures: there is a small red/orange mark where the trowel handle meets the fingers, which looks like a stray spot.
- All figures: at the small size the trowel is a tiny grey triangle that is hard to recognise as a tool.
- Woman: the hip and back area is lumpy, and the hunch is quite deep.
- Older man: the dark belt or waistband juts out behind the hip.
- Boy: the hood strings dangle and clutter the area in front of the chest.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. The half-kneel is easier to read, separates the legs clearly and looks more natural and lively for a friendly cartoon. Its main weaknesses are the long arms and the small trowel at picture size.
