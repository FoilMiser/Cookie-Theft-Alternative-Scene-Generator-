# Review of pair_7

## 1. How the rows differ

Both rows show the same four figures standing side-on and holding a paint roller. In row a each figure holds a long pole with both hands at chest height, angled up and forward, and a blue roller sits up above head height with its drum standing upright. In row b each figure holds a short handle low at the hips with arms reaching down and forward, and a pink roller lies flat at waist height in a wide rectangular frame.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in purple top**
- Row a: standing, both hands at chest, pushing a long-pole roller up to above head height.
- Row b: standing, arms down and forward, holding a short-handled pink roller flat at hip height.
- Better: a. The long pole raised upward reads at once as "painting a wall"; in b the flat roller in its frame could be a tray, a rolling pin or a cart handle.

**Man in red shirt**
- Row a: standing, both hands at chest on a long pole, blue roller up above his head.
- Row b: standing, arms hanging forward, holding a pink roller out flat at hip level.
- Better: a. The reach up is a clear painting action, while in b his straight, low arms look like he is carrying or pushing something.

**Older man in cap**
- Row a: standing, hands at chest on a long pole, roller raised above head.
- Row b: standing, hands at waist, short-handled pink roller held flat in front.
- Better: a. The pose and tool read as painting, and in b the dark vest and white sleeve merge with the low hands, so the hold is harder to make out.

**Older woman in cardigan**
- Row a: standing, hands at chest on a long pole, roller above head.
- Row b: standing, hands at waist, pink roller held flat in front.
- Better: no difference. Both read about equally well. Her arms are clearer in b, but the tool is clearer in a.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- All figures: the roller drum stands upright and hangs from a hooked frame, so it looks like a can, a bottle or a fire extinguisher on a hook rather than a roller against a wall. This is worse at the small size, where it reads as a blue can on a stick.
- All figures: the head faces straight ahead and does not tilt up toward the roller above, so the eyes are not on the work.
- Woman in purple top: the near arm crosses the body and the far arm is mostly hidden, so the grip is muddled.
- Older man in cap: the white sleeve against the dark vest makes the arm look like a separate patch, and at the small size the hands are hard to find.
- Older woman in cardigan: the teal sleeves blend into the teal top, so the arms are hard to separate from the body.

**Row b**
- All figures: the pink roller sits in a wide rectangular frame that wraps under it, so it looks like a tray, a rolling pin in a holder or a hand truck rather than a paint roller. At the small size it reads as a pink block on a rail.
- All figures: the roller floats at waist height with nothing to press against, so it is unclear what is being painted.
- All figures: the arms hang stiffly down and forward with the hands low at the hip, which looks more like carrying or pushing than painting.
- Man in red shirt: both hands bunch at the hip and the handle is very short, so the grip is hard to read.
- Older man in cap: the hands meet against the dark vest and trousers and are hard to make out, especially at the small size.
- Older woman in cardigan: the skirt flares out stiffly and the hands are low against it.

## 4. Choice

Row a. It is fairly sure: its upward reach with a long pole reads as painting a wall. Its upright roller drum is a real fault, but row b's flat pink roller in its frame is harder to recognise at both sizes.
