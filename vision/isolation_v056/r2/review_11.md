# Review of pair_11

## 1. How the rows differ

Row b gives the seated figures a longer torso and a longer, fuller skirt or dress that covers most of the thighs down toward the knee, so the lower body reads as a dress over a lap. Row a has shorter torsos and hems that ride high, so the thighs show and the skirts can look like shorts. The pink girl from the front also has a different arm pose: raised fists out to the sides in row a, hands drawn in to the upper chest in row b.

## 2. Figure by figure

**1. Girl in pink dress, seated, front**
- Row a: sitting, both arms out to the sides with elbows bent and fists raised to shoulder height, like flexing or cheering; hem high on the thighs.
- Row b: sitting, both hands drawn up to the upper chest just under the chin, forearms in front of the body; dress covers the thighs.
- Better: **a**. The raised fists read as a clear, cheerful gesture at both sizes, while row b's hands at the chest hold nothing and look like an unfinished or uncertain pose (though row b's longer dress is better).

**2. Same girl seated, side view**
- Row a: sitting upright, hands clasped over the knees, feet flat; seat of the dress rounded.
- Row b: the same pose with a slightly larger, more upright torso; the dress seat and lap are a flatter, boxier block.
- Better: **no difference**. The pose and read are the same. Row a's rounded seat is slightly softer and row b's torso is slightly better proportioned, and these balance out.

**3. Woman in yellow dress, seated, front**
- Row a: sitting, holding an open red book with both hands at chest height, looking down at it; short torso, dress ends high on the thighs.
- Row b: the same reading pose; longer torso, and the dress falls in a wider skirt to just above the knees.
- Better: **b**. The skirt reads as a dress over the lap; in row a the lower part looks like shorts and the legs look overly long.

**4. Older woman in skirt, seated, front**
- Row a: sitting, eating a pink ice cream cone held to the mouth with one hand; other arm hangs straight down beside the body; short grey skirt with the knees and lower thighs bare.
- Row b: the same pose; longer cardigan and a wider, longer grey skirt over the thighs.
- Better: **b**. The skirt reads as a skirt and the hanging arm sits better against the longer body; in row a the skirt looks like shorts.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- Figure 1: upper arms are thin and the raised fists with outturned elbows look stiff; dress hem is very high, so it reads almost like a top and shorts.
- Figure 1 (small): a thin strip of seat colour shows between the hips, which reads as a gap.
- Figure 3: short torso and a hem high on the thighs make the legs look too long; the dress bottom reads as shorts, and the bunched waist band looks lumpy.
- Figure 4: grey skirt is so short it reads as shorts; the hanging arm looks long because it reaches past the short skirt.
- Figure 4 (small): ice cream covers the mouth and lower face, so the face is hard to read.

**Row b**
- Figure 1: hands clasped at the upper chest with nothing in them make the gesture unclear; at the small size the arms merge into the dress and the figure looks bundled up.
- Figure 1: the neck is hidden; the head sits right on a straight neckline.
- Figure 2: the dress over the lap is a flat, boxy block with a hard square corner at the seat.
- Figure 3: the bunched waist band still looks lumpy, and the hips and skirt are quite wide.
- Figure 4: the hanging arm still ends in a pale hand floating beside the skirt; the ice cream still covers the lower face at the small size.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, **fairly sure**. Its longer torsos and skirts make three of the four figures read as people in dresses sitting down, which is friendlier and clearer. Its one weaker figure is the pink girl's arm pose, and that would be better with row a's arms.
