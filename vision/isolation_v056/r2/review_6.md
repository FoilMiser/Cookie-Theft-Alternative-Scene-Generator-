# Review of pair_6

## 1. How the rows differ

The poses, faces, clothes and held objects are the same in both rows. Row b draws the upper bodies broader and boxier: the torsos, shoulders and upper arms are wider, and the lap and seat are bigger and puffier. Row a keeps the trunks slimmer, so the figures look lighter and more in proportion with their heads and legs.

## 2. Figure by figure

1. **Woman in yellow dress, seated (book)**
   - Row a: sits and reads an open red book held close to the chest, looking down at it.
   - Row b: the same, but the book is held a little further out and lower, with thicker, wider-set arms.
   - Better: **a**. The book sits snugly in her hands and the body is not swollen, so the reading pose reads at a glance.

2. **Older woman in skirt, seated (ice cream)**
   - Row a: sits and licks a pink ice cream cone held at her mouth.
   - Row b: the same action, with a wider cardigan body and a large, cushion-like skirt and seat block.
   - Better: **a**. In b the skirt bulges out behind like a pillow and the torso looks heavy for a slight older woman.

3. **Girl in pink dress, seated (cup with straw)**
   - Row a: sits and sips from a red cup through a straw, her head tilted back and her eyes closed in a smile.
   - Row b: the same, but the dress body is wider and squarer.
   - Better: **a**. The narrower body suits a child better; in b the trunk is nearly as wide as the big head and looks like a block.

4. **Woman in yellow dress again, seated (cup with straw)**
   - Row a: sits and drinks from a large red cup with a straw, holding it with both hands at her chin.
   - Row b: the same, with a broader torso and thicker arms.
   - Better: **no difference**. The drink and hands read equally clearly in both; b is only somewhat bulkier.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All figures: the bottom of the seat or lap is cut flat and square, like a box, which looks stiff where the seat is not drawn.
- Figure 3 (girl): the head is very large for the slim body; at the small size the head nearly hides the cup and arm.
- Figure 3 (girl): the near shoulder of the dress is cut off sharply and looks flat.
- Figure 2 (older woman): at the small size the cone and the face run together into one blob near the glasses.

### Row b
- Figure 1 (woman, book): the upper arm is a thick slab and the torso is wide, so she looks heavy-set; the book floats a little away from her body.
- Figure 2 (older woman): the skirt and seat form a large puffy cushion that juts out behind her, which reads oddly without a chair.
- Figure 3 (girl): the dress body is boxy and as wide as the head, which makes her look less like a child.
- Figure 4 (woman, drink): the broad torso and thick crossing arm make the chest area crowded.
- All figures: the same flat, square seat edge as row a, and more noticeable here because the block is bigger.
- At the small size the differences are slight, but the b figures look squatter.

## 4. Choice

Row **a**, for a friendly, clear cartoon style: its figures are lighter and better proportioned, and they read just as clearly as row b's. How sure: **fairly sure**.
