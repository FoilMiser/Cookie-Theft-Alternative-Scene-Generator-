# Review of pair_17

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a, each figure kneels on one knee in a long lunge. The back leg stretches out behind, the body leans forward, and both hands hold a grey trowel point-down beside the front foot. In row b, each figure kneels on both knees with the thighs upright and leans forward. The hands hold a brown root ball, and a pink flower with two green leaves rises from it, so the figure looks like it is planting a flower. Row b reads as a gentler, more upright pose with a coloured prop. Row a is a more stretched-out, effortful pose with a plain grey tool.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in a purple top**
- Row a: on one knee in a lunge, reaching down with both hands to a trowel by her front foot.
- Row b: on both knees, leaning forward, holding a root ball with a pink flower rising past her forearm.
- Better: b. The flower stands clear of her skin-coloured arm and the kneeling pose is calm and easy to read, while in a the hunched lunge looks strained.

**Man in a red shirt**
- Row a: same one-knee lunge, gripping a trowel point-down next to his front shoe.
- Row b: on both knees, leaning forward, holding a root ball with a flower beside his forearm.
- Better: b. The flower and leaves show clearly against the background, and the action of planting reads at once.

**Older man in a cap**
- Row a: one-knee lunge in a brown vest and cream sleeves, trowel held down by his front shoe.
- Row b: on both knees, leaning forward, cream arm down to a root ball, with the flower just in front of the sleeve.
- Better: b. The pink and green stand out well against the cream sleeve, and the upright kneel suits an older man better than a deep lunge.

**Boy in a red cap**
- Row a: one-knee lunge in a blue hoodie, both hands on a trowel by his white shoe.
- Row b: on both knees, leaning forward, holding a root ball, with the flower mostly behind his blue sleeve.
- Better: a. In b the bloom and one leaf are largely hidden by his arm, so at the small size the prop shrinks to a brown dot, while the trowel in a stays readable.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- All four: the trowel is held point-down in the air beside the front shoe, with nothing to dig into, so the action is only half clear ("holding a tool" rather than "digging").
- All four: the lunge is deep and hunched. The back leg lies long and flat along the ground, which makes the pose look strained rather than friendly.
- All four: there is a small red mark at the grip between hand and handle that looks like a cut or a stray colour spot at the large size.
- Woman and man: the back shoe is a dark flat wedge that merges with the trouser hem, so the back foot is hard to make out.
- Boy: the hood bunches into a large blue lump behind the neck, and loose strap or drawstring bits at the waist look like stray marks.
- At the small size the trowel becomes a tiny grey triangle that is easy to miss.

**Row b**
- All four: the back feet are drawn as two overlapping shoes stacked behind the knees. They look doubled and oversized, most noticeably on the woman and the man.
- All four: the root ball hangs in the air below the hands, and the flower stem passes behind the wrist, so the flower seems to grow out of the hand rather than being held or put into the ground.
- All four: the hands stop well above the knee line, so the forward lean has no support and the figures look slightly off balance.
- Boy: the flower is mostly covered by his blue sleeve, and at the small size only the brown ball and a speck of pink show.
- Boy: the same bunched hood lump and the waist straps as in row a.
- At the small size the flower on all four is small and partly covered by the arm. It reads as "something green and pink" more than as a flower.

## 4. Choice

Row b, fairly sure. The upright kneel and the bright flower give a friendlier, clearer "planting" picture for three of the four figures. Its doubled back shoes and the boy's hidden flower should be fixed.
