# Review of pair_6

## 1. How the rows differ

Both rows show the same four figures standing side-on, facing right, with both arms raised. The difference is in the arms and hands. In row a both arms go up together, parallel, with the hands stacked or pressed together at the top. In row b the arms spread into a V: the nearer hand is higher and holds a small thin brown object, and the other hand is a rounded closed hand a little lower and further forward.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in a yellow dress**
- Row a: standing, both arms straight up and forward, hands pressed together like a diver.
- Row b: standing, arms up in a V, one hand gripping a small brown stick, the other hand open-ended and rounded.
- Better: **b**. Two hands at different heights read as reaching for or holding something, where row a's joined hands look like diving or praying.

**Man in a red shirt**
- Row a: standing, both arms straight up together, hands stacked at the top.
- Row b: standing, arms up and apart, one hand holding a small brown stick, the other raised.
- Better: **b**. The spread arms give a clearer silhouette, and the pose looks like an action rather than a stretch.

**Woman in a purple top**
- Row a: standing, both arms raised together, hands pressed together above the head.
- Row b: standing, arms raised in a V, one hand holding a small brown stick.
- Better: **b**. Same reason as the others: the pose reads as reaching to do something, and the two hands are easier to tell apart.

**Older man in a cap**

- Row b: standing, arms up in a V, one hand holding a small brown stick, with the cream sleeve again crossing his chin and chest.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All four: the two hands overlap into one shape with doubled finger outlines. It looks messy at the large size and becomes a smudge at the small size.
- All four: arms pressed together straight overhead look like diving, praying or stretching. Nothing tells you the figure is reaching for anything.
- All four: the arms are long and thin, and the pose makes the figures look stretched.
- Older man in a cap: the cream sleeve sweeps across the front of his face and chest and seems to start low on the vest, so the arm looks attached to his stomach. At the small size the white band hides most of his face.

### Row b
- All four: the held object is a tiny brown sliver that seems to be cut off at the top edge. It is hard to tell what it is, and at the small size it almost disappears.
- All four: the lower or further hand is a plain rounded blob with no fingers, so it is unclear whether it is open, closed or holding something.
- Older man in a cap: the same fault as in row a. The cream sleeve crosses his chin and chest and seems to come out of the middle of the vest. At the small size the white sleeve is the strongest shape on him.
- Woman in a purple top and woman in a yellow dress: at the small size the raised hands are tiny and the held stick is barely visible.

## 4. Choice

I would choose **row b** for a friendly, clear cartoon style. The V of the arms gives a cleaner silhouette, the hands don't merge into a smudge, and the pose reads as reaching up to do something rather than as a dive. The held object in row b still needs to be bigger and clearer.
