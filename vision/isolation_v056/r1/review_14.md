# Review of pair_14 (grey cat, ginger cat)

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a both cats are up on their legs in a low, stretched creeping walk: four separate legs show, the hind legs stand tall and one front leg reaches far forward. In row b the same cats are flattened into a deep crouch with the belly almost on the ground. The legs are folded under the body, so only stubby paws and a few contour marks show. Heads, tails, colours and faces are the same in both rows.

## 2. Per figure

**Grey cat**
- Row a: creeping forward on four visible legs, body sloping down from the hips to the head, one front paw reaching forward, tail straight out behind.
- Row b: crouched flat, belly near the ground, legs tucked under, paws just showing, tail straight out behind.
- Better: **a**. Its legs and stride are easy to read and it clearly moves, while row b turns the body into a long low lump with no readable legs.

**Ginger cat**
- Row a: the same creeping stride as the grey cat, four legs visible (far legs in a darker orange), one front paw reaching forward.
- Row b: the same flat crouch as the grey cat, legs folded under, small paws at the bottom edge.
- Better: **a**. The darker far legs separate cleanly from the near legs, so the pose reads at both sizes; row b's crouch blurs into one orange shape.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- Both cats: the near hind leg comes down from the hip, bends forward, then drops straight to a paw, so the joint looks like a knee bending the wrong way for a cat.
- Both cats: the reaching front leg runs flat and long under the chest, close to rubbery, and its paw looks lifted rather than planted.
- Both cats: the body is very long and stiff, with a deep dip behind the shoulders, so the back looks odd.
- Grey cat: the far legs are almost the same grey as the near legs, so at the small size the legs tangle a little.
- Both cats: the tail sticks straight out, stiff, which looks wooden.

**Row b**
- Both cats: the legs are hidden almost entirely; at the small size each cat reads as a sausage or seal shape rather than a cat on four legs.
- Both cats: the lumpy hind paw and haunch shape at the back bottom looks like a bulge, and the small curved contour marks on the flank and leg do not explain any limb.
- Both cats: the front leg is a flat strip along the bottom edge, so it is hard to tell where the chest ends and the leg begins.
- Both cats: the long, flat, heavy body makes the cat look lying down or squashed rather than crouching to pounce.
- Both cats: the stiff, straight tail, as in row a.

## 4. Choice

Row **a**, fairly sure. Its legs make both cats read clearly as cats moving, at the large size and the small one, and it looks friendlier and more lively. Its odd hind-leg bend is a fault, but a smaller one than row b's legless, lumpy crouch.
