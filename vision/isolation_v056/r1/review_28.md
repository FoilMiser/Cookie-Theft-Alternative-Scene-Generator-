# Review of pair_28

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the hose is held almost upright in front of the chest, the yellow nozzle is large and sits level at face height with its wide mouth pointing back toward the figure's own face. In row b the hose is tilted more steeply across the body, the nozzle is smaller and angled up and away, and the lower hand sits at chest-to-waist height so the hose reads more like something being aimed. Heads and bodies are drawn a little larger and more upright in row b.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Older woman in a cardigan**
- Row a: stands leaning slightly forward, arms stiffly out, holding a near-vertical blue tube with a big yellow cone at eye level, cone mouth facing her glasses.
- Row b: stands upright, holding the tube diagonally across her front, small yellow nozzle tipped up and forward above her hands.
- Better: b. The smaller nozzle pointing away from her reads as a spray nozzle rather than something she is peering into.

**Woman in a purple top**
- Row a: leans forward with both arms reaching, near-vertical tube, large yellow cone level with her face and opening toward it.
- Row b: upright, tube tilted across her body, small nozzle at the top angled up and outward.
- Better: b. The pose looks like aiming water; in a the cone in front of her face looks like a telescope or megaphone.

**Bearded man in green**
- Row a: leans forward, arms straight out, tube nearly upright, large cone at mouth height pointing back toward his face.
- Row b: upright, tube slanted across his body, small nozzle tipped up and away from him.
- Better: b. Same reason: the nozzle no longer points at his own face, so the action reads as watering.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- All three figures: the large yellow cone faces back toward the figure's own face, so it reads like a telescope, megaphone or drinking from it, not spraying water.
- All three figures: the tube is a short straight rod that ends in mid-air below the lower hand, with no hose trailing away; at small size it looks like a stick or pole.
- All three figures: arms are stiff and straight, both reaching nearly the same way, which looks wooden.
- All three figures: forward lean with nothing in front makes the stance look off-balance.
- Older woman: the lower hand clutches the tube at an odd angle and the tube seems to pass behind the knuckles awkwardly.

**Row b**
- All three figures: the tube still ends abruptly in mid-air near the hip; no hose trails to the ground, so it can read as a pole or baton.
- All three figures: the nozzle is small; at the small size it is only a yellow fleck and the "hose" reading depends on the blue tube alone.
- All three figures: the nozzle tips up and slightly back over the head rather than clearly forward, so the direction of spray is still not obvious.
- Older woman: the tube cuts across her torso and lower hand, and at small size the hand and tube merge.
- Bearded man: the lower forearm and the tube overlap in a way that is hard to read at small size.

## 4. Choice

Row b, fairly sure. Neither row draws a convincing hose (both lack a trailing line), but row a's big cone aimed at each figure's face is a clear misreading, while row b looks like people holding and aiming something.
