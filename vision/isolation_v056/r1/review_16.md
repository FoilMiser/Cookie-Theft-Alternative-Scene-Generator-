# Review of pair_16

## 1. How the rows differ

Heads, faces, clothes and legs are the same in both rows. Only the arms differ. In row a the elbows are bent and the fists are held up beside the shoulders, like a cheer or a flex. In row b the arms hang closer to the body and both hands rest on the upper chest near the collar.

## 2. Figure by figure

1. Child in yellow, seated, front view
   - Row a: both arms out to the sides, elbows bent, fists raised to shoulder height.
   - Row b: elbows down at the sides, both hands resting on the upper chest.
   - Better: a. The arms stand clear of the body and read as a happy cheer, while in b the hands merge into the shirt.

2. Girl in pink dress, seated, front view
   - Row a: arms out and bent, open hands raised beside the shoulders.
   - Row b: hands resting on the chest just below the collar, elbows at the sides.
   - Better: a. The outline is open and easy to read, while b looks like she is clutching the front of her dress.

3. Child, seated, three-quarter view
   - Row a: far arm raised with the fist up, near arm bent with the fist pressed at the shoulder.
   - Row b: both hands held together at the chest, elbows down.
   - Better: b. The near arm in a is a short bent stub that is hard to read, while b's hands-at-chest pose is simple and consistent.

4. Girl, seated, three-quarter view
   - Row a: far arm raised and waving, near arm bent sharply up with the fist at the shoulder.
   - Row b: both hands together at the chest.
   - Better: b. In a the near arm looks cut short and lumpy where it meets the shoulder, and b avoids that.

## 3. Faults

Row a
- Figure 1: raised fists beside the head look like flexing; it is an odd pose for someone sitting still.
- Figure 2: the hands are oversized, flat mitten shapes at the large size.
- Figure 3: the near arm is foreshortened into a short bent stub with the fist on the shoulder, and the elbow is unclear.
- Figure 4: the near arm looks amputated or stumpy at the shoulder. At small size it is a blob on the shoulder.
- Figures 3 and 4: the two arms do different things (one waves, one is tucked), which looks a little random.

Row b
- Figures 1 and 2: the hands on the chest look like clutching or tugging at the shirt, which could read as worry or pain rather than calm.
- Figures 1 and 2 at small size: the hands melt into the top, so the arms are hard to make out.
- Figures 3 and 4: the hands overlap at the chest in a small knot, and at small size it is unclear what the hands are doing.
- Figure 4: the far hand barely shows past the collar.

## 4. Choice

Row a, for its open, cheerful arm outlines that read clearly at small size in the front views. Row b's side views are tidier, though, so this is a toss-up.
