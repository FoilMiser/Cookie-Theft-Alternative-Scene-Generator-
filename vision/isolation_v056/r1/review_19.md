# Review of pair_19

## 1. How the rows differ

The bodies, poses, faces and clothes are the same in both rows. There are two differences. In row a the frying pans are shallow with long, thin handles and an inside the same blue-grey as the outside, and the buckets have a wire handle that sticks out at an angle across the side of the face. In row b the pans are deep with short, thick handles and a lighter grey inside, and the buckets have no handle, only a small rivet dot.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in yellow dress**
- Row a: stands bent slightly forward with one arm straight out, holding a long-handled frying pan by the end of its handle.
- Row b: the same pose, holding a deep, short-handled pan close to the hand.
- Better: **a**. The long, flat pan reads at once as a frying pan at both sizes, while the deep pan with a stubby handle looks more like a pot or bowl, especially at the small size.

**Older man in cap**
- Row a: leans forward with one arm out, holding a long-handled frying pan.
- Row b: the same pose, holding a deep pan with a short, thick handle.
- Better: **a**. Same reason as the woman: row a's pan is the clearer, more familiar frying-pan shape.

**Bearded man in green**
- Row a: stands with both hands on his hips, wearing an upside-down bucket on his head. A wire handle runs diagonally down past his ear and ends in an orange tip.
- Row b: the same pose with the bucket on his head and no handle sticking out.
- Better: **b**. Row a's diagonal handle looks like a stick going through or into the side of his head, and row b's plain bucket is cleaner and still reads as a bucket.

**Man in red shirt**
- Row a: stands with both hands on his hips under an upside-down bucket. The handle angles across his ear and cheek.
- Row b: the same pose with a plain bucket and no handle.
- Better: **b**. The handle in row a cuts across the face and ear and is confusing, especially at the small size.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- Bearded man in green: the bucket handle runs diagonally across the ear and jaw and looks like a rod stuck into his head. At the small size it reads as a stray stick.
- Man in red shirt: the same problem. The handle crosses his ear and cheek and clutters the face.
- Woman in yellow and older man: the inside of each pan is the same blue-grey as the outside, so the opening has little contrast (a minor fault).
- Woman in yellow and older man: the straight-armed pose with a pan held out does not make clear what they are doing (serving, showing, cooking?). This applies to row b too.

**Row b**
- Woman in yellow: the deep pan with a short, thick handle reads more like a pot or bowl than a frying pan, more so at the small size.
- Older man in cap: the same pot-like pan. The dark handle block also merges with his hand and makes the grip hard to read at the small size.
- Bearded man in green and man in red shirt: without a handle the bucket depends on its shape alone. It still reads as a pail, but it could pass for a plain grey hat (a minor fault).
- Woman in yellow and older man: the outstretched-arm pose is unclear in this row as well.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. The buckets in row a have a real fault: a stick-like handle across the face that looks wrong at both sizes. Row b's fault is milder: the pans look a bit like pots but still read as cookware held out. For a friendly, clear cartoon style, row b's cleaner heads matter more. A redraw that combines row a's frying pans with row b's buckets would be better than either row.
