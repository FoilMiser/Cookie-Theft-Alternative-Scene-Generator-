# Review of pair_7

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a every figure kneels on both knees with the shins flat on the ground behind. The body leans far forward and both arms hang straight down, holding a small plant with a pink flower and a brown root ball. In row b every figure kneels on one knee with the other foot planted in front, leans forward, and holds a grey hand trowel point-down beside the front foot. Row b's pose is more open and has more life in it. Row a's held object is clearer.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in purple top**
- Row a: kneels on both knees, bent forward, both arms straight down, holding a flower seedling by its root ball.
- Row b: kneels on her back knee with her front foot planted, leans forward, and holds a trowel pointing down near her front shoe.
- Better: b. The half-kneel reads as a natural working crouch, while row a's straight-armed, both-knees pose looks stiff, close to being on all fours.

**Man in red shirt**
- Row a: same as the woman, kneeling on both knees and holding the seedling low with straight arms.
- Row b: same half-kneel as the woman, with the trowel held point-down by the front foot.
- Better: b. The legs read as two separate legs in b, while in a they merge into one flat shin and shoe slab.

**Older man in cap**
- Row a: kneels on both knees, bent forward, holding the seedling down at ground level.
- Row b: half-kneels with his brown shoes clearly placed, holding the trowel.
- Better: b. The pose looks steadier and the separate legs are easier to read in dark trousers. In a the dark trousers fuse into one shape.

**Boy in red cap**
- Row a: kneels on both knees and bends forward, holding the seedling.
- Row b: half-kneels and leans forward, holding the trowel.
- Better: no difference. Both rows have the same bunched hood lump and loose jacket tab at the hip, and b's crossed dark legs under the jacket are as busy as a's flat shins.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- All figures: both shins and feet lie flat behind in one stacked slab, so at the small size it is hard to tell there are two legs.
- All figures: the arms hang dead straight to the ground. With the deep forward lean the pose reads as stiff, or as crawling on all fours, rather than gardening.
- All figures: the flower's leaves sit behind and across the forearm, so the plant partly merges with the arm and hand.
- Older man: a dark waistband sticks out at the back of the vest like a separate tab.
- Boy: a large blue hood lump at the neck, and a small blue strap or tab sticking out at the hip.
- Small size: the root ball is a clear dot, but the pink flower is only a speck.

**Row b**
- All figures: the trowel blade is a pale grey triangle outlined thinly. At the small size it almost disappears against the background and is hard to recognise as a tool.
- All figures: the trowel hangs in the air beside the front shin instead of touching the ground, so the action (digging?) is unclear. Small red or orange marks at the handle look like stray specks.
- All figures: the arms are very long, reaching from the shoulder to below the front knee.
- All figures: the hand, trowel handle and front knee crowd together into one cluttered spot.
- Woman: an awkward hump at the hip and rear thigh where the back leg leaves the body.
- Older man: the same dark waistband sticks out at the back.
- Boy: the same hood lump, a jacket tab at the hip, and dark legs that cross under the jacket and are a little hard to sort out at the small size.

## 4. Choice

Row b, fairly sure. Its half-kneel is a friendlier, more natural pose that reads well at both sizes. However, the trowel needs a stronger colour or outline, and it should touch the ground, to be as clear as row a's seedling.
