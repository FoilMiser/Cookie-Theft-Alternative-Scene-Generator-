# Review of pair 21

## 1. How the rows differ

The faces, legs, clothes and seated poses are the same in both rows; only the arms differ. In row a the arms are folded up tight against the chest, with the fists at the collar or shoulders. In row b the arms are lifted away from the body: the front-view figures hold both fists up and out like a cheer or a flex, and the turned figures raise the far hand in a wave.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Figure 1, child in yellow top, seated, front view**
- Row a: sits with both fists drawn up to the shoulders, elbows tucked against the sides.
- Row b: sits with both arms bent out to the sides and fists raised at shoulder height (a cheer or strongman flex).
- Better: **b**. The arms stand clear of the shirt and read at once, while in row a the hands merge into the yellow top.

**Figure 2, girl in pink dress, seated, front view**
- Row a: sits with both fists held at the top of the chest, elbows in.
- Row b: sits with both arms bent outward and fists raised beside the shoulders.
- Better: **b**. The silhouette is open and cheerful; in row a the arms are mostly lost against the pink dress.

**Figure 3, child again, seated, turned three-quarter**
- Row a: sits turned, near fist on the chest and far fist at the shoulder, both arms bunched against the body.
- Row b: sits turned, near fist raised at the shoulder with the elbow out to the side, far arm lifted in a wave.
- Better: **b**. The wave gives a clear gesture. The near arm in row b is a bit awkward, but row a is cramped and hard to read.

**Figure 4, girl again, seated, turned three-quarter**
- Row a: sits turned, both forearms held up against the chest, fists near the collar.
- Row b: sits turned, near arm reaching forward and down, far arm raised in a wave.
- Better: **b**. The two arms are separate and readable; row a's arms are a tucked bundle.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- All figures: the hands sit on top of the clothing in a similar skin tone and the forearms lie against the torso. At the small size the arms are almost invisible and the figures look armless or hunched.
- Figures 1 and 2: the fists at the shoulders look like gripping backpack straps or shrugging from cold, an unclear gesture.
- Figure 3: the near fist presses into the chest and the far fist pokes up at the shoulder line; the arms look cramped and tangled.
- Figure 4: the far fist sticks up at the shoulder edge like a bump on the outline, and the near arm is hard to separate from the dress.

**Row b**
- Figures 1 and 2: the wide flex pose looks like a particular action (showing muscles, cheering) rather than calm sitting, which might be misread in a scene where the figure is only meant to be seated. The fists are large mitten blobs.
- Figure 3: the near arm is odd. The fist sits high at the shoulder, the forearm drops to an elbow sticking out at the left, and the joint reads as kinked. The far hand has a small circle drawn on it (thumb) that looks like a mark.
- Figure 4: the far raised hand has the same odd circle on the palm. The near arm reaching forward and down is slightly stiff and its hand floats with nothing to hold.
- Small size: still readable. The raised hands make little spikes beside the head, but the pose is clear.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. Its arms are separated from the body, so the figures stay readable and look lively and friendly at picture size, while row a's arms disappear into the clothes. The main caveat is that row b's flex or cheer pose is a stronger action than plain sitting.
