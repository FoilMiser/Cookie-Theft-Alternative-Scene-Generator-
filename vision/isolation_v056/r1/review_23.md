# Review of pair_23

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a every figure leans forward with knees bent and holds a paint roller level at chest height, both hands close to the face, with the pink roller pointing straight ahead. In row b every figure stands upright and holds the pole on a diagonal, so the blue roller sits upright at head height in front of them. Row b looks like someone painting a wall. Row a looks more like someone pushing or aiming something, and the handle runs up into the face.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in a purple top**
- Row a: leaning forward, both fists at her chin, pushing a level roller out in front of her chest.
- Row b: standing straight, both hands on a diagonal pole, roller raised in front of her face.
- Better: b. The raised roller reads at once as painting a wall, and the handle stays clear of her face.

**Man in a red shirt**
- Row a: hunched forward with bent knees, the pole end at his mouth, roller held level at chest height.
- Row b: upright, hands at chest and shoulder on a diagonal pole, roller up by his head.
- Better: b. His upright stance and raised roller are clear and natural. In row a it looks as if he is blowing into the handle.

**Older man in a cap**
- Row a: stooped forward, the pole end at his mustache, both fists raised, roller level in front.
- Row b: upright, gripping a diagonal pole at his chest, roller raised in front of his face.
- Better: b. Row b shows a clear painting action. Row a's stoop plus the handle at his mouth is the most confusing of the four.

**Older woman in a cardigan**
- Row a: leaning forward with knees bent, the handle crossing her mouth and chin, roller level in front of her chest.
- Row b: standing upright, hands at her chest on a diagonal pole, roller up at head height.
- Better: b. The pose suits her and reads cleanly. In row a the handle covers the lower part of her face.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All four: the end of the roller handle touches or crosses the mouth, so the figures look as if they are blowing into a pipe or playing a flute rather than painting.
- All four: the roller is held level and pointing straight ahead at chest height. That is not a recognizable painting position. It reads as pushing or aiming something.
- All four: the forward lean with bent knees looks strained and awkward, as if they are bracing against something.
- Older man in a cap: the stoop together with the handle at his mustache makes his head area crowded and hard to read.
- Older woman in a cardigan: the handle hides her mouth and chin, and her raised arms bunch up against her face.
- Small size: the fists, handle and face merge into one cluster around the head on every figure, so the action is unclear.

### Row b
- All four: the roller's wire frame bends over the top of the cylinder in a hook that looks a little stiff and mechanical, but it is still readable.
- Older man in a cap: his white sleeve and vest overlap around his lower hand, so his grip on the pole is somewhat hard to make out.
- Older woman in a cardigan: her arms are drawn mostly inside the teal sleeve shape, which makes her grip less distinct than the others'.
- Small size: the pole is thin and the roller is small next to the head, but the diagonal pole plus the raised roller still reads as painting on all four.

## 4. Choice

Row b. It is the friendlier, clearer cartoon: upright poses, faces left uncovered, and an action you recognize as painting a wall at both sizes. Sure.
