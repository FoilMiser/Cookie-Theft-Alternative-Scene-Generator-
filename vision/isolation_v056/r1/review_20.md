# Review of pair_20

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the two lying dogs face the viewer head-on with closed, curved eyes, and the two sitting dogs lift one front paw and point their snouts sharply upward. In row b the lying dogs turn their heads to the side and bow them down toward the front paws, and the sitting dogs keep all four feet on the ground and look ahead, only slightly upward. Body colours, collars and patches are the same in both rows.

## 2. Figure by figure

**1. Brown dog (lying)**
- Row a: lying flat, head facing the viewer and resting on the front paws, eyes closed, tail curled at the back.
- Row b: lying flat, head turned to the right and bowed so the muzzle nearly touches the paws, eyes closed.
- Better: **a**. The face-on sleeping face, with two even closed-eye arcs, reads at once as a calm, sleeping dog. Row b's near eye is a sharp angled chevron that looks more like a frown or a squint.

**2. White dog with brown patches (lying)**
- Row a: lying flat, head facing the viewer on the front paws, eyes closed, curled tail raised at the back.
- Row b: lying flat, head turned right and bowed low over the paws, eyes closed, blue collar showing round the neck.
- Better: **a**. Same reason as figure 1. The front-facing sleeping face is friendlier and easier to read. In row b the big round white head and chevron eye make the face hard to make out.

**3. Brown dog (sitting)**
- Row a: sitting, near front paw lifted and curled, head tipped far back with the snout pointing up.
- Row b: sitting upright with all four feet down, head looking ahead and a little upward.
- Better: **b**. The pose is calm and solid and the head angle looks natural. Row a's steeply raised snout looks strained, a little like a seal.

**4. White dog (sitting)**
- Row a: sitting, one front paw lifted (drawn in grey), head tipped far back with the snout pointing up.
- Row b: sitting upright with all four feet down, head looking ahead with a small smile.
- Better: **b**. The outline is cleaner, and the face, with its smile, reads as friendly. In row a the grey raised paw looks loose from the body and the upturned head is awkward.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- Figure 1: the red collar shows only as a strap dangling under the chin, like a loose ribbon rather than a collar round the neck.
- Figure 1 and 2: the closed-eye arcs run out to the tops of the ears, so the eyes and ear edges merge into one line. The head looks pasted on top of the body, with no neck.
- Figure 2: the curled tail is a bulgy, lumpy shape that is hard to read as a tail. The grey far paw behind the face is barely visible.
- Figure 3: the snout points almost straight up, and the long neck and raised head look stiff and seal-like.
- Figure 4: the lifted paw is pale grey and looks detached from the body. The same strained, upturned head as figure 3.
- Small size: the sitting dogs' raised paws are only just visible, and the upturned heads look like the dogs are howling.

**Row b**
- Figure 1 and 2: the near closed eye is a sharp ">"-like chevron that reads as a frown or angry squint. The far eye is a cramped tick at the very edge of the head.
- Figure 1 and 2: the head is a very large plain round ball, and the muzzle is tucked under it, so the face is hard to find.
- Figure 2: at the small size the white head blends into the white body, and the dog reads as a white lump.
- Figure 1: no tail is visible.
- Figure 3 and 4: the pose is fairly static, and the long neck and torso still look a little stiff.
- Figure 4: the grey far hind leg is long and post-like.

## 4. Choice

**a**, a toss-up. Row a's face-on sleeping dogs are clearly friendlier and easier to read than row b's frowning, bowed heads. However, row b draws the sitting dogs more cleanly and naturally than row a's strained, upturned poses.
