# Review of pair_25

## 1. How the rows differ

The bodies, clothes and stances are almost identical in both rows. The difference is the hose end. In row a the yellow nozzle is a small cone on a hose that bends near the top. In row b the nozzle is a much larger yellow cylinder on a straighter hose, the arms reach a little further forward, and the nozzle sits closer to face height.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Older woman in cardigan (left)**
- Row a: standing, both hands on a dark blue hose held diagonally across the chest. The upper hand is near a small yellow cone tip and the hose bends just below it.
- Row b: standing, arms reaching forward, both hands on a straight diagonal hose. A large yellow nozzle sits at chin level.
- Better: **b**. At the small size the nozzle reads clearly as a separate yellow fitting, whereas in a it shrinks to a yellow fleck.

**Woman in purple top (middle)**
- Row a: standing and leaning slightly forward, holding a diagonal hose with one hand at the top by a small cone and one hand at mid-length.
- Row b: same stance, with the hose a little straighter and a large yellow nozzle at the top, level with her shoulder or face.
- Better: **b**. The nozzle is legible at both sizes, so she reads as someone holding a hose end, not a stick.

**Bearded man in green (right)**
- Row a: standing with arms raised high, gripping a diagonal hose that bends at the top under a small yellow cone.
- Row b: same pose, with a straighter hose and a large yellow nozzle near head height.
- Better: **no difference**. His raised arms and the long hose read about equally in both rows. The small cone in a is weak, but the large cylinder in b looks just as much like a telescope.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- All three: the yellow nozzle is tiny, and at the small size it is just a yellow tick, so the object reads as a blue stick or pole.
- All three: the hose stops abruptly at the lower end in mid-air, with no length of hose trailing away, so it does not read as a flexible garden hose.
- Older woman: her lower hand and the hose overlap the cardigan front in a muddled way, and it is hard to tell which hand is which.
- Bearded man: the arms are raised very high, so the grip looks like holding a pole or a bat rather than aiming a hose.
- All three: the cone's wide end faces back toward the figure, so it is unclear which way the water would come out.

**Row b**
- All three: the large yellow cylinder points back toward the figure's face. Together with the head-height position, it can read as looking through a telescope, binoculars or a torch, especially at the small size.
- All three: the hose is a short stiff straight bar that ends in mid-air at the bottom, so it looks even more like a rigid pole than in a.
- Older woman: the nozzle sits right in front of her glasses and mouth, which adds to the "peering through it" look.
- Bearded man: the nozzle overlaps his beard and face line, which crowds the head at the small size.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, but it is a **toss-up**. Its nozzle actually reads at picture size, which row a's does not. However, both rows draw the hose as a short rigid bar, and row b's big nozzle aimed at the face invites a telescope reading.
