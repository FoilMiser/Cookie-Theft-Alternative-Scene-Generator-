# Review of pair_18

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the two lying dogs have their heads turned in three-quarter view, facing right and down, with their eyes drawn as sharp angled strokes. In row b the lying dogs face the viewer: both ears hang down, the eyes are soft closed curves and the tail shows. The sitting dogs are almost the same in both rows, except that in row b each lifts its far front paw, where in row a all four paws are on the ground.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Figure 1: brown dog**
- Row a: lying flat with the head down and turned right, eyes shut as angled strokes, no tail showing.
- Row b: lying flat with the head down and facing front, eyes shut as curves, both ears hanging, tail curled at the back.
- Better: **b**. The frontal head with curved closed eyes reads as a peaceful sleeping dog, while row a's angled eye strokes look like a frown or a squint.

**Figure 2: white dog with brown patches**
- Row a: lying with the head down and turned right, angled eye strokes, no tail.
- Row b: lying with the head facing front, curved closed eyes and a slight smile, tail showing.
- Better: **b**. Same reason as figure 1. The face reads as calm and friendly, and the small figure still shows a face.

**Figure 3: brown dog again**
- Row a: sitting upright with the head raised to the right and all four paws on the ground.
- Row b: sitting upright with the head raised and the far front paw lifted and bent.
- Better: **no difference**. Both read as a clear, tidy sitting dog. Row b adds a paw-lift that is livelier but adds nothing to clarity.

**Figure 4: white dog again**
- Row a: sitting upright with the head raised and all four paws on the ground.
- Row b: sitting upright with the head raised and the far front paw lifted.
- Better: **no difference**. Both are clean. Row b's lifted leg is pale grey and a little hard to read, which offsets the extra life it gives.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- Figures 1 and 2: the closed eyes are hard angled strokes that look like angry brows or a grimace, not sleep.
- Figures 1 and 2: a small stray dark stroke at the far edge of the head (the far eye or brow) looks like a mark on the outline.
- Figures 1 and 2: the head is turned down into the chest, so at the small size the face is mostly hidden and the dog reads as a lump.
- Figures 1 and 2: no tail is visible, so the back end looks shapeless.
- Figure 4: the far legs are flat pale grey and look detached from the white body.

**Row b**
- Figure 1: the red collar shows only as a thin broken streak under the chin, with a small white fleck near the front paw.
- Figures 1 and 2: the eyelash curves run out to touch the ear outlines, so eye and ear join.
- Figures 1 and 2: the frontal head is large compared with the side-on body, so the neck turn is a little abrupt.
- Figure 2: the blue collar is barely visible.
- Figures 3 and 4: the lifted far paw comes out from behind the chest without a clear elbow and is hooked, so it looks slightly rubbery.
- Figure 4: the lifted leg is pale grey, and at the small size it reads as a vague hook.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. Its sleeping dogs are clearly calm and friendly, and their faces stay readable at the small size. Row a's angled eye strokes make the sleeping dogs look cross. The sitting dogs are about even.
