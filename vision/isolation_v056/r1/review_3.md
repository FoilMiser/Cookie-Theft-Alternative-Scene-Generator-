# Review of pair_3

## 1. How the two rows differ

All eight figures are pointing upward with one hand. In row a the pointing arm goes almost straight up beside the head and the head is tipped far back so the face looks up at the finger. In row b the arm goes up and out on a diagonal, about 45 degrees, away from the head, and the head is only a little tipped, so the face is turned more toward the viewer. Row b figures also look a little broader and fill more of their space.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Older woman in a cardigan**
- Row a: standing, right arm straight up beside her head, head tipped back looking up at her finger.
- Row b: standing, right arm raised on a diagonal out to the side, head slightly raised, glasses facing the viewer.
- Better: **b**. The arm stands clear of the head and the face with glasses is easier to read, especially at the small size.

**Woman in a purple top**
- Row a: standing, arm straight up close to the head, head tipped back, long neck showing.
- Row b: standing, arm raised on a diagonal, head turned slightly up toward the hand.
- Better: **b**. The pose reads as pointing at something rather than stretching, and the neck no longer looks stretched.

**Girl in a pink dress**
- Row a: standing, arm straight up pressed against the side of her big head, face tipped back showing the underside of the chin.
- Row b: standing, arm raised on a diagonal from behind her head, face looking forward and a little up.
- Better: **no difference**. Row b's face is friendlier, but her very large head crowds the arm in both rows, and at the small size both read about the same.

**Boy in a red cap**
- Row a: standing, arm straight up beside the cap, head tipped back looking up.
- Row b: standing, arm raised on a long diagonal, face toward the viewer with the cap brim clear.
- Better: **b**. The cap and face read more clearly and the arm makes a clean, open pointing shape.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All four: the arm hugs the head, so at the small size arm, head and hair merge into one narrow upright shape and the pointing hand is the only clear cue.
- All four: heads tipped back hide much of the face; the eyes are pushed to the top of the head and the chin underside shows.
- Older woman: the arm is stiff and pole-like and seems to start above the shoulder.
- Woman in purple: neck looks long and stretched; the raised sleeve is a small wedge that looks pinched.
- Girl in pink: the arm runs along the edge of her very large head and partly merges with it; the tipped-back head looks heavy.
- Boy in red cap: the cap tipped back turns the face into a narrow strip; at small size the face is hard to make out.

### Row b
- All four: the long diagonal arms look slightly over-long, especially the boy's and the older woman's.
- Woman in purple: the raised sleeve shows as an odd purple tab sticking up at the shoulder.
- Girl in pink: the arm seems to come out from behind her very large head, with a bump of sleeve at the shoulder; the head nearly touches the hand.
- Girl in pink: at the small size she is tiny next to the others and her hand is hard to see.
- Figures look a little broad and stocky in the body compared with row a.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. The open diagonal arm makes "pointing at something" read at a glance at both sizes, and the faces stay turned toward the viewer, which suits a friendly, clear cartoon style better than row a's tipped-back heads.
