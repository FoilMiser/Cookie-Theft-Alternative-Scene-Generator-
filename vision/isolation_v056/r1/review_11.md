# Review of pair_11

## 1. How the rows differ

Both rows show the four figures with a paint roller. In row a each figure stands upright with both hands close together at chest height, holding a long pole that rises diagonally to a blue roller beside and above the head. In row b each figure leans forward with arms stretched out in front, holding a short handle near the face, and a pink roller hangs horizontally at about waist height in front of the body.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in purple top**
- Row a: standing upright, both hands gripping a long pole at chest height, roller raised above head height in front of her.
- Row b: leaning forward, arms reaching out level, near hand right under her chin, roller hanging at waist height ahead of her.
- Better: a. The raised roller reads at once as painting a wall, while in b the hand at her mouth and the low roller make the action unclear.

**Man in red shirt**
- Row a: standing upright, hands together on the pole at chest height, roller up beside his head.
- Row b: leaning forward, arms out, rear hand and pole end against his mouth and moustache, roller low in front.
- Better: a. The pose is clean and the face is fully visible; in b the pole end pokes into his face.

**Older man in cap**
- Row a: standing upright, hands on the pole at chest height, roller raised in front of him.
- Row b: leaning forward, arms out, near hand covering his mouth and chin, roller hanging at waist height.
- Better: a. In b his face is half hidden by the hand and it looks as if he is holding something to his mouth.

**Older woman in cardigan**
- Row a: standing upright, hands on the pole at chest height, roller raised above head level.
- Row b: leaning forward, arms out, near hand just below her glasses and mouth, roller low in front.
- Better: a. Row a is clearer at the small size; in b her hand and the pole crowd her face.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- All four: the roller cylinder stands upright (its long axis vertical), which is unusual for a wall roller and looks a bit like a can on a stick.
- All four: the two hands are bunched together close to the chest with short bent arms, so the grip is cramped and a little stiff.
- All four: the pose is very upright and static; nothing shows the pushing or rolling motion.
- Older man in cap: the light sleeve against the dark vest makes the arm and hand a little hard to separate from the pole at the small size.
- All four, small size: the pole is thin and the hands are tiny, so the grip is hard to make out, though the roller itself stays readable.

### Row b
- All four: the near hand and the back end of the handle sit at the mouth and chin, so the figures look as if they are eating, drinking or blowing into something.
- Man in red shirt and older man in cap: the hand and pole end cover the mouth and moustache, hiding part of the face.
- Older woman in cardigan: the hand sits just under her glasses, so the face is crowded.
- All four: the roller hangs below the handle at waist height with no surface drawn, so it reads more like pushing a cart, mower or vacuum than painting.
- All four: arms are stretched straight out and the body leans, which looks strained.
- All four, small size: the cluster of hands, handle and face around the head turns into a muddle; the pink roller is visible but its purpose is unclear.

## 4. Choice

Row a, for a friendly, clear cartoon style: the faces are unobstructed and the raised roller reads as wall painting even at the small size. Fairly sure.
