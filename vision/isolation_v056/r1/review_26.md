# Review of pair_26

## 1. How the rows differ

The poses, clothes and faces are the same in both rows. The two figures holding a pan hold different pans. Row a has a short, thick black handle with a light grey inside, and the hand closes around a bulky grip. Row b has a longer, thinner handle with a darker blue-grey inside, and the hand wraps the thin handle. On the two figures with a bucket on the head, row b adds the bucket's wire handle, which hangs down at a slant past the left ear. Row a shows only a small dark lug on the rim.

## 2. Figure by figure

**Woman in yellow dress**
- Row a: stands leaning a little forward, holding a frying pan out at arm's length in one hand. The hand clutches a thick black block of a handle.
- Row b: the same pose and the same held-out pan. The handle is longer and thinner, and the pan's inside is dark.
- Better: **b**. The thin handle and the hand around it read as a real frying pan grip, where row a's grip looks like a lump.

**Older man in cap**
- Row a: stands bent a little forward, holding a pan straight out in one hand, with the other arm down.
- Row b: the same pose. The pan has a longer, thinner handle and a dark inside.
- Better: **b**. As with the woman, the hand clearly holds a handle, and the pan reads as a frying pan at the small size too.

**Bearded man in green**
- Row a: stands with both hands on hips and an upturned bucket on his head.
- Row b: the same pose. The bucket's wire handle hangs down at a slant across the side of his head by the ear.
- Better: **a**. Without the hanging handle the head stays clean, and the bucket still reads as a bucket.

**Man in red shirt**
- Row a: stands with both hands on hips and an upturned bucket on his head.
- Row b: the same pose. The bucket's wire handle hangs down across the left side of his face and ear.
- Better: **a**. In row b the hanging handle looks like a stick poking into the head, most of all at the small size.

## 3. Faults

**Row a**
- Woman in yellow: the hand on the pan is a dark, blocky grip, so it is hard to see where the hand ends and the handle starts.
- Older man: the same bulky, stubby grip. At the small size the hand and handle merge into one dark blob.
- Older man and woman: the pan's light grey inside is close to the background colour, so the pan reads a little faint at the small size.
- Bearded man and red-shirt man: the small dark lug on the bucket rim looks like a stray speck. This is minor.

**Row b**
- Bearded man: the bucket's wire handle hangs down at a slant over his ear and cheek. It reads as a rod stuck in his head, and at the small size as a stray line.
- Red-shirt man: the same hanging handle crosses near his face and ear and is just as confusing, with the end knob looking like a small blob by his ear.
- Woman and older man: the pans' long handles push the pans far from the bodies. This is fine, but it makes the figures wide. Minor.

## 4. Choice

For a friendly, clear cartoon style I would choose **a**, but it is a **toss-up**. Row b's pans are clearer, but its hanging bucket handles are the more striking fault, because they cut across two faces and look wrong at both sizes. Row a's stubby pan grips are a smaller problem. The best result would take row b's pans and row a's buckets.
