## What was read

**Every forced event was read**: twenty-four of twenty-four in the eight pictures. That is a count of what a reader of mine wrote about a picture built to show it; it is not the screen, where a scorer who has not seen the picture marks each item against the description.

**The game of catch** (R1; tests 1, 2, 5 and 6). The thrower is the thrower in all four: a child to a teenager, "apparently having just thrown the ball." (1); a child to a man, "His right arm is stretched toward the ball as if he has just thrown it." (2); a small girl to a man, "has just thrown a red ball (a dashed line shows its flight from her hand) towards a man in a red shirt and blue trousers" (5); a teenager to a child, "a dashed line from his hand shows its path" (6). Two of the four offer the other way and set it aside: "but it could also be read as the boy having thrown it and the girl about to catch; the boy's ready-to-catch pose makes the first reading more likely." (1); "but the dash trail starting at her hand favours her throwing." (5). The catcher is ready to catch in each: "Both arms are raised in front of him with open hands, facing left toward the ball, ready to catch." (2). One reader of four still doubts the arms: "he could be reaching to catch or could just have thrown." (1). In the street the catcher's facing is in doubt, "the arms point left toward the thrower, but the head and body seem to face the shop window", and the ball is "close to the awning edge" (6).

**The square wheel** (R2; the barrow in tests 1, 3 and 7, the bicycle in test 6). Square in all four, and no reel: "The wheelbarrow wheel is square (looks like a box or a frame with a cross inside) rather than round" (1); "The wheelbarrow's wheel is a square frame (with a cross in it) instead of a round wheel." (3); "Bicycle wheels are square instead of round." (6); "its wheel is drawn as a square frame at the back and the barrow sits low or tilted, as if parked half on its side." (7). In tests 1 and 7 a hose is in the picture, the sprinkler's and the stopped one, and neither is by the barrow. Test 7's reader names the wheel only among the unsure things, and the barrow as tilted with it: the contradiction is seen and not said as one.

**The bread for the ducks** (R5; tests 3 and 5). In the hand in both: "With his other hand he holds out a piece of bread to a white duck" (3); "holding a slice of bread" (5). No cup and no container. One reader counts the things held: "The yellow-shirted boy seems to hold two things (a slice of bread and a second bread piece/bag); unclear exactly what is in each hand." (5).

**The lost balloon** (O1; tests 3, 4 and 8). Escaped in all three, and the string's end tied to nothing: "Its string ends in mid-air well above her hand, so she is not holding it." (3); "Its string dangles loose and is not attached to anything." (8). In the garden it ends on the fence's plain boards: "The yellow balloon's string ends in mid-air near the fence top, well away from the girl's hand." (4). What is left is the old doubt of whose it was, in two of three: "but from this picture alone you can't tell whether it was ever hers." (4); "or the child could be reaching for a balloon that already belonged to someone else or was already floating there." (8).

**The fence painter** (O3; tests 4 and 7). Painting in both, the tin at the feet in both: "Paint can (centre, on the grass at the woman's feet): grey can with blue paint, for the roller." (4); "a paint tin stands on the grass at his feet." (7). No pale cylinder. One reader of the teenager: "the roller head looks small and rather far from the blue boards, and it is unclear if it touches the fence." (7).

**The stopped hose** (R4; test 7; v0.50's drawing, kept by the bench). Offered as one reading of two, as before: "It could be read as stopping the water (so the woman checks the nozzle), or the dog may simply be standing beside the hose by chance." (7).

**The man in the red shirt** (O2; tests 4 and 5; v0.50's moustache, kept by the bench). A man in both, sweeping in one of them: "Man (right, middle ground, on the path near the gate): brown hair, red shirt, blue trousers." (4).

## What the readers added

- The duck feeder at the water's edge, where the template stands him on purpose: "his body overlaps the pond outline, so it is a little unclear whether his feet are on the bank or he is very close to the water." (3). O6 of the list.
- The hose's way to the holder's hands: "One length seems to pass behind or along her leg, which looks a bit tangled." (7). Said of every hose held at the waist since v0.25.
- The swing's one seat is not remarked in either garden: "Swing set (centre-right): grey metal A-frame with one red seat, empty and still." (4).

Nothing here asked for a rule, and the set was built on the calibration that ran beside these readings.
