# v0.57 design tests

Placement revision 51 holds the rules of v0.57, each behind a switch of its own (`scene.sw57(NAME)`; the header comment of `scenegen/scene.py` has each with what the readers wrote and what it costs). Most of them act where v0.56 already had a picture, and were read there, in v0.56's own pictures built again (`isolation_v057.md`). Two could only be read in new pictures, and those are the design tests: fifteen pictures built with forced sets under revision 51, each read by fresh readers who were dealt them among the bench's other pictures.

| Rule (switch) | The fault it is for | New pictures | Readings | Read with no doubt | The fault named |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The ball and its dashes off the fence's top line, away from a cat on the fence and off the pond (`CATCHLINE`), with the flat flight (`ARC`) | a throw over the fence or at the cat | 5 (and the same 5 with the old arc) | 10 (and 5) | 4 of 10 (1 of 5) | the fence, a cat or the pond with the ball: 0 of 10 (0 of 5; in v0.56's own gardens 4 of 4) |
| The catcher is a child or a teenager, and the taller of the two throws (`CATCHCAST`) | who threw the ball, where a grown figure with raised hands caught it | 5 | 10 | 6 of 10 | who threw it: 2 of 10, for 4 of 8 where a grown figure caught |

**Round 1** (`isolation_v057/bench`, arms `F` and `G`): two gardens, a park, a playground and a street, each built twice on the same seed, with the flat flight and the line rule and with v0.56's arc. The flat flight was read as intended wherever a child caught it. Where a grown figure caught a child's throw, four readings of eight asked who had thrown: "- The man's raised hands could be read as catching, or as a startled/defensive gesture." That is what the casting rule is for.

**Round 2** (`isolation_v057/bench2`, arm `H`): five new pictures with the casting rule, among them a garden with a cat on the fence and a painter at it, and a playground with partners that need no child. No reading names the fence or the cat with the ball. Two readings ask who threw it, both where two small children of one height play, and both settle it by the dashes: "- The ball's dotted line starts at the thrower's hand and stops at the ball, so it reads as thrown toward the girl in pink; it could alternatively be read as the girl in pink having thrown it, but the trail suggests the girl in yellow threw it."

**Left:** the garden fence's two rails have no anchor, and the flat ball can stand on one; no reader remarked on it. A game beside an angler places in no arm, v0.56's code included.

**What the other rules were read in.** The pond's own figures, the wind's lines off the pond, the kite's slack string, the seat and the buffers, the pan's sheet of water and the unused watering can were each read in v0.56's pictures built again on their own records' plans, where the old drawing's reading is known: 27 pictures in round 1 and ten in round 2. The pictures that did not come back on their own plan are in `isolation_v057.md`: the tier-4 park, which held the kite, and one plain seesaw.

No picture of the set or the probes was built again after a reading.
