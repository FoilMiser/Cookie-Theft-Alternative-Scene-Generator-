# v0.46 isolation bench

The brief for v0.46 was Sam's: "make moves to improve the art style for v.046 and clean up issues like the woman in the yellow dress having a neck that is too long". The art-style item was already on file: the v0.45 set review (`tpdb_work/review45/review_set.md`, item 26, "Two outline systems in one picture") found that "people and animals have soft shading and thin outlines in a darker shade of their own colour; props and set have bold black outlines and flat fills. In colour it is acceptable; in grey it is the main reason figures sink into the background", and asked for "a dark outline of constant width on the cast". The figure clean-ups came from Sam's remark and from my own look at v0.45's cast at full size. The scene items of `flaws_v045.md` (R1 to R3, O1 to O6) were not worked on in this release; they are carried to `flaws_v046.md`.

The bench is eight tier-3 pictures, two each of the street, the park, the playground and the garden (cases `stx`, `pkx`, `pgx`, `gdx`, `_1` and `_2`; seeds 46011 to 46018), with events forced among the planner's own so that all ten people of the cast and both dogs stand in at least one. Each was built on revision 43's rules in v0.45's cast and in v0.46's on the same seed, so only the cast differs. Every reader and reviewer was a fresh subagent on Opus that was given its prompt's text and nothing else, as every reader and scorer of this release is. There were five rounds:
- round 1: twelve readers of 24 whole pictures, two to a reader, with the screen's own whole-picture prompt (`tpdb_work/iso_round45.py`): the eight new pictures were read twice, by different readers (`isolation_v046/round1a`, `round1b`), the eight old ones once (`round1o`). And four pair reviewers, two pairs each (`pairs1`, `tpdb_work/v046/pairs.py`): the two whole pictures of a scene side by side as "a" and "b", which is which drawn at random, with questions about how the people and animals are drawn, whether they belong to the drawing round them, and which version the reviewer would use;
- round 2: the new pictures built again after round 1's changes: four readers (`round2a`) and four pair reviewers (`pairs2`);
- round 3: four figure reviewers of twelve pairs, three each (`figs3`, `tpdb_work/v046/figpairs.py`): one figure cut out of a bench picture in both casts and enlarged to 640 px high (in the pictures the twelve are 216 to 320 px tall), again as "a" and "b" at random, with questions about faults, differences, the neck and the head, the outline and the shading, and which is better drawn;
- round 4: six figure reviewers of sixteen pairs, with round 3's prompt and the release's own cast: round 3's twelve figures again, round 3's drawing against the release's (`figs4`, three pairs each for four reviewers), and four of them, v0.45's drawing against the release's (`figs4o`, two pairs each for two reviewers). In each folder half the pairs had the newer drawing as "a";
- round 5: seven figure reviewers of fourteen pairs, two each, with the same prompt, after the mouth was drawn again: six of the twelve figures, round 4's drawing against the release's (`figs5`: mouth C against mouth D, the one thing that differs), and eight of the twelve, v0.45's drawing against the release's (`figs5o`). Again half the pairs in each folder had the newer drawing as "a".

`map.json` in each folder says which picture is which case. The first three letters of a case name the scene, the fourth the cast: O v0.45's (`BENCH_CAST=assets/cast/out045`, its library copied to `tpdb_work/v046/lib_O`), N v0.46's. N was five casts in turn, and three of them were rebuilt only in part (the runbook's "a bench cast rebuilt in part is two casts": what was rebuilt is named here):
- round 1: `tpdb_work/v046/out_b5`, every family built with the five round-1 switches at B;
- round 2: `out_b6`, which is `out_b5` with the eight people who are not small children built again (`TPDB_COLLAR46`, `TPDB_GLASSW46` and `TPDB_MOUSTACHE46` at B);
- round 3: `out_b7`, which is `out_b6` with ben and tomas built again (`TPDB_MOUSTACHE46` C);
- round 4: `assets/cast/out046`, the release's own cast, every family built again with every switch at the letter it then had;
- round 5: the same folder with the ten people built again (`TPDB_MOUTH46` D). The eight animals have no such mouth, and their files are round 4's.

Three forced sets were swapped before any reading for ones the planner could place: `pgx_1` lost its swing, `gdx_2` went from the grazing goat to the washing line to the carried box, and the bench reader of `pgx_2` became a photographer. The eight accepted pictures came out on the same seeds in every cast.

The changes are in the cast, behind switches read when it is built ("O" is the drawing before the switch). Five went into round 1:

Looked at and left before the readers, on sheets of single figures at picture scale (`tpdb_work/v046/styles.py`): one 9 px line for the edge and the inner lines alike; the two line weights over v0.45's three soft tones; and the two line weights over flat colour with no shadow tone at all.

## The line's colour

Before round 4 the release's cast was built whole (`assets/cast/out046`), its library and its gallery were rendered, and the validator was run on them, which the runbook asks of a bench winner before it is chosen. All 386 library records and all 180 gallery records passed. But 374 and 175 of them carried the soft warning that under 90 % of a figure's solid pixels are palette colours, where v0.45 had 83 of 382 and 38 of 180, and the median share had fallen from 0.921 to 0.821.

The colour off the palette was the line's own. Line Art's strokes were lit by the scene's lamp like any surface and came out at (28, 28, 34), where the palette's outline colour, which the settings are drawn in, is (38, 38, 46). It had been so in every release since Line Art came in, and a thin line hid it. At the new widths the line is 7 to 8 % of a figure's solid pixels.

`TPDB_INK46` B: the Line Art layers take no light, so the line is the palette's colour. Rendered again, 386 of 386 and 180 of 180 passed, 154 and 69 carried the warning, and the median share was 0.905 and 0.909 (lowest 0.809, a pecking bird). What is left is no colour of its own. Measured on four gallery records (mei, nia, a dog and a bird), the off-palette pixels within 2 px of a line are 6.5, 10.4, 8.8 and 15.9 % of the solid pixels (v0.45: 2.3, 4.8, 2.6 and 8.2) and those elsewhere 1.4, 1.2, 1.0 and 3.3 % (v0.45: 4.5, 5.5, 4.7 and 9.9). It is the blended band where a line meets a fill, which grows with the line and is largest on a small figure; the soft tones' gradients, v0.45's share, are gone. The 90 % target was set when the line was thin. It stays as it is, a soft warning: the validator was not loosened, and the count is on the flaws list.

## The mouth drawn again

`TPDB_MOUTH46` D, the release's mouth, is v0.45's mouth made as geometry. v0.45's line mouth was a thin line in the mouth's colour that Line Art edged in dark. Under v0.46's heavier line that edge closed over the colour (round 3's drawing, "a dark smudge" to a reviewer of round 4), and C, which took the edge away and thickened the line, could be read as a cut. In D the coloured line has v0.45's radius again and rides on a wider line in the outline's colour that is sunk into the face by half its radius and runs a little past the coloured line at both ends, so that about 2.5 px of dark show all round the colour at render size, half the weight of the lines inside a figure, as v0.45's edge was.
