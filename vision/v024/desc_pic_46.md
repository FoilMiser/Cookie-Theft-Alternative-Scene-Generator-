# Picture 46

## Free description

This is a back garden on a sunny day, with a cream house with a red roof and blue door on the left, a tall wooden fence along the back, and a gate and paved path on the right. In the middle, a woman with a long dark ponytail, purple top and teal trousers stands with both arms stretched forward while a red-and-white ball flies towards her hands. A dashed curved line traces the ball's flight back to a child in a yellow top and orange shorts, who has an arm stretched out, so the child has just thrown it and she is about to catch it. In the foreground, a white dog with brown spots crouches low in a play bow with its tail up and wagging, nose right next to a yellow-green tennis ball on the grass. On the left, in front of the blue door, a tan dog with a white chest sits with its eyes shut, holding a red lead in its mouth, as if asking to go for a walk. At the far right, an elderly woman with a grey bun and glasses, teal cardigan and grey skirt stands on the path sweeping with a broom with yellow bristles. A round flower bed full of colourful flowers, two pots of green plants and a teal watering can decorate the lawn. Everyone is busy with their own activity, and nothing looks wrong or impossible.

## Unsure or ambiguous

- The ball is level with the top of the woman's head and slightly ahead of her hands, so it could be read as about to land in her hands, or as going over them. The dashed line suggests it came from the child, but with no arrow it could in principle be read as she threw it to the child; the ball being close to her hands while the dashed line runs to the child points to the first reading.
- The tan dog holding its own lead could be a dog wanting a walk, or one just playing with the lead. Its closed eyes could mean it is happy or waiting patiently.
- The spotted dog's pose is contorted: its front legs stretch out along the ground and its rear stands high, which reads as a play bow but looks a little awkward. It could be about to pick up the tennis ball or just be sniffing it.
- There are two separate balls (red-and-white and tennis ball), but it is not clear whether the second ball belongs to the same game.
