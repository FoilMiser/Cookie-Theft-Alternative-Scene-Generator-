# desc_test_3

## Free description

It is a sunny day in a back garden, with a cream-coloured house with a red roof and a blue door on the left and a tall wooden fence along the back with a gate on the right. Behind the fence are a green hedge and a tree. Two people are cleaning up. At the back right, an old man in a grey cap, white shirt, brown waistcoat and dark trousers stands near the fence and sweeps a pile of orange, red and brown autumn leaves with a yellow broom. At the front right, a boy in a red shirt and blue trousers stands on a pale stone path that runs from the gate to the bottom of the picture. He is using a huge silver fork that looks like a giant dinner fork from the kitchen, not a garden tool. He scrapes it along the path toward a small pile of dirt and grit, which is throwing up a little puff of dust. A red dustpan lies on the ground just left of the pile, so he seems to be trying to push the dirt into it. That is the odd part: you would sweep dirt into a dustpan with a broom or brush, and a fork's prongs would let most of it slip through. Elsewhere there is a bed of colourful flowers, two potted plants, a grey wheelbarrow and a teal watering can, all just sitting on the grass.

## Unsure or ambiguous

- The boy's tool: it looks most like a giant kitchen or dinner fork (silver, curved prongs, cutlery-style handle). Another reading is that it is meant to be a garden fork or rake drawn oddly. Either way it is the wrong tool for sweeping dirt into a dustpan, and it seems deliberately silly.
- What the boy is doing with the dirt: he is probably pushing it toward the dustpan, but he could also just be raking or scratching at the path. The dust puff suggests he is stirring the dirt up instead of collecting it.
- The dustpan is on the grass, a little away from the boy and not held by anyone. It could have been put down ready to use, or just dropped.
- There is no tree near the old man, so it is unclear where his pile of leaves came from (the only tree is far left, behind the fence). This is a minor point.
- Scale: the old man looks smaller than the boy because he is further back, which seems to be intended perspective. Nothing obviously floats or passes through anyone. The broom handle and the fork handle both sit naturally in the hands holding them.
