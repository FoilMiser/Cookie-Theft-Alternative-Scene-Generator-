# Picture 61

## Free description
This is a paved town square in front of a row of four shops, under a grey sky with three dark storm clouds overhead. A man in a red shirt and blue trousers stands near the middle, holding a green watering can and pouring water towards a stone planter of flowers. The water leaves the can in an odd way: it arcs upwards in a thin blue stream to the left and there are droplets above it, rather than simply falling on the flowers. A woman in a purple top and teal trousers stands to the right of a bench, looking up with one arm raised and pointing at the sky, apparently at the dark clouds. It looks as though she is noticing that rain is coming, which would make the man's watering pointless. No rain is actually falling yet. There is a fruit and vegetable stall on the left with a cardboard box beside it, a lamp post, a bench, a tree in a round planter, a green bin, a red bicycle in a metal rack at the front, a small café table with two chairs, and two pot plants in the front corners. Nobody else is around.

## Unsure or ambiguous
- The water stream is the strangest detail. It curves up and to the left from the can, then seems to come down onto the flowers, so it could be a normal pour drawn awkwardly, or water going the wrong way. The shape looks more like a thin bent ribbon than a spray, and there are two or three droplets floating above it.
- The woman could be pointing at the storm clouds (warning about rain) or at something on or above the building, such as a window. Her finger points up and to the left; the clouds are the only obvious target.
- It is unclear whether the man has noticed the woman or the clouds. He seems absorbed in watering, so the point could be that watering is unnecessary because rain is on its way.
- The planter's position in front of the green shop door makes it look as if the door is partly blocked, and the man's hands overlap the flowers, so it is hard to tell exactly where the can sits relative to the planter.
- The red bicycle's frame and the grey rack overlap, so it is hard to tell whether it is locked or merely leaning.
