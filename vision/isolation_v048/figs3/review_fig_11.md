# Review fig 11

## 1. Who and what
A child (boy, dark skin, short curly hair, yellow T-shirt, orange shorts, blue shoes) standing still turned three-quarters to the viewer's right, hands loosely closed, holding nothing. In a he looks worried (open mouth); in b he looks calm with a slight smile.

## 2. Faults in a
- Ear: none drawn; the side of the head is a plain flat area.
- Mouth: small open pink mouth reads as worried or startled.
- Head: very large and wide compared with the body (acceptable for a child, but heavy).
- Neck: thin stalk under the big head.
- Near (viewer's left) arm: comes out from under the sleeve with no shoulder shape; long and tube-like.
- Far arm: thin and pressed against the side, joined loosely.
- Hands: mitten-like fists without fingers.
- Shorts: the leg openings are very wide and boxy.

## 3. Faults in b
- Head: large and wide for the body, though the ear helps it read as a head side.
- Neck: thin under the big head.
- Near arm: same tube-like arm with no shoulder shape.
- Far arm: thin and pressed to the side.
- Hands: mitten-like fists without fingers.
- Shorts: wide boxy leg openings, as in a.

## 4. Differences
- Ear: b has an ear drawn; a has none.
- Mouth: a open and worried; b a closed slight smile.
- Face: b's eyes and brow sit a little differently, giving a calmer look.
- Torso: b's T-shirt is slightly longer, the shorts start lower.
- Legs: b's legs look slightly shorter.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: head big with a thin neck; shoulders not shown, arms long tubes; hands fingerless fists; hips and legs reasonable for a child; feet fine.
- b: same proportions, with an ear making the head read better; arms, hands, legs and feet as in a.

## 6. Pose
- a: a neutral standing pose with a slight turn; natural enough but stiff in the arms, and the open mouth makes him look startled.
- b: the same pose; reads as relaxed and natural, still slightly stiff in the arms.

## 7. Better drawn
b. The added ear makes the head read properly and the calm expression is more natural; the rest of the drawing is essentially the same as a.
