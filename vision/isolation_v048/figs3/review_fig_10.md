# Review: fig 10

## 1. Who and what
An older man with grey hair, a grey moustache and a blue flat cap, in a brown waistcoat over a cream long-sleeved shirt, dark trousers and brown shoes, standing side-on and holding a long fishing rod with both hands, angled up to the right. In a the face is turned three-quarters toward the viewer, with eyes closed. In b the head is in profile, with an ear showing and the face at the right edge.

## 2. Faults in a
- Head: turned toward the viewer while the body is side-on, so the head looks twisted on the neck.
- Eyes: drawn closed, so he looks asleep or dozing rather than watching the rod.
- Legs: very long and thin compared with the short torso; the trousers look stretched.
- Waist: the trousers bunch in a thick dark band at the waist below the waistcoat, which looks heavy.
- Feet: the two shoes are stacked one over the other and look flat.
- Rod: no fishing line is drawn; the rod reads as a long pole.

## 3. Faults in b
- Face: the closed eye, nose and moustache are squeezed onto the right edge of the head, so the face is small and hard to read.
- Cap: the brim is longer and pointier, so it reads more like a baseball cap than a flat cap.
- Eyes: again closed, so he looks asleep.
- Legs, waist, feet and rod: the same long thin legs, bunched waistband, stacked shoes and missing line as in a.

## 4. Differences
- Head turn: a is three-quarter view facing the viewer; b is closer to profile, facing the rod.
- Ear: b shows an ear on the side of the head; a has none visible.
- Face: a shows the closed eyes, nose and moustache clearly in the middle of the face; b pushes them to the edge.
- Cap: b's brim is longer and more peaked; a's is a flatter cap.
- Hair: b shows more grey hair at the side and back of the head.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: The head is a good size but turned against the body, so the neck join looks twisted. The shoulders are narrow under the waistcoat. Both arms are bent forward and the hands grip the rod plausibly, one above the other. The torso is short. The hips sit low under the bunched waistband. The legs are too long and thin. The feet are flat and stacked. Nothing is detached.
- b: The head is in line with the side-on body, so the neck join looks more natural. The arms, hands, torso, legs and feet are the same as in a: the legs are too long and thin and the feet stacked.

## 6. Pose
- a: The two-handed grip on the rod reads well as fishing. Standing very upright, slightly leaning back, with the head turned out and eyes closed, he looks posed or asleep rather than attending to the rod.
- b: The same grip and upright stance. With the head in profile facing along the rod, the pose reads more naturally as watching the line, though the closed eye still suggests dozing.

## 7. Better drawn
b, narrowly. The head is in line with the side-on body and faces the rod, so the man reads as fishing without the twisted neck of a, though a's face is easier to see.
