# Review of pair 11

## 1. Who and what
A brown dog with a red collar, eyes closed and tongue out, doing a stretch or play bow: front end down, rear end up, tail raised. In a the forelegs lie flat on the ground (a clear play bow). In b the forelegs reach forward on a slant with the chest still off the ground, so it reads more as a forward stretch or lunge.

## 2. Faults in a
- Tongue: a small red oval at the very tip of the muzzle, below the nose. It looks stuck on rather than coming out of the mouth.
- Hind legs: straight, thick columns with almost no hock angle. They look stiff and post-like.
- Hip: a curved inner line on the haunch and a second line lower on the thigh. They read as stray seams more than muscle.
- Forelegs: the forearms and paws are one long, flat slab on the ground. There is no clear elbow, wrist or toe shape, and the near and far forelegs merge.
- Head: large compared with the body and tipped steeply up. The muzzle points skyward, which is a little odd for a bow.

## 3. Faults in b
- Forelegs: long, straight tubes with no visible elbow or wrist bend, ending in blob-shaped paws. They look like planks propped against the ground.
- Chest and foreleg join: a crease line where the forelegs leave the chest makes the legs look attached on, not grown from the shoulder.
- Collar: drawn as a red patch or bib on the front of the neck rather than a band going round it, so it looks unfinished.
- Hip: a notch or crease where the near hind leg meets the rump, and a fold line on the thigh. The join looks pinched.
- Tongue: a tiny red sliver at the mouth, barely readable.
- Body: long and stretched, a little tube-like between the shoulders and hips.

## 4. Differences
- Forelegs: flat on the ground with the elbows down in a; reaching forward on a diagonal with straight legs in b.
- Chest: low, near the ground in a; higher off the ground in b.
- Hind legs: both near-vertical and straight in a; in b the near hind leg is angled with a slight knee bend, and the far leg is set further back.
- Head: larger and tipped more steeply upward in a; smaller, rounder and more level in b.
- Ear: smaller oval in a; larger and longer in b.
- Collar: a full red band wrapping the neck in a; a red patch or bib shape in b.
- Tongue: a separate red oval below the nose in a; a small sliver at the mouth in b.
- Muzzle: a larger white muzzle patch in a; a narrower white patch in b.
- Tail: thicker at the base in a; thinner and more tapered in b.
- Body: shorter and more steeply sloped in a; longer and flatter in b.
- Paws: long and flat front paws in a; round blob paws at the end of straight legs in b.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: The head is a bit big for the body and the neck is short but joins well behind the collar. The body reads as solid. Forelegs have no clear elbow or wrist and blend into one flat mass. Hind legs are too straight and thick, with almost no hock. The tail is well joined. Overall the proportions hold together, with stiff hind legs.
- b: The head is well sized and joined. The body is somewhat long and thin through the middle. Forelegs are too long and straight, with no joints, and look attached at a crease at the chest. Paws are blobby. The hind legs have a slight bend but are pinched at the hip join. The tail is well joined. The odd join is the forelegs at the chest.

## 6. Pose
- a: Reads naturally as a dog's play bow or stretch: elbows on the ground, rump high, tail up. The stiff column hind legs and the head tipped sharply skyward make it a little rigid, but the action is clear.
- b: Less clear. With straight, unbent forelegs propped forward and the chest raised, it reads as stiff, somewhere between a stretch and a lunge. The forelegs look like poles more than legs taking weight.

## 7. Better drawn
a. Its forelegs rest on the ground in a believable play bow and the collar is a proper band. In b the forelegs are rigid, jointless props joined at a crease, and the collar is unfinished.
