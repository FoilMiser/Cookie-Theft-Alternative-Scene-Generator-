# Review fig 14

## 1. Who and what
A white duck with an orange bill and orange foot, standing still in side view facing right. Both versions read the same.

## 2. Faults in a
- Neck: drawn as a separate tube with outline lines across its top (under the head) and across its base (into the body), so the head, neck and body look like stacked separate pieces.
- Legs/feet: only one leg and foot shown, set under the rear-middle of the body; the foot is a flat doubled orange shape.
- Tail: a straight rectangular stub sticking up and back, looks pasted on.
- Wing: a pale oval lying on the body with an extra curved shape above it on the back; reads flat.
- Bill: long flat bill drawn as two stacked slabs, slightly detached-looking from the head.

## 3. Faults in b
- Legs/feet: only one leg and foot shown, set under the rear-middle of the body; foot is a flat doubled orange shape.
- Tail: straight rectangular stub, looks pasted on.
- Wing: flat pale oval with an extra curved shape on the back above it.
- Bill: long flat two-slab bill.
- Neck: smooth, but a slight bump where the back of the neck meets the head.

## 4. Differences
- a has join lines across the neck, at the head and at the body; b's head, neck and body flow as one continuous outline.
- Otherwise none seen: body, wing, tail, bill, eye, leg and foot appear identical.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: head and bill fine in size; neck reads as a detached tube because of the joining lines; body round and full; one leg only, thin, foot flat; tail a stiff stub.
- b: head, neck and body join smoothly and read as one bird; body fine; one leg only, thin, foot flat; tail a stiff stub.

## 6. Pose
- a: standing, stiff and upright; acceptable for a duck standing still, but the segmented neck makes it look assembled.
- b: same standing pose, reads more naturally because the neck flows into the body.

## 7. Better drawn
b — it is the same drawing but without the lines across the neck, so the duck looks like one body rather than parts stacked together.
