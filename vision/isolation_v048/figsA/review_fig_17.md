# Review of figure 17

## 1. Who and what
A brown dog with a red collar, a dark brown ear and a cream muzzle and chest, standing on all four legs, facing right with its tail up; both versions read the same.

## 2. Faults in a
- Legs: all four are very long and thin for the body, so the dog looks stilt-legged.
- Front legs: the near front leg starts low under the chest as a straight tube, with no shoulder or elbow shape.
- Hind legs: the near hind leg has a sharp backward kink at the hock that looks bent the wrong way, close to a human knee.
- Belly: the outline under the body between the legs is a plain gap with no belly curve, so the legs look like posts set under a box.
- Far legs: the darker far legs are almost as long and as visible as the near legs, which adds to the "too many long legs" look.

## 3. Faults in b
- Hip and haunch: an extra jagged, wobbly line crosses the near hip and thigh, like a crease or tear; it looks unfinished.
- Front legs: the far front leg is a straight dark tube of even width with a flat top, set against the chest like a separate post; the join is abrupt.
- Belly: a short lumpy line runs along the underside where the near legs meet the body, so the join looks patched.
- Collar: the collar is a wide red bib shape rather than a band; it reads more as a scarf.
- Legs: still long and tube-like, with little shape at the knees or hocks.

## 4. Differences
- Legs: b's legs are a little shorter and thicker relative to the body than a's.
- Hind leg: a's near hind leg has a strong backward kink at the hock; b's is a softer, rounder bend.
- Hip: b has an extra jagged crease line across the near hip; a has a smooth hip with only a small notch.
- Collar: a's collar is a narrow curved band; b's is wider and hangs like a bib or scarf.
- Ear: b's ear is a little larger and hangs lower over the collar.
- Muzzle: a's muzzle is longer and more pointed, with a small mouth line; b's is shorter and rounder.
- Head: b's head is slightly larger relative to the body.
- Chest: the white chest patch is a bit smaller and more oval in b.
- Tail: b's tail is a bit thicker at the base and straighter; a's is thinner and curves back.
- Far front leg: in b it is a straight even tube with a flat top; in a it tapers and blends more into the chest.
- Body: b's body is slightly deeper and shorter.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: Head and neck fine and well joined; body reasonable; legs too long and too thin; front legs lack shoulder and elbow shape; near hind leg kinks sharply backward at the hock; feet are rounded blobs, acceptable; tail fine.
- b: Head slightly large but well joined; body fine; legs still long but better in thickness; far front leg looks attached like a separate post; extra crease lines at the near hip and underside make the hind leg join look patched; feet rounded blobs, acceptable; tail fine.

## 6. Pose
- a: A standing pose, readable, but stiff and stilted; the long straight legs and the backward-kinked hind leg make it look like the dog is on stilts or crouching oddly behind.
- b: A standing pose that reads more naturally as a dog standing still, with steadier proportions, though the legs are still post-like and stiff.

## 7. Better drawn
b, slightly: its leg length and thickness and the hind-leg bend look more like a real dog, whereas a looks stilt-legged. b's stray crease line on the hip is a flaw that a does not have.
