# Review fig 05

## 1. Who and what
A young man or teenager with brown hair, in a red cap, blue hooded top, dark trousers and white trainers, walking forward while pointing ahead and upward with one arm outstretched. In a he looks surprised or calling out (open mouth); in b he is smiling.

## 2. Faults in a
- Pointing hand: a flat, narrow wedge with no thumb or separate finger, so it reads more like a blade than a pointing hand.
- Pointing arm: completely straight and rigid from shoulder to hand, and very long.
- Back hand: the hand of the arm swung back is a round lump with a stub.
- Back arm: the upper arm of the swinging arm is hidden and the forearm seems to come out of the side of the hoodie.
- Hood/neck: the hood sits behind the neck as a lump; the neck is a separate pale band between hood and head.
- Rear foot: the back trainer is tilted steeply and looks large.
- Face: two eyes are shown on a mostly side-on head, and the open mouth sits on the edge of the face.

## 3. Faults in b
- Pointing hand: the same flat wedge hand as a.
- Pointing arm: the same rigid, very long straight arm.
- Back hand and back arm: the same lumpy hand and arm coming out of the hoodie's side.
- Hood/neck: the same lump of hood behind the neck.
- Rear foot: the same steeply tilted, large back trainer.
- Mouth: the smile is a small red mark on the edge of the face.

## 4. Differences
- Eyes: a shows two eyes; b shows one eye, with the head more side-on.
- Mouth: a has an open mouth (talking or surprised); b has a closed smile.
- Eyebrows: a has raised eyebrows; b has one smaller brow.
- Ear: in b the ear is a little larger and more visible.
- The figure in b sits a little lower in the frame; body, clothes, arms and legs otherwise look the same.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: Head and neck join fine though the neck shows as a separate band; pointing arm too long and rigid; pointing hand a thin wedge; back hand a lump; hips fine; legs long but in proportion; feet attached, back foot large and steeply angled.
- b: Same as a in every body part; the side-on head matches the side-on body better.

## 6. Pose
- a: Reads as walking and pointing, but the dead-straight pointing arm and flat hand look stiff; the walking stride is natural; the two-eyed face does not match the side-on body.
- b: Same stride and the same stiff arm; the side-on face with one eye fits the body direction better.

## 7. Better drawn
b, slightly: the one-eyed side-on face matches the turn of the body, while a's two-eyed face looks twisted toward the viewer; the body and limbs are the same in both.
