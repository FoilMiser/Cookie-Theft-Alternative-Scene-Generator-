# v0.58's bench, round 1: brief for an analyst (common part)

You count what blind readers wrote about one thing in a set of cartoon pictures. You never see a picture, and you open exactly two files: this brief's row file (named to you) and the tally file it names. Do not open pictures, code, keys or any other file.

**What the bench is.** Twenty-three pictures of the last release (v0.57) were built again with one thing drawn another way. Fresh readers, who know nothing of the intended reading, described each picture in three sections: a free description (lines marked `F:` in the tally), an inventory (`I:`) and a section of what they were unsure of or found ambiguous or strange (`U:`). The tally holds, for every reading, only the lines that touch the row's subject.

**Arms.**
- `S`: what the reader of v0.57's own screen wrote about the same picture as v0.57 drew it (one reading a picture).
- `O`: a fresh reading of the same picture as v0.57 drew it.
- `B`: the picture with v0.58's new drawing.
- `C`: the picture with an alternative new drawing (not every row has one).
S and O are the same drawing; count them apart and also together as "old".

**Readers.** In arms O, B and C a reader has two pictures of the row (one in the two-arm rows), always in the same arm. So a habit of one reader shows twice in one arm. Give every count twice: by reading (of the arm's readings) and by reader (of the arm's readers, a reader counted when any of its readings has the thing).

**How to count.**
- Read every reading whole (all its lines) before putting it in a class. A hedge is a hedge wherever it stands; say in which section it stands (description, inventory, or only the unsure section).
- A class is defined in the row file. If a reading fits no class, say so and quote it; do not force it.
- Quote the readers' own words for every class, at least two quotations where there are two, each on one line, copied exactly, with the picture and reader as the tally's header gives them (for example `T3_garden_s23030 arm B reader 4 (pic_017)`).
- Do not judge which drawing is better. Say what was written, in numbers, and what the numbers cannot show (too few readings, one reader behind a count).

**What to write.** One file, with the Write tool, at the path the row file names: a table of the classes by arm (S, O, old = S + O, B, C), by reading and by reader; under it the quotations by class; then a list of anything a reader found odd about the row's subject that the classes do not hold, with counts by arm; and a closing paragraph of at most eight sentences on what differs between the arms and what does not. Then reply with that closing paragraph and the table only.
