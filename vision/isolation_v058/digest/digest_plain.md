## How the classes were applied

- A reading is "with a hedge" when a hedge on the class's subject stands anywhere in it, even if the description or inventory says the thing plainly. The section of the hedge is given with the quotations.
- Class 1: plain when both children are said to be on, sit on, ride or play on the seesaw and no line doubts that either is on its seat; hedged when a line doubts whether one of them is on the seat or the board.
- Class 2 holds remarks on the raised child's legs, placement or posture. A bare "sits on the raised end" is not a remark. "Holding the handle" is counted apart (list under the quotations), not in class 2.
- Class 3: nobody in any arm says the raised child is stuck, kept up or unable to get down. Everything counted here is a weight or size given for the tilt. A remark that the two are the same size "yet" the seesaw tilts, with no weight or size offered, is not class 3 and is counted in the list at the end.
- Class 4 holds "feet on the ground" (the mirror of "legs dangling") and doubts about the low child's place at the seat. "Holding the handle" is counted apart.
- Class 5: a remark on the handle that is not phrased as where a child sits. A handle remark phrased as where a child sits is in class 2 or 4 and is not counted again.

## Closing

All 20 readings have the two children on a seesaw (old 12 of 12, B 8 of 8), and a doubt about whether a child is properly on its seat attaches to that in S 2 of 5, O 1 of 7 and B 1 of 8 readings, always in the unsure section only; B's one is about the low child. The clearest difference is the raised child's legs: they are said to hang, dangle or be in the air in old 3 of 12 (S 0 of 5, O 3 of 7) and in B 7 of 8, in B always in the description or inventory. Remarks that place her off or above the seat, in front of or beside the handle, floating or over the handle stand in old 3 of 12 (S 2 of 5, both hedged; O 1 of 7) and B 0 of 8, and any remark on her seat, legs or posture in the unsure section in old 5 of 12 and B 0 of 8; no reading in any arm mentions a foot on the plank or hands on the knees. She is said plainly to hold the handle in old 8 of 12 (one more O reader could not tell) and B 8 of 8. Nobody in any arm says the raised child is stuck, kept up or unable to get down; a weight or size is offered as the reason for the tilt in S 5 of 5 (one a loose fit), O 2 of 7 and B 3 of 8, hedged in both O cases and stated flatly in two of B's three (readers 9 and 15, who both also call the girl smaller). The low child is treated alike: feet on the ground in old 8 of 12 and B 7 of 8, a hedged doubt about his place at the seat in O 1 of 7 and B 1 of 8, plus one B reader (7) unsure of the handle across his body. What the numbers cannot show: each O and B reader has one picture here, so by reading and by reader are the same and no count rests on a reader's repeated habit, but the arms hold only 5, 7 and 8 readings, and S and O, which are one drawing, differ from each other (class 3: 5 of 5 against 2 of 7) by more than O differs from B on that class, so gaps of two or three readings cannot be laid to the drawing. The old placement remarks on the raised child all come from two of the five pictures (s23704 and s23730), which have only three B readings between them, and the S headers name no reader.
