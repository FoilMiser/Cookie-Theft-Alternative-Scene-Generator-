## How the rows were cut

- "Flat" means said without may, could, seems, appears, as if, possibly or an "or" beside it. "Only with a hedge" means the thing stands nowhere in the reading without one.
- Where things stand. Class 3 stands only in the unsure section, except s23742 B reader 13 (description and unsure). Every class 5 remark stands in the unsure section; the one exception is s23708 S's inventory line "sitting on the seat", which its own unsure section takes back. Every class 6 "odd" remark stands in the unsure section, except s23708 S (inventory and unsure) and s23708 C reader 14 (description and unsure).
- WEIGHT. "much bigger" (B reader 1, both readings) is counted as weight; without it B's "any form" and "flat, in the description" rows are 5 · 4. "tilted down heavily on his side" (s23709 B reader 7) and "heavily tipped toward the man" (s23741 O reader 3, description) are not counted as the man's weight. s23741 B reader 7 turns the inference round ("tipped steeply ..., so he is much heavier") and is counted. s23709 O reader 12 gives it by likeness ("As with a heavy adult on a seesaw") and is counted flat, unsure section only.
- WEIGHT and the other readings. A flat weight line is kept flat when the same reading's unsure section offers that he holds the seesaw or the girl on purpose without naming weight (s24740 S; s23708 and s23709 O reader 6; s24740 O reader 9; s23741 B reader 13), and when the unsure section names weight again as one of two readings (s23741 S; s23708 and s24740 B reader 10). If both kinds are taken as hedges on weight, "flat, in the description" becomes S 1, O 1, old 2/15, B 4/10, C 5/10.
- STUCK. Counted when the girl is said to be stuck, kept up, held up, kept raised or unable to come down. "holding the seesaw down" (class 3: s24740 S, s23708 and s23709 O reader 6, s24740 O reader 9) is not counted as the girl being kept up; if it were, old's "any form" would be 11/15. "holding the girl up" and "keeping her raised" (B reader 13, both readings) are counted, always hedged, and are that reader's only form of it.
- The doubts counted in "the unsure section comes back to it with a doubt" are of three kinds: about her face only (s23741 S, s24740 C reader 2, s23708 C reader 14), "appears stuck" (s23709 O reader 12), and another event offered (s23741 O reader 12: the seesaw suddenly dropping; B reader 10 twice: normal play).
- s23742 C reader 11 is counted in STUCK for "keeps the girl up in the air", though the same sentence opens "Nothing looks wrong".
- Class 4. No reading uses "playing" or "taking turns" without also naming weight or stuck, so the row counts readings that describe the two on the seesaw with no word of stuck, kept up or weight. Two of C's four (reader 11 on s23741, reader 14 on s23709) carry an unsure line that she may be slipping, so they are not readings of "nothing odd".
- Class 5. "sits on the low end", "feet on the ground" (old 14/15, B 10/10, C 9/10) and "hands on (his) knees" (old 2/15, B 0/10, C 3/10) are not counted as remarks. "5 any remark" is the union of the three rows under it; s23708 S, s24740 O reader 9, B reader 7 (twice) and s24740 C reader 8 stand in two of them.
- Class 6. "holding the handle" and "sits on the raised end" are not counted. "looking down at him" or "looks down toward the man" stands in old 3/15 (s23741 S, s23741 O reader 3, s24740 O reader 9), B 0/10, C 0/10, and is not counted as a pose remark.

## Closing paragraph

The arms differ most on the man's seat: a reader puts him off the seat or not surely seated (in front of the seat, on the plank, half-standing, squatting beside it) in 6 of 15 old readings (S 1/5, O 5/10; 4 of 10 readers), in 0 of 10 B readings and in 1 of 10 C readings (one reader, who adds "it looks acceptable"), and any remark at all on his seat or legs stands in old 10/15, B 3/10 (two readers: knees over the beam but "reads as sitting", bent knees as "pushing off"), C 1/10, nearly always in the unsure section. Hardly any reader in any arm writes "on the seat" in so many words (old 3/15, B 2/10, C 1/10, each time with a qualifier or a doubt beside it); the usual wording is "sits on the low end", so being placed on the seat shows only as no doubt raised (old 5/15, B 7/10, C 9/10), and B and C, which share the man, differ by two readings, which one reader with two pictures can make. The on-purpose reading goes the same way: old 7/15 (6 of 10 readers, always hedged and in the unsure section, five times in the same line as a remark on his seat or legs), B 2/10 (one reader), C 0/10. Stuck does not clearly differ: stuck or kept up in any form old 7/15, B 6/10, C 4/10 (readers 5/10, 3/5, 3/5), flat in the description old 4/15, B 4/10, C 3/10, where the flat word is "stuck" itself in old 4, B 2 (one reader) and C 1, the others being "kept up", "held up" or "keeps her raised". No reader in any arm says she wants down, tries to climb down or reaches for the ground, and none names B's lean back and bent head or C's bend forward; what every B and C reading does note is her feet or legs dangling or off the ground (old 3/15). C's girl is read as off her seat and slipping or sliding off in 4 of 10 readings (two readers, both pictures each) and her leg is remarked on in 3, readings absent from old and B, whose seat remarks are "perched" once each (O, B) and "slightly floaty" once (B). Weight as the cause stands in old 12/15, B 7/10, C 5/10 (flat in the description 7/15, 7/10, 5/10), weight together with stuck in old 6/15, B 4/10, C 3/10, and no word of either in old 2/15, B 1/10, C 4/10; ordinary play, a bounce or a passing moment is offered as a reading in B 5/10 (three readers) against old 1/15 and C 0/10, and B has no word on her face (worried or alarmed: old 6/15, C 3/10, B 0/10). With ten readings from five readers in each new arm and five unnumbered readings in S, gaps of two or three readings on stuck and weight can be one reader's habit; the gaps wider than that are seat doubt and on purpose between old and the two new arms together (6/15 against 1/20, 7/15 against 2/20), the dangling-legs note (3/15 against 20/20) and C's slipping reading (4/10 against 0/25), which rests on two readers.
