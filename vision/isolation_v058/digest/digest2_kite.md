## Closing paragraph

Every one of the 48 readings says in its free description that the kite is stuck or caught in the tree, and classes 2, 4 and 5 are empty in both arms. Stuck with nothing beside it stands in 17 of 24 readings in B and 21 of 24 in C; by reader that is 6 of 8 and 8 of 8 with at least one such reading, and 5 of 8 and 6 of 8 with all three. The gap rests on few readers: B's seven "stuck, or flying in front of the tree" readings come from three readers (two of them wrote the same doubt on all three of their pictures) and C's three from two readers, always in the unsure section only, settled toward stuck in six of B's seven and one of C's three; B would lose three more plain readings if "caught rather than flying", where flying is named only to rule it out, counted as a second reading. No class-1 reading in either arm says the child's pose or awareness does not fit a stuck kite, so 1a equals class 1 in both arms (17 and 21). What the child does is stated plainly in both arms: in B all 24 readings (8 of 8 readers) say the child holds the reel, spool or string, 23 of them in the free description, and 23 also have points or reaches in the description or inventory; in C all 24 free descriptions have points (18), reaches up (5) or "points/reaches" (1), and none has holds. The arms differ in what gathers in the unsure section: in C 22 of 24 readings remark that the spool lies on the ground and not in the hand, 11 (6 readers) raise dropped or let go as a possibility only, 6 (3 readers) say it may be someone else's, nobody's, just lying there or never held, and 11 say nothing of whose it is; in B nobody doubts whose kite it is (6 readings call it his or her kite, the other 18 tie it through the held reel or string), but 4 readings (2 readers) call the slack string odd for a kite that is held or tugged and 5 (3 readers) offer tugging or pulling as what the child may be doing, neither of which occurs in C. The raised arm is left open between pointing, reaching, waving or tugging in the unsure section in 10 B readings (5 readers) and 7 C readings (4 readers), and the choice of "points" or "reaches" follows the reader more than the arm. In T4_garden_s9111_iso no reading in either arm (4 and 4) mixes up the two children or the balloon and the kite; with eight readers an arm and three pictures a reader, a difference of four readings or of one reader cannot be told from reader habit.
