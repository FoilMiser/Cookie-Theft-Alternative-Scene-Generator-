# iso_11

## What I see
An older woman with gray hair in a bun and glasses stands on green grass in front of a wooden plank fence. She wears a teal long-sleeved top with a white collar, a gray skirt and brown shoes. She holds one arm straight out toward a round dirt flower bed and pours a curved stream of light-blue water onto the flowers. Small droplets fall near the soil. The bed holds many simple flowers on green stems in red, yellow, purple, pink and orange. The object in her hand seems to be for watering the flowers.

## Unsure or ambiguous
- The watering object does not clearly read as a watering can. It is a round gray disc on the end of a long, thin dark rod or handle, and no can body is visible. It could be read as (a) the sprinkler head of a watering can with a very long spout, or (b) a frying pan or ladle tipped to pour water. The water comes out of the edge of the disc rather than from a spout, which adds to the frying-pan reading.
- The long, straight dark rod between her hand and the disc looks stiff and unusually long, as if she were holding the disc at arm's length on a stick.
- I cannot see her other arm clearly. It seems to be hidden behind her body.
- At the far left edge there is a white vertical strip with a dark outline that is cut off by the frame. It could be the edge of a house wall or post, or it could be an artifact of the cut-out.
- The water stream passes in front of one of the purple or pink flowers on the left. It reads fine as falling water but slightly overlaps the petals.
- Her figure is tall and narrow, with long thin legs, but nothing looks detached.
