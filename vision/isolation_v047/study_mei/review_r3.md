# Review r3: figure critique (fig_01 to fig_08)

## fig_01
1. A woman in her 20s or 30s, black hair, yellow dress, standing in three-quarter view with her head turned to her left (image right).
2. Faults:
- Neck: a separate skin-coloured peg or ball tucked under the jaw. Its outline carries on round the top, so the head looks balanced on a post, not joined to the body.
- Shoulders: the top of the dress is a hard flat shelf with square padded corners sticking out past the arms. The figure reads as a box with arms hung below it.
- Near arm (image left): it starts below the sleeve corner, with a visible step between the sleeve and the arm. It looks plugged in, not growing from the shoulder. No elbow, no wrist. One even-width tube.
- Hands: rounded mitten lumps with one curl line. No thumb, no fingers, no wrist narrowing.
- Face: the far eye sits right on the head outline and is half cut off. The nose is a small circle stuck on the cheek edge. The mouth is a tiny red smear, off-centre and lopsided.
- Hair: the fringe is a solid block with a dead-straight lower edge. The ear sits on top of the hair mass, not tucked under it.
- Legs: straight tubes from hem to shoe. No knee, no calf, no ankle. They come out of the hem like table legs, with no sense of hips above.
- Shoes: flat red ovals with a separate black sole line under them. There is no foot shape. The leg just ends on top of the oval.
- Torso: a rectangle with a waist seam, no taper at the waist and no chest shape.

## fig_02
1. The same woman seen from behind, standing straight, arms at her sides.
2. Faults:
- Hair: a round helmet on top of a separate rectangular slab with a scalloped bottom edge. It looks like two pasted pieces, and the slab covers the neck and shoulders like a board.
- No neck at all. The hair slab sits directly on the dress.
- Shoulders: square padded corners with small notches at the outer corners of the sleeves. Very boxy.
- Arms: they hang from under the sleeves with a double outline at the sleeve edge. Tubes with no elbow.
- Hands: plain round balls with a line across the wrist. No fingers or thumbs, even from the back.
- Feet: tiny red buns with a sole line. The legs end with no ankle or heel. From behind, the heel and the back of the shoe should show.
- Legs: tubes with no calf curve, which matters from behind.

## fig_03
1. The same woman from the front, walking towards the viewer.
2. Faults:
- Skirt hem, bottom left: the near thigh is drawn over the dress. A skin-coloured bump bites into the yellow hem, so the leg looks pasted on top of the skirt.
- Neck: a peg with the chin line drawn across it. The head looks set on top.
- Shoulders: the dress top is a flat straight bar with square corners.
- Walking pose: both legs are rigid and straight, with no knee bend. The step is shown only by one foot being lower, so it reads as sliding, not walking.
- Shoes: the far foot (image right) is bigger than the near one, which is backwards. The near shoe is a red oval over a black disc, like a hoof.
- Smudges: a dark blotch on the upper arm (image left) and a dark streak down the right shin look like dirty marks, not shading.
- Hands: mitten lumps with a "C" curl. No thumb.
- Face: the eyes are very wide apart and the mouth is an open red box. The face looks startled even though she is just walking.

- Back arm (image left): the elbow is a soft rounded corner, not a joint. The forearm and the mitten hand dangle.

- Face: the far eye is clipped by the head outline again. The nose is on the edge.
- Neck: a peg.

## Top faults
1. **Legs pushed through or pasted over the skirt** (fig_03, fig_04, fig_05, fig_07). Thighs show as capped tube ends inside the dress, or skin patches bite into the hem. Instead: the skirt should be drawn on top of the legs, and its outline should stay unbroken. A bent leg shows only as a raised shape under the cloth, with the knee as the skirt's high point and the lower leg coming out cleanly below the hem. No leg end should ever show inside the dress.
2. **Bent limbs that break apart** (fig_04, fig_05, fig_07). The running back leg is a floating sausage, the kneeling shin is a separate piece, and the arms cross over knees in a muddle. Instead: build each bent limb as two clear segments, upper and lower, joined by a visible knee or elbow that keeps the same width. Pose them so they overlap as little as possible and the line from hip to foot or shoulder to hand can be followed by eye.
3. **Neck as a separate pasted peg or ball** (fig_01, fig_03 to fig_08; missing entirely in fig_02). Instead: draw a short neck slightly narrower than the jaw, its side lines running straight into the shoulder slope. No closed outline round its top, and the jawline should overlap it with a single chin line.
4. **Mitten hands with no thumb or wrist** (all eight). Instead: narrow the wrist, then draw a simple hand with a clear thumb split. For hanging hands, a rounded hand with the thumb shown in front. For raised or open hands, three or four short finger bumps plus a thumb on the correct side. For gripping hands (fig_07), fingers that wrap visibly round the handle.
5. **Boxy shoulder-pad shoulders with arms plugged in underneath** (fig_01, fig_02, fig_03, fig_06, fig_08). Instead: give the shoulder line a gentle slope from the neck down to a rounded deltoid. Draw the sleeve as a short cuff round the upper arm, with the arm's outline running continuously out of the shoulder. No square corners, notches or steps.
6. **Straight tube legs with no knee, calf or ankle** (all eight). Instead: keep the simple shape but give a slight inward curve at the knee, a soft calf swell, and a clear narrowing at the ankle before the shoe. Walking and running legs (fig_03, fig_04) need a visible knee bend.
7. **Arms too long, straight and propped on nothing** (fig_05, fig_07, and close to it in fig_01). Instead: the hanging fingertips should reach about mid-thigh on a standing figure. A supporting arm should bend slightly at the elbow, and its hand should sit flat on a ground line at hip level.
8. **Shoes as floating ovals with a detached sole line** (all eight). Instead: draw a foot-shaped shoe, with a rounded toe and a heel, wider at the toe and joined to the ankle. Make the sole a thin band attached to the shoe, not a separate dark line under it. Keep near feet larger than far feet (fig_03 is reversed).
9. **Three-quarter face construction: far eye clipped by the head outline, nose stuck on the cheek edge** (fig_01, fig_04, fig_05, fig_07). Instead: keep both eyes fully inside the face, with the far eye slightly narrower and set in from the edge. Put the nose just inside the face contour, overlapping it slightly, and centre the mouth under the nose. Open box mouths (fig_03, fig_06) should become a small simple smile or a closed line unless the action calls for speech.
10. **Stray marks, smudges and confused overlaps** (fig_03 shin and arm smudges, fig_04 hem tick, fig_05 skirt creases and outline gap, fig_07 dots and red speck, fig_08 arm across face). Instead: remove all loose dots, blotches and creases that do not follow cloth folds. Keep the outline closed and even. Pose arms so they never cover the face, with raised arms going up beside the head, not across it.
