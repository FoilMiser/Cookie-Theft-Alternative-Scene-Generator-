# Pair 11

## 1. Who and what
A young woman with long dark hair tied back, in a purple top and green trousers, kneeling on one knee and digging with a garden trowel held in both hands. In a she is seen in profile; in b her face is turned three-quarters toward us.

## 2. Faults in a
- Front foot: the foot is a flat dark oval lying separately under the trouser hem, so it looks like a disc or stone rather than a shoe on her foot.
- Face: a profile with one eye, the nose and the mouth on the face edge. The head is wide at the back and the face looks small and pushed to the front.
- Arms: both arms hang very straight and long from the shoulders down to the trowel, with no bend at the elbows.
- Hands: the two hands merge into one lump on the trowel handle.
- Trousers: the seat and thigh have a lumpy outline, with a bump at the hip.

## 3. Faults in b
- Arms: the same very long, straight arms with no elbow bend.
- Hands: the same merged lump of hands on the trowel handle.
- Trousers: the same lumpy seat and hip.
- Face: the face is turned toward the viewer while the body is in side view, so head and body look slightly twisted.

## 4. Differences
- a shows the face in profile with one eye; b shows a three-quarter face with two eyes.
- In a the front foot is a separate flat oval under the hem; in b it is a shoe shape joined to the end of the leg.
- The mouth is drawn differently (a: small, at the profile edge; b: small red mouth below the nose).
- The hair and ear placement shift a little with the head turn.
- The body, arms, trowel and back leg are the same.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: the head is large and wide and the neck is fine. The shoulders are fine, but the arms are long and straight. The hands are fused together. The hips are lumpy. The legs read as a kneel. The front foot looks detached from the leg.
- b: the head and neck are fine. The arms are the same long, straight ones, and the hands are fused the same way. The hips are equally lumpy. The legs are fine, and both feet are attached shoes.

## 6. Pose
- a: kneeling and leaning forward to dig reads clearly. The straight, rigid arms make it a little stiff, and the detached front foot weakens the kneel.
- b: the same pose and the same stiff arms, but the feet are planted properly, so the kneel reads more naturally.

## 7. Better drawn
b. The front foot is a proper shoe on the leg rather than a loose oval, and the face is clearer. Otherwise the two are the same.
