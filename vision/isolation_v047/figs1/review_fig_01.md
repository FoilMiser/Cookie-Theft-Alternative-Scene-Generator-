# Review: fig 01

## 1. Who and what
A man (a street cleaner or worker) in an orange cap, an orange high-visibility vest with white stripes over a grey shirt, blue trousers and dark shoes, sweeping with a long-handled broom. Both versions read the same.

## 2. Faults in a
- Body: he stands bolt upright with straight legs while sweeping. There is no lean or bend toward the broom.
- Feet: the dark shoes are mostly hidden behind the broom head, and the near shoe is only a dark blob.
- Lower hand: it sits over the broom handle without clearly wrapping around it.
- Vest: at the left shoulder the vest outline juts out wider than the body, with a grey sleeve poking out under it, which looks bulky.
- Face: the mouth sits at the edge of the head outline, so the profile looks pinched.

## 3. Faults in b
- Body: the same bolt-upright, straight-legged stance while sweeping.
- Feet: the same, mostly hidden behind the broom head.
- Lower hand: the same unclear grip on the handle.
- Face: the mouth is again at the edge of the head.

## 4. Differences
- Vest at the left shoulder: wider and more jutting in a; slightly narrower and neater in b.
- Grey sleeve band on the near arm: it shows as a bigger patch in a and as a thinner band in b.
- Otherwise the cap, face, arms, legs and broom look the same. Apart from these small outline changes I saw no other differences.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: The head, cap and neck are fine. The shoulders are bulky on the left because of the vest outline. The arms are reasonable in length, and the hands are simple shapes. The hips and legs are long and straight. The feet are barely visible.
- b: The same as a, with a slightly tidier shoulder line. Otherwise the proportions are identical.

## 6. Pose
- a: Stiff. The arms hold the broom diagonally, but the body is straight and upright with the legs together, so it reads more like posing with a broom than actively sweeping.
- b: Equally stiff, for the same reasons.

## 7. Better drawn
b, by a very small margin: its shoulder and vest outline is slightly neater. The two are otherwise essentially the same, sharing the same stiff upright pose.
