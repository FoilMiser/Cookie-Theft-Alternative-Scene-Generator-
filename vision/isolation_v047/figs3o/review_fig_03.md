# Review: fig 03

## Who and what
A small red bird (robin-like, white belly, yellow beak) bending forward as if pecking at the ground. In a it is tipped steeply nose-down; in b it is nearly level with only the head dipped, so b reads more as standing and looking down than pecking.

## Faults in a
- Leg: the one visible leg comes out at the very back of the body, under the tail end, so the bird looks as if it would topple forward onto its face.
- Leg/foot: the leg is short and partly tucked behind the body outline; the foot is a small stub.
- Only one leg is visible.
- Wing: the dark wing oval is very large and covers most of the body, reading almost like a shell or saddle.
- Back: a darker band above the wing (far wing) peeks out as a strip along the back and looks like a separate stuck-on shape.
- Tail: a flat rectangular slab with a square end, sticking straight out, looks stiff.

## Faults in b
- Only one leg is visible; it is thin and set under the rear half of the belly.
- Tail: the same flat, square-ended rectangular slab, stiff.
- Wing: large flat oval, again reads as pasted on rather than folded against the body.
- Back: a narrow dark strip above the wing (far wing) looks like a stray band.
- Eye: grey rather than black, which looks slightly dull/unfinished compared with the outline.

## Differences
- Body angle: a is tilted steeply forward (head low, tail high); b is almost horizontal.
- Head: in a the head is low and in front of the belly; in b it is level with the back and only slightly bent.
- Leg position: in a the leg is at the rear end of the body; in b it is under the belly, nearer the middle.
- Leg length: the leg in b is longer and more visible than in a.
- Eye: a has a larger black eye; b has a smaller grey eye.
- Beak: in a it points down and right more steeply; in b it points down-right at a shallower angle.
- Wing oval: in a it is tilted and fills more of the body; in b it is flatter and more horizontal.
- Overall shape: a is a compact, rounder silhouette; b is longer and flatter.

## Anatomy
- a: head and body join smoothly, but the leg is attached too far back for the body's tilt and is short; one leg only; tail a rigid slab; wing oversized.
- b: head, body and wing in reasonable proportion; leg thin but placed plausibly under the body; tail a rigid slab; only one leg shown.

## Pose
- a: reads clearly as pecking (head down), but awkward because the leg is under the tail so the bird looks off balance and about to tip over.
- b: balanced and natural as a bird standing, but the head is only slightly lowered, so it reads weakly as pecking or eating.

## Better drawn
b, because the body sits balanced over its leg and the proportions look right; a conveys pecking more strongly but the rear-placed leg makes it look as if it is falling over.
