# Cast review, page p1 (v0.47), figures 01 to 09

## fig_01
**First read:** a young adult woman with long dark hair, in a purple short-sleeved T-shirt, teal trousers and black shoes. She stands facing front with her arms hanging down.

Faults:
- **Shoulders:** the top of the shirt is one flat horizontal line with square corners, like a coat hanger. The shoulders should slope down from the base of the neck to a rounded shoulder point.
- **Sleeves:** the sleeves are separate patches stuck on the corners of the shirt box. Draw them coming out of the shoulder, with one continuous outline running from the neckline over the shoulder.
- **Arms:** each arm is a straight tube that starts outside the shirt box and hangs with a gap from the body, with no elbow or wrist. Join the upper arm to the shoulder under the sleeve, narrow it a little at the elbow (level with the waist) and again at the wrist, and let it hang close to the side.
- **Hands:** each hand is a round mitten with one curl line and no thumb. Draw a relaxed hand with the thumb shown and the fingers hinted, and make it a little longer than wide, reaching mid-thigh.
- **Torso:** the shirt is a rectangle, with no bust, no waist and no hip. An adult woman needs a narrower waist and a slight curve out to the hip.
- **Hips:** the trousers step outwards in a notch just below the shirt hem, which looks like a pasted-on hip. Run one smooth outline from the waist over the hip.
- **Neck:** the neck is a thick plain cylinder that meets the shirt with no neckline. Add a round or scoop neckline and taper the neck slightly into the trapezius.
- **Hair:** the long hair stops abruptly at the shoulder line, as if cut by the shirt. Let it fall in front of or behind the shoulders, with a soft ragged end.
- **Face:** the mouth is a small open grimace with the teeth showing, which reads as distress. Use a neutral closed mouth or a slight smile.
- **Legs and trousers:** the legs are two straight tubes with no knees, and the trouser bottoms balloon into cuffs. Narrow them slightly at the knee and let the trousers fall straight to the shoe.
- **Feet and shoes:** the shoes are flat dark blobs with no sole, heel or toe direction. Draw shoes with a toe, a sole line and an ankle opening, turned slightly outwards.

## fig_02
**First read:** an adult man with short brown hair and a moustache, in a red T-shirt, blue trousers and dark shoes. He stands turned three-quarters to the viewer's right, looking right.

Faults:
- **Face:** the eyes are pushed to the right edge of the head, and the far eye is cut by the head outline. Move the features in from the edge so the far eye sits fully inside the outline, slightly narrower than the near one.
- **Nose:** the moustache sits where the nose should be and there is no nose. Draw a nose above the moustache, breaking the profile edge in three-quarter view.
- **Hair:** the hair is a flat, cap-like slab with a square step at the sideburn. Give it a natural hairline and a softer, layered edge.
- **Neck:** the neck is a narrow peg with a ring outline, too thin for an adult man. Make it wider and let it flow into the shoulders.
- **Shoulders and torso:** the shirt is a tilted slab. The near shoulder bulges outside the arm and the far shoulder ends in a sharp corner. Round both shoulders and give the chest and back some shape.
- **Near arm:** a straight tube with no elbow, hanging from under the sleeve edge, which is drawn as an inside curve. Draw the sleeve wrapping the arm and add an elbow bend.
- **Far arm:** drawn wholly outside the torso on the right, thin and starting halfway down the shirt, so it looks pasted on. Start it at the far shoulder, partly hidden behind the body.
- **Hands:** mitten blobs with no thumb. Draw relaxed hands with a thumb.
- **Trousers:** a darker smudge sits at the crotch, like a stray mark. Remove it, or draw a proper fly or fold line.
- **Hips and waist:** the waistband is a straight horizontal line facing front while the body is turned. Angle the waistband to match the three-quarter turn.
- **Legs:** tubes with no knees, which balloon at the bottom. Add knees and let the trousers break on the shoe.
- **Shoes:** flat dark ovals. Draw shoes with a toe and a sole.

## fig_03
**First read:** a girl with dark, tightly curled or braided hair pinned up, in a yellow T-shirt, orange shorts and blue flat shoes. She walks to the right, seen in profile.

Faults:
- **Hair:** the curls are drawn as heavy lumpy blocks like a cobbled helmet. Draw smaller, lighter curl shapes or clear braids that follow the skull.
- **Ear:** the ear sits too far back, at the edge of the hair. In profile it belongs just behind the middle of the head, level with the eye and nose.
- **Face:** one eye, a tiny nose circle on the edge and a tiny mouth. The profile edge needs a nose bump, lips and a chin.
- **Neck:** a thin stalk under a very large head. Make it a little wider and set it slightly forward of the back of the head.
- **Near arm:** the upper arm starts in the middle of the chest, with a seam line across it like a cut. Start it at the shoulder, with the sleeve over the top of the arm.
- **Far arm:** it comes out of the front of the shirt at hem level, so it looks stuck on. Start it at the far shoulder, partly hidden by the chest.
- **Arm swing:** it is unclear which arm goes with which leg. In a walk, the arm opposite the forward leg swings forward. Make that clear.
- **Shorts:** an oversized baggy box. A stray diagonal line runs across them from the hip to the knee. Remove the line, or turn it into a proper leg opening, and fit the shorts closer to the thighs.
- **Legs:** no knees. The rear leg bends only at the ankle, in a sharp corner. Bend the rear leg at the knee and lift its heel, with the foot pushing off on the toes.
- **Feet and shoes:** the rear shoe points backwards, to the left. Both feet must point in the direction of travel (right). The shoes are flat paddles with no ankle. Add an ankle and a shoe shape with a toe.
- **Hands:** mitten blobs. Draw hands with a thumb.

## fig_04
**First read:** an elderly woman with grey hair in a bun and round glasses, in a teal cardigan with a white front band and buttons, a blue-grey skirt and brown shoes, with bare legs. She sits with one hand on her lap and the other held out.

Faults:
- **Glasses:** one lens is fully drawn, but the second is squashed against the head outline, and the frame bar is thick and heavy. In three-quarter view, draw both lenses inside the face, with the far one narrower, and use a thin bridge and arms.
- **Ear and hair:** a small stray mark sits at the ear and the hair edge. Clean it up and draw a clear ear below the hair.
- **Neck:** a thick plain cylinder. Taper it slightly and add a collar or neckline on the cardigan.
- **Cardigan front:** the white band is off-centre to the right, and the buttons sit on its outer edge, almost on the outline. Centre the band on the body's front line, with the buttons along it.
- **Near arm:** it runs down the side and bends across to the lap with no elbow, and its outline merges with the torso. Draw the elbow at waist level and keep a separate outline for the arm.
- **Hand on lap:** lumpy and pasted on. Draw the hand lying on the thigh, with the fingers draped over it.
- **Far arm:** a long straight diagonal with the hand floating in the air, touching nothing. Rest it on the lap or an armrest, or give it a clear gesture.
- **Skirt and lap:** the lap is a sack shape whose underside curves like a bowl. A seated lap is a flat top surface with the skirt falling over the knees.
- **Knees:** the knees are round shapes sitting on top of the skirt, with a light gap or break in the outline where they meet it. Either the skirt covers the knees, or the knees show below the hem with a clean outline.
- **Legs:** the shins are long straight tubes with no calf shape, too long for the body. Shorten them so knee to floor roughly equals the thigh length, and add a calf curve.
- **Feet and shoes:** brown ovals with no ankle. Draw sensible low shoes with a toe and a heel.

## Top faults

1. **Arms are stiff tubes with no shoulder joint, elbow or wrist** (all 9; shared). The arms start outside the torso box or under a stuck-on sleeve, and often hang with a gap from the body. Instead, start the arm at a rounded shoulder, bend it slightly at the elbow (level with the waist), narrow it at the wrist, and keep it close to the side.
2. **Hands are mitten blobs with one curl and no thumb** (all 9; shared). Instead, draw relaxed hands that are longer than wide, with a clear thumb and the fingers hinted, reaching mid-thigh.
3. **Square coat-hanger shoulders and box torsos with no chest, waist or hip shape** (1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 9 most strongly, and the rest too; shared). Sleeves are pasted on the corners, and men, women and children all have the same build. Instead, slope the shoulders from the neck, and give each body type its own shape: a waist and hip for the women, broader chests for the men, a softer build for the children.
4. **Legs are straight tubes with no knees, and the trouser bottoms balloon** (all 9; shared). Instead, narrow the leg at the knee and add a calf curve. Bend the knee in walking and seated poses. Let the trousers fall straight and break on the shoe.
5. **Shoes are flat ovals or paddles with no toe, sole, heel or ankle** (all 9; shared). In the walking profiles, the rear foot points backwards (3, 6). Instead, draw shoes with a toe, a sole line and an ankle opening. In a walk, both feet point the way of travel, with the rear heel lifted.
6. **In three-quarter and profile views, the face is pushed to the edge of the head** (2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9; most). The far eye or lens is clipped by the outline, and the nose is missing or a dot on the edge. Instead, keep both eyes (and both lenses) inside the outline, with the far one narrower. Give profiles a nose, lips and chin, and place the ear just behind the middle of the head (it is set too far back in 3 and 6).
7. **The far arm in turned and walking poses is pasted to the front or side of the torso** (2, 3, 6, 9; most of the turned figures). Instead, start it at the far shoulder, partly hidden by the chest, and make the arm swing clearly opposite the forward leg.
8. **The neck is a thin peg, a plain cylinder or missing, with no neckline or collar** (all 9; shared; missing in 5 and 7). Instead, draw a neck of suitable width that tapers into the shoulders, with a neckline, collar or hood opening.
9. **Hats sit like lids, with peaks drawn as thin flat ledges** (5, 7, 9; shared by all the capped figures). Instead, fit the crown round the skull and draw a short curved peak that points where the face points. Give the flat cap (5) a short front peak.
10. **Single-figure defects that read as errors at once:**
    - fig 9: a stray blue-grey patch on one shoulder. Remove it or draw sleeves on both arms.
    - figs 2 and 9: a crotch smudge. Remove it.
    - fig 4: knees pasted on top of the skirt with a broken outline, a floating outstretched hand, and an off-centre cardigan band. Cover the knees with the skirt or draw them cleanly below the hem, rest the hand on the lap, and centre the band.
    - fig 5: no neck, and a white plank instead of a waistcoat opening. Add a neck and collar, and draw a V-neck opening.
    - fig 7: the pocket sits high on the chest and the drawstrings float. Draw a kangaroo pocket on the belly, with the drawstrings starting at the hood.
    - fig 6: the beard reads as a jaw shadow. Give it volume.
    - fig 8: the hair buns read as ears. Move them higher.
    - fig 3: a stray diagonal line across the shorts. Remove it.
