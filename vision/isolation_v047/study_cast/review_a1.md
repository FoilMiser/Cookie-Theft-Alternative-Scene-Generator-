# Cast review a1 (eight figures)

## fig_01

First read: a brown beagle-type dog with a red collar, standing in side view facing right, tail up.

- Legs are plain tubes with no joints. The front legs are straight posts with no elbow, wrist or pastern. The hind legs bend only in a soft S, with no stifle or hock angle. Draw it instead with the elbow tucked against the chest and a slight forward break at the pastern, plus a clear backward hock angle on the hind leg.
- The legs are too long for the body, so the dog stands on stilts. Leg length (elbow to ground) should be about equal to the chest depth.
- Feet are flat oval blobs with no toes. They should be compact rounded paws with two or three toe notches and a clear front.
- There are seams and scratchy hatch lines where the legs meet the belly (the whole underside, especially between the near hind leg and the far front leg). A crease line runs from the rump down to the hind leg top, so the hindquarter looks bolted on. Draw one continuous belly line with the thigh flowing out of it, and remove the jagged strokes.
- The far legs are a darker brown and stop in a hard horizontal cut under the body, so they read as separate stockings. Use a slightly darker tone of the same colour and tuck the tops behind the body outline, with no cut line.
- A black crescent sits on the front edge of the head above the nose, like a stray mark or a misplaced far eye. Remove it.
- The head is a ball with a white patch for a muzzle. There is no stop and no projecting muzzle, the nose sits on the rim of the circle, and there is no mouth line. Give the head a short, squarish muzzle that projects forward, a step at the brow, the nose at the muzzle tip and a short mouth line.
- There is no neck: the head sits straight on the collar. Show a short thick neck rising up and forward from the shoulders.
- The ear hangs from the very back of the skull. It should hang from the top side of the head, just behind the eye.
- The white chest patch below the collar reads as a tag or bib pasted on. Blend it into the chest as a soft-edged blaze, or drop it.
- The tail is a long straight vertical stick about as long as a leg. Draw a tapering tail that curves slightly forward, about two-thirds that length.
- The body is a flat-backed tube with no chest depth and no tuck-up. Give it a deeper chest behind the front legs and a rise in the belly line towards the hind legs.

## fig_02

First read: the same brown dog, probably sitting or rearing up on its haunches facing right. The pose is unclear.

- The sitting pose does not read. The hind legs are straight standing tubes and the body slopes up in a straight wedge like a sea lion. A sitting dog needs a big folded thigh (an oval haunch on the ground), the hock lying flat along the ground, and the front legs straight and vertical under the chest.
- The tail and hindquarters are one shape. The rear tapers into a point at lower left with no tail root. Draw a separate tail coming from the base of the spine, lying on the ground or curled round the feet.
- The near front leg is drawn as an inner line running up the chest, with a small stray tick at its top. Close the leg into its own shape, joined at the elbow.
- The two front feet at the bottom centre overlap in a muddle of double outlines. Separate them, or overlap them cleanly with one outline in front.
- Feet are blobs with no toes, and the legs have no joints, as in fig_01.
- The same black crescent appears on the front edge of the head.
- The same ball head has no muzzle projection, stop or mouth.
- There is no neck. The head sits on the collar, and the collar sits on a narrow point at the top of the wedge-shaped body. A sitting dog's neck rises from broad shoulders.
- The white tag under the collar looks pasted on.
- The far front leg (the darker one on the right) has no top. It just appears out of the chest outline. Tuck it behind the near leg with a visible shoulder.

## fig_03

First read: a white dog with brown spots and a blue collar, walking to the right, tail up.

- The far legs are a mid grey, so they read as grey socks or metal legs. Use a pale cool tint of the white, only slightly darker than the near legs.
- There is a ragged brown smudge with white drips inside it at the top of the near front leg. It is a paint artefact. Remove it, or draw a clean-edged spot.
- The rump spot bulges outside the body outline as a separate disc, with a second spot butted against it. Spots should sit inside the outline and follow the body curve, never changing the silhouette.
- The spot on the back pushes up above the back line as a lump. Keep it inside the outline.
- The near hind leg bends forward at the knee like a human leg, and its foot points backwards. The hind leg should angle back at the hock, with the paw pointing forward.
- The legs have no joints and the feet are blobs, as in fig_01. The grey far front foot is a flat disc.
- The head again has a black crescent on the front edge and a small dash beside the nose. Remove both.
- A brown tab hangs under the chin, reading as a stray mark or a hanging tongue. Either draw a clear open mouth with a tongue, or remove it.
- The muzzle does not project. The nose sits on the edge of the round head. Add a short muzzle.
- A short vertical stroke on the flank behind the shoulder is a stray line. Remove it.
- The tail is a straight stick, too long and stiff. Draw a shorter curved, tapering tail.
- There is a seam at the top of the near front leg where it meets the belly.

## fig_04

First read: a grey-blue cat with a white chest, standing and facing right, tail up. The muzzle reads as if the cat has a brush or a mouse in its mouth.

- The whiskers and muzzle are a black and white bristly block sticking far out past the face. This is the biggest recognition problem in the picture. Draw a small white muzzle pad inside the head, a small pink nose, a mouth line, and three thin whisker lines on each side that do not touch the head outline.
- A black crescent on the front edge of the head reads as a stray mark. Remove it.
- The far ear is a thin black spike, and both ears sit at the back of the head. Draw two triangular ears with pink insides on top of the head, the far one partly hidden.
- The legs are long straight posts, so the cat is on stilts and reads as a dog. Cat legs should be shorter, with a visible elbow, a bent wrist, and a hind leg with a long hock angle.
- The cat uses the same body, leg and feet template as the dogs. Give the cat a longer, lower, more flexible body, a smaller head that sits lower, and smaller neat round paws.
- Feet are oval blobs with no toes.
- The white chest patch is a large circle pasted over the chest and the tops of the legs. It should be a bib that starts under the chin and narrows down the chest.
- A hip crease line runs across the rump.
- There is a notch with a small white gap in the outline at the top of the near front leg. The outline is broken there. Close it.
- The tail is a straight vertical stick. Draw a cat tail as a thin, even-width tube with a slight S curve and a rounded tip.
- The body is a rectangle with no curve. Give it a gentle dip behind the shoulders and a rounded rump.

## fig_05

First read: an orange cat, apparently sitting upright facing right. The figure could also read as a seal or a small dinosaur.

- There is no eye on the head. Add one eye in the upper front third of the head.
- The head is a separate circle balanced on a narrow neck stalk, so it looks pasted on. The head should overlap the shoulders, with the neck as wide as the head's lower half.
- The face is a black crescent plus a black bristle block plus a white patch squeezed onto the far right edge of the circle. Draw a small muzzle inside the head with a nose, mouth and thin whiskers.
- Only one ear shows, as a thin sliver at the back. Draw two triangular ears on top.
- The sitting pose does not read. There is no folded haunch, the hind legs stand straight, and the body is a straight wedge. Draw a big rounded haunch on the ground, with the front legs straight down under the chest.
- The tail and back form one long tapering wedge, so the tail has no root. Draw the tail as a separate thin tube from the base of the spine, curled round the feet or lying along the ground.
- There is a small curled hook shape on the chest under the white patch, like a stray claw. Remove it, or make it a clear raised paw.
- The white chest patch has ragged cloud edges and runs over the outline. Give it a clean edge inside the outline.
- There are stray marks on the flank: a small dot, a short curved stroke and faint ticks near the hind leg. Remove them.
- The feet at the bottom overlap in double outlines. The far front foot has an inner duplicate outline. Clean them up to one outline per foot.
- The front leg is one long column from chest to foot, with no elbow or wrist.

## fig_06

First read: a white goat with dark horns and a red collar, standing and facing right.

- Legs are thick straight tubes with no knees or hocks. Goat legs should be thin, with knobbly knees on the front legs and a sharp hock on the hind legs.
- The hooves are dark blue round balls, which read as shoes or wheels. Draw small wedge-shaped cloven hooves with a split at the front, wider at the ground.
- The far legs are grey, so they read as a different colour. Use a pale tint of the white.
- The beard is a small grey tab stuck under the jaw, so it looks pasted on. Draw a pointed tuft hanging from the chin, the same colour as the head or slightly darker, with no hard box shape.
- The outline breaks where the head meets the neck at the back of the jaw, with small stray dots. Close it with a single line.
- The ear sticks out horizontally from the back of the skull like a leaf. That is acceptable for a goat, but it should come from just below the horn base, angled slightly down.
- The body is a box with a flat back and a hip crease line across the rump. Draw a slight hollow behind the withers, a bony hip point and a sloped rump down to the tail.
- There is a seam at the top of the near front leg.
- The tail is an upright square tab. Draw a short pointed tail flicked up.
- The neck is short and straight. Give it a slight forward slope.

## fig_07

First read: a red bird with a white belly, perhaps a robin, standing and facing right.

- Two eyes are drawn side by side on a head in side view, one dark grey and one black. This reads as a front-view face on a side-view body. In profile, draw one eye only, in front of the head centre.
- A black lump sits at the top back of the head (the same stray crescent artefact as on the mammals). Remove it.
- The head is a full circle with its outline drawn across the neck, so it looks stuck onto the body. Merge the head into the body with no line across the throat.
- The beak is two small yellow plates stacked on the head outline. Draw one short pointed cone-shaped beak with a thin split line, growing out of the face.
- The feet are tiny flat pads with no toes. Draw three thin toes forward and one back, gripping the ground.
- The legs are orange sticks with thick outlines, so they look heavy. Draw thinner legs with a backward-bent ankle joint.
- The wing is a flat dark oval stuck high on the back, with no feather tips. Draw a teardrop wing along the side with two or three feather points at the rear end.
- The tail is a straight rectangular paddle with no feather ends. Draw a tapered fan with a notched or pointed tip.
- The species is unclear. A red body with a white belly matches neither a robin (orange breast, brown back) nor a cardinal (crest, black face). Pick one and give it the key marks: for a robin, a brown back and an orange breast; for a cardinal, a crest and a black mask round the beak.

## fig_08

First read: a white duck standing in side view facing right.

- The head, neck and body are three stacked pieces, with hard lines across the neck at the top and bottom, like a snowman. Draw the neck as one smooth S curve flowing from the head into the breast, with no crossing lines.
- The wing is a cream oval stuck at the rear of the body and poking out past the outline at the left. Draw it along the side of the body, inside the outline, with the tips pointing back and up towards the tail.
- The tail is a thin white stick poking up and back. Draw a short upturned pointed tuft at the end of the body.
- The body is a round ball. A duck body is boat-shaped: full breast forward, flatter underside, rising to the tail.
- The bill is two stacked plates sitting on the head outline. Draw one broad flat bill growing from the face, with a small nostril dot and a slight hook at the tip.
- The feet are flat orange ovals with no webbing or toes, and they overlap into one shape. Draw two webbed feet with three toe lines and a scalloped front edge, separated so both read.
- The legs are short orange tubes set too far forward under the belly centre. Set them slightly back of centre.
- The top of the head looks cut flat where it touches the top edge of the picture, and the back of the head outline wobbles. Draw a full round crown and a smooth outline.

## Top faults

1. **Legs are straight tubes with no joints (shared: figs 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6).** There are no elbows, wrists, stifles or hocks, and the legs are too long, so the animals stand on stilts. Draw the elbow against the chest, a slight pastern break and a clear backward hock angle on the hind legs. Leg length should be about equal to chest depth (shorter for the cat). The goat needs thin legs with knobbly knees.
2. **Feet are round blobs (shared: all eight).** The dogs and cats have no toes, the goat has dark balls instead of hooves, the robin has flat pads and the duck has flat ovals. Draw paws with toe notches, cloven wedge-shaped hooves for the goat, three toes forward and one back for the robin, and webbed scalloped feet for the duck.
3. **A stray black crescent or lump sits on the edge of the head (shared: figs 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7).** It reads as a misplaced eye or a dirty mark. Remove it everywhere.
4. **The muzzle and face don't work.** The cats' whiskers are a black bristle block sticking out past the face, reading as a brush or mouse in the mouth (figs 4, 5). The dogs have ball heads with no projecting muzzle, no stop and no mouth (figs 1, 2, 3). Draw cats with a small muzzle pad, a pink nose and thin separate whisker lines. Draw dogs with a short square muzzle, a brow step and a mouth line.
5. **Eyes are missing or wrong.** Fig 5 has no eye at all. Fig 7 shows two eyes of different shades on a side-view head. Draw one eye per profile head, in the upper front part of the head.
6. **Seams, crease lines and outline breaks where the parts join (shared: figs 1, 3, 4, 5, 6).** These include scratchy hatch lines along the belly, hip crease lines, cut lines across the tops of the legs, a notch with a gap (fig 4), a break at the jaw (fig 6), and stray flank strokes and dots (figs 3, 5). Draw one continuous outline per animal, with the limbs flowing out of the body, and delete the stray marks.
7. **Sitting pose unreadable (figs 2, 5).** The hind legs stand straight and the body is a straight wedge, so the figures read as a sea lion or dinosaur. Draw a folded oval haunch on the ground, the hock flat, the front legs vertical, and the tail as a separate shape lying on the ground. Do not merge the tail with the haunch.
8. **Parts look pasted on (shared: figs 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8).** Head circles sit on the body with a line across the neck (figs 5, 7, 8; the duck is head, neck and body stacked like a snowman). Chest patches are circles or tags (figs 1, 2, 4, 5). The goat's beard is a tab (fig 6), the duck's wing sticks out past the body (fig 8), and the cat's head is on a stalk (fig 5). Merge the head and neck into the body with no crossing line. Make the bibs start under the chin, the wings sit inside the outline, and the beards hang as pointed tufts.
9. **Far legs are in a different colour, not a shade (shared: figs 3, 6; milder in 1, 4).** Grey far legs on white animals read as socks or metal. Draw far legs as a slightly darker tint of the same coat colour, with their tops tucked behind the body.
10. **Tails are stiff sticks or paddles (shared: figs 1, 3, 4, 7, 8).** The dog and cat tails are long straight vertical sticks, the robin's is a rectangular paddle and the duck's is a thin stick. Draw a shorter tapering curved tail for the dogs, an even-width S-curved tail for the cat, a feathered fan for the robin and a short upturned tuft for the duck.

Also: the spots on fig 3 bulge outside the outline and there is a dripping smudge on the near front leg; overlapping feet have double outlines (figs 2, 5, 8); and the cat uses the dog body template (fig 4).
