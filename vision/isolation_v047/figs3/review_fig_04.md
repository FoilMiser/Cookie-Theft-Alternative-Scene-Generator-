# Review: fig_04

## 1. Who and what
A grey-blue cat seen side-on, facing right, moving forward. In a it reads as crouching or creeping low with its legs bunched under it. In b it reads as walking or trotting in a long stride.

## 2. Faults in a
- Legs: all four are bunched under the body in a tangle, and it is hard to tell which leg is which or which side each is on.
- Front leg (middle of the belly): the upper part runs almost flat along the belly to a sharp elbow, like a folded arm rather than a cat's foreleg.
- Paws: big, blunt, boot-like blobs, especially the two hind feet, which overlap into one lump.
- Hind thigh: lumpy outline with a bump at the back of the thigh and a stray inner line across it.
- Shoulder: a small round knob sticks out where the front leg meets the body, like a separate ball joint.
- Head: a near-perfect circle stuck onto the front of the body with almost no neck, so it looks attached rather than grown on.
- Tail: a straight, stiff, even-width stick sticking out backwards.
- Muzzle: a small white patch on the front of the circle with whiskers poking out. It is tiny and reads more like a beak or a tag than a snout.

## 3. Faults in b
- Legs: long, thin and tube-like, like stilts, with an even width from top to bottom and little sign of a cat's bent hind leg.
- Paws: blunt, rounded boot shapes at the ends of the legs.
- Shoulder: the same round knob where the near front leg joins the body.
- Far front leg: a faint line where it joins the chest makes it look slotted in rather than grown out.
- Head: the same circle stuck on with almost no neck.
- Tail: the same straight, stiff stick.
- Muzzle: the same tiny white patch with whiskers.

## 4. Differences
- Leg pose: in a the legs are folded and bunched close under the body; in b they are spread out in a wide walking stride, with four separate feet on the ground.
- In b the legs look longer and straighter; in a they are shorter and more bent.
- In b the four paws are clearly spaced apart; in a the hind paws overlap and the front paws are close together.
- In a the body sits lower over the legs; in b there is more open space under the belly.
- The head, ear, eye, muzzle, whiskers, body outline, tail and shoulder knob look the same in both.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: The head is a ball with almost no neck. The body is long and smooth. The legs are short, folded and tangled, with an oddly flat front elbow and a lumpy hind thigh. The paws are too big and blob-like. The shoulder knob looks detached. The tail is stiff and evenly thick.
- b: The head is a ball with almost no neck. The body is long and smooth. The legs are clearly separated but too long, thin and straight, like tubes. The paws are blunt. There is the same shoulder knob, and the far foreleg join is visible. The tail is stiff.

## 6. Pose
- a: Somewhat readable as a low creep or crouch, but the folded legs are confusing, so it can also look as if the cat is collapsing or tripping. Awkward.
- b: Reads as a natural walking stride; the leg spread is clear. It is a little stiff because the legs are straight tubes and the tail is rigid.

## 7. Better drawn
b. Its four legs are clearly separate and give a readable walking stride, while a's legs are bunched into a confusing tangle with blob paws.
