# Review: fig 03

## 1. Who and what
A red bird with a white belly and yellow beak, leaning forward with its head low, as if looking at or pecking the ground. In a it stands on a single leg; in b it is tipped further forward with two legs bent, so it reads more as crouching, hopping or running than standing.

## 2. Faults in a
- Tail: a flat, straight-edged rectangular slab sticking straight back; looks stiff and plank-like rather than feathered.
- Tail join: the tail comes out of the upper back above the wing, not from the rear of the body, so it looks stuck on.
- Legs: only one leg is shown; it reads as a bird standing on one leg rather than a normal stance.
- Leg: very thin and placed under the rear of the belly, so the large body looks barely supported and front-heavy.
- Foot: a small square L-shaped hook, not a bird's foot with toes.
- Wing: a very large flat oval covering most of the body; reads more like a lid or patch than a folded wing.

## 3. Faults in b
- Tail: same flat rectangular slab joined high on the back, stiff and stuck-on.
- Back leg: comes out from under the rear of the body at a sharp angle and sticks out behind; looks like a stick poking out rather than a bird leg.
- Front leg: very short and squashed under the belly, mostly hidden; its foot is a small orange smudge.
- Feet: square L-shaped hooks with no toes.
- Wing: a small stray scribble or nick in the outline near the top-right edge of the wing.
- Head: tipped so low that the beak points down-right below body level; the head looks slightly sunk into the body.
- Wing: same very large flat oval covering most of the body.

## 4. Differences
- a stands on one leg; b shows two legs, one angled backward and one tucked under the belly.
- b's whole body is rotated further forward (more tilt) than a's.
- b's head is lower relative to the body and the beak points more downward.
- b's eye is slightly darker and sits lower on the head, closer to the beak.
- a's beak shows upper and lower halves more clearly; b's beak is a single more pointed shape.
- b's white belly patch is lower and flatter; a's is rounder and further forward.
- b has a small stray mark on the wing outline that a does not have.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: head round and well joined to the body; body oval reasonable; wing too large; tail too long, flat and joined too high on the back; only one leg, too thin for the body, with a toeless square foot.
- b: head round but set low and slightly sunk; body and wing as in a (wing too large); tail same plank shape joined too high; back leg too long and oddly angled out from the rear; front leg too short, nearly lost under the belly; feet toeless.

## 6. Pose
- a: reads as a bird leaning down to look at or peck the ground, which is natural, but standing on one thin leg makes it look a little unbalanced and stiff.
- b: reads as lunging or running forward rather than pecking; the mismatched legs (one long and splayed back, one squashed) make the pose awkward and hard to read.

## 7. Better drawn
a. Its single upright leg and level body read clearly as a bird bending to peck, while b's odd splayed back leg and crushed front leg make the pose confusing.
