# Review: fig 01

## 1. Who and what
A small round blue bird with a white belly and yellow beak, bending its head down as if looking at or pecking the ground. In a it is tipped steeply forward, seen side-on, with stubby legs nearly hidden; in b it stands upright on two long legs with its face turned toward the viewer, so it reads more as standing and looking down.

## 2. Faults in a
- Legs: very short orange stubs, mostly hidden under the belly; they read as blobs rather than legs and feet.
- Head: very large compared to the body and set low at the front, so the bird looks top-heavy and close to toppling forward.
- Body: tilted so steeply that the tail end points up-left and the whole figure looks like it is falling forward.
- Wing: a very large dark oval covering most of the back; looks like a patch stuck on.
- Tail: a flat, wedge-shaped slab rising from the upper back, stiff and plank-like.
- Beak: a rounded yellow blob pointing straight down at the bottom of the head; reads more like a tongue than a beak.

## 3. Faults in b
- Eyes: the two eyes do not match: one is a larger grey oval, the other a smaller black dot set lower and to the right, giving a lopsided, odd face.
- Legs: long, thin, straight sticks set close together; stiff and slightly too long for such a round bird.
- Feet: small square L-shaped hooks with no toes.
- Tail: the same flat wedge-shaped slab from the upper back, stiff.
- Wing: very large dark oval covering most of the body.
- Back: a small dark patch on top of the back between tail and head looks like an unexplained extra shape (a second wing seen edge-on).

## 4. Differences
- a's body is tipped steeply forward; b's body is much more level.
- a shows one eye (side view) with a white highlight; b shows two eyes facing the viewer, of different size and colour, with no highlight.
- a's legs are short stubs hidden under the belly; b's legs are long, straight and fully visible with feet.
- a's head is lower and larger relative to the body; b's head is higher and sits more beside the body.
- a's beak points straight down from the bottom of the head; b's beak points down-right from the side of the face.
- a's dark back patch (far wing) is larger and runs along the top of the back; b's is a small cap on top.
- a's white belly is smaller and pushed under the head; b's belly is larger and clearly visible.

## 5. Anatomy
- a: head too large and set too low; body round and fine; wing too large; tail flat and joined high on the back; legs far too short and almost detached-looking stubs; feet not readable.
- b: head a reasonable size but face lopsided because the eyes differ; body and belly fine; wing too large; tail flat and joined high; legs a little too long and thin, joined cleanly under the belly; feet toeless hooks.

## 6. Pose
- a: reads as bending down to peck, but the extreme forward tilt with almost no legs makes it look like it is tumbling over rather than pecking.
- b: reads as a bird standing and looking down at the ground, which is natural, though the straight parallel legs make it stiff, and the mismatched eyes distract from the action.

## 7. Better drawn
b. It has clear legs and a stable, readable standing pose, while a looks like it is falling forward on stubs; b's mismatched eyes are its main fault and should be fixed.
