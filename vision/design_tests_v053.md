# v0.53 design tests

The two changes of placement revision 49 were read blind in whole test scenes (`tools/cycle/design_tests.py`, the five jobs marked v0.53) by five fresh readers, one picture each, with the screen's prompt cut to one picture. The cast is v0.52's. The larger square wheel had its own bench in whole pictures first (`isolation_v053.md`); these tests read it among other events, and read the first rule for the jump.

| Test | Forced | Placed |

| 2 | A playground: a child jumping for joy, a game of catch, a child on the slide | first seed |
| 3 | A garden: a sprinkler soaking a package (`sprinkler_package`), the square-wheeled barrow, a game of catch (v0.51's first test, with the larger wheel) | second seed |
| 4 | A park: bread for the ducks (`ducks_bread`), the square-wheeled barrow, a kite flown | second seed |
| 5 | A garden: the square-wheeled barrow, a child jumping for joy, the fence painted | first seed |

I looked at the five before any reader had them and found nothing to mend. No picture was built again after a reading.

## What was read

**The jump beside a game of catch** (R2; tests 1 and 2, built under the first rule, the jumper's box 150 units to the side of the players). In both pictures the jumper stands just behind the catcher, clear of the ball's path, and both readers still tie the jump to the game: "Who will catch the ball: the woman is reaching for it, but the boy is jumping with his arms up, so he could be trying to catch it too." (1); "The jumping girl: could be cheering at a game, or jumping to try to catch the ball; she is not looking at the ball (eyes closed)." (2). A gap does not end the reading, so the rule became the other kind: the jump and the game of catch are never planned into one picture (`templates.JUMP53` X). That rule draws nothing a reader could be shown, so it has no test of its own; the two pictures are the reason for it.

**The jump alone** (test 5). "He has both arms raised, with his feet off the ground above his shadow, so he is jumping or cheering." No ball, and no second reading of what the jump is for.

**The larger square wheel** (R1; tests 3, 4 and 5). Named as the barrow's wheel, and square, in the barrow's own inventory line by all three: "Its wheel is a black square with a cross inside instead of a circle." (3); "On the right, where the wheel should be, is a square black wheel with spokes." (4); "Its front wheel is a square black frame." (5). Each goes on to say that it could not roll or is impossible. No crate, no grate and no reel. Two remarks on the drawing, one reader each: "It also looks a bit flat and pasted onto the side of the barrow." (3); "Also, the wheelbarrow's front leg seems to sit under the tray near the wheel, so its parts look jumbled." (4).

**The game of catch** (tests 1, 2 and 3). The thrower is the thrower in all three: "She has one arm stretched out after throwing the ball." (1). One reader has the catcher of test 2, a man in profile with his hands out toward the ball, facing the other way: "both hands raised forward as if to catch, but his body and face are turned to the right, away from the boy". On a crop of that figure at full size his face is toward the ball, the eye and the moustache on the ball's side of the head. It is one reader of one figure, and goes on the watch list.

**Every forced event was read**: fifteen of fifteen in the five pictures, the wet package ("The sprinkler is spraying it and drips are splashing off it.", 3) and the bread for the duck among them. That is a count of what a reader of mine wrote about a picture built to show it; it is not the screen.
