# Review: fig_08

## 1. Who and what
An older woman with grey hair in a bun and round glasses, in a teal jumper and blue-grey skirt, walking and holding a long thin red stick (a cane) out in front of her with both hands. Both versions read the same.

## 2. Faults in a
- Chest: a thin white strip runs down the front of the jumper with no clear meaning (a strap or part of the cane is not obvious).
- Hands: the two hands are bunched into a lump on the stick; the lower hand is a pale blob hanging below it.
- Mouth: a red smear right at the front edge of the face that looks like a mark rather than lips.
- Legs: long thin tubes with no knees; the back leg bends at an odd angle.
- Shoes: flat brown ovals.
- Jumper: a shiny highlight on the chest and arm makes it look plastic.

## 3. Faults in b
- Chest: the same unexplained thin white strip down the front.
- Hands: the same bunched hands with a blob below.
- Face: the profile is hard to read; the glasses sit at the very edge and the mouth is barely visible.
- Legs: the same tube legs with no knees and an odd back-leg bend.
- Shoes: the same flat brown ovals.

## 4. Differences
- Jumper: a has a glossy highlight and stronger shading; b is flatter.
- Mouth: a shows a red mouth at the face edge; b shows only a small dark mark.
- Glasses: in a the lenses sit a little further onto the face; in b they sit more at the front edge.
- Outlines: b's outlines look slightly more even in weight than a's.

## 5. Neck and head
- a: neck about right in length, slightly thick; head a little large for the body but suits the cartoon style.
- b: neck about right, slightly thick; head a little large for the body, the same as a.

## 6. Outline and shading
- a: dark outlines with glossy, rounded shading and highlights.
- b: dark even outlines with mostly flat colours.
- More like the things behind the figure: b (the floor is drawn in flat colours with plain lines).

## 7. Better drawn
b, by a little. Its flat colours match the scene and it has no shiny plastic look, though a's face is easier to read; the hand, leg and white-strip faults are in both.
