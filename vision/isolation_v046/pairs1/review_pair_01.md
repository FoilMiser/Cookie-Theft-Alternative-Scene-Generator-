# Review: pair 01 (garden scene)

At normal viewing size I could not see any difference between how the people and animals are drawn in a and b. The notes below therefore apply to both versions.

## People and animals in a
- Kneeling man (red shirt): his head looks large for his body, and his neck is a short, narrow join between head and shoulders.
- Kneeling man: there is a dark brown blob around his mouth that reads more like a smudge than a moustache or mouth.
- Kneeling man: his far arm, the one holding the trowel, is a thin bare-skin stroke. At this size it is hard to see where it joins the shoulder.
- Woman on bench (purple top): the sandwich and the bird on the bench overlap her lap and thigh, so the figure, the food and the bird run into each other.
- Woman on bench: her face is small and turned down toward the book, so her features barely show.
- Older man with box: the box sits in front of his body and his hands only touch its bottom edge. The box looks slightly pasted on rather than held.
- Older man with box: his sleeves are plain white with very little shading, which makes them look flatter than the brown vest and dark trousers.
- Bird on bench: it is small and partly hidden by the sandwich, so it is hard to read as a bird.

## People and animals in b
- I saw the same points as in a: the kneeling man's large head, short neck and mouth smudge; the woman's lap overlapping the sandwich and bird; the box looking pasted onto the older man; and the small, partly hidden bird.
- I saw no flaw in b that is not also in a, and none in a that b lacks.

## Belonging
No difference. In both versions the figures have the same dark outlines, flat colours and light shading as the house, fence, bench and plants.

## Easier to make out
No difference. In both:
- The woman reading on the bench, the man planting with a trowel and the older man (grey hair, cap, moustache) carrying a box are all equally easy to make out.
- The bird beside the sandwich is equally hard to make out.

## Choice
Either. I could not see a difference in how the figures are drawn between the two versions.
