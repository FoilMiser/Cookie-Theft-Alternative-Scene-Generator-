# Review: pair 05 (shopping street)

## 1. People and animals in a
- Man with camera (left of centre): arms raised to the camera are thin and bend at sharp angles. The near forearm and hand blur into the camera. The red camera strap hangs as a loose loop off the body. The trousers are long and the legs look slightly too long for the torso.
- Woman in yellow dress with ice cream (centre back): the arm holding the cone crosses in front of the chest and the other arm hangs close to the dress. The cone is held out at chin height, in front of the face. Neck is thin. The hand on the cone is small.
- Woman sweeping (right of centre): arms bend sharply to the broom handle and the hands are small knobs. The neck is short and the head sits low on the purple top. Legs in teal trousers are close together; one foot is mostly hidden by the broom.
- No animals in the picture.

## 2. People and animals in b
- Man with camera: drawn the same as in a (thin angular arms, hand merging with camera, loose strap).
- Woman in yellow dress: the ice cream is held at the mouth, partly overlapping the face, so it looks more like she is eating it. The arm is the same stiff crossing shape as in a.
- Woman sweeping: drawn the same as in a.
- No animals in the picture.

## 3. Belonging
No difference. In both versions the figures have the same dark outlines, flat colours and small ground shadows as the shops, tree, tables and stall. I see no difference in how the figures are drawn; they look like the same drawing in both.

## 4. Easier to make out
No difference overall. Man photographing, woman eating ice cream and woman sweeping are equally easy to identify in both. The only change I see is the ice cream: in b it is at her mouth, which reads slightly more clearly as "eating". In a it is held just in front of her chin, which is still clear. All three figures read as adults. None of them clearly reads as an older person or a child in either version.

## 5. Choice
Either. The two versions look essentially identical; if one must be chosen, b's ice cream at the mouth reads a touch more clearly as eating.
