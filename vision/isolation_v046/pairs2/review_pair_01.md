# Review, pair 01 (back garden: older man raking, older woman watering flowers, cat, bird)

At the size the pictures open, a and b look almost the same in how the figures are drawn. The clearest change is that the raking man faces left in a and right in b. The cat and bird are in different places, which is not being compared here.

## 1. People and animals in a
- Raking man: his face is small and turned to the side, so the moustache and features run together into a dark patch under the cap.
- Raking man: the white sleeve of the near arm crosses his chest up to his chin. The arm and hand are hard to follow, and it is not clear where the hand grips the rake.
- Raking man: the rake handle runs down in front of his legs and its head lies flat on the leaves. The rake head is small and hard to see as a rake.
- Older woman: the glasses sit out at the edge of her face in profile and look stuck on rather than worn.
- Older woman: her neck and the join where the shoulder meets the teal top are narrow and stiff. The arm holding the watering can is straight and tube-like.
- Cat: the legs are tucked and bunched under the body, so it is hard to count them. The face is a small blob with the eye at the very front, and it reads less clearly as a cat's face.
- Bird: no problems seen.

## 2. People and animals in b
- Raking man: the face is again small and dark under the cap, with the same run-together look.
- Raking man: both white sleeves bend forward to the rake handle. The arms read a little more clearly than in a, but the hands are still tiny.
- Raking man: the rake head and leaves at his feet are small and scratchy, like in a.
- Older woman: drawn the same as in a, with glasses at the edge of the face and a stiff neck and shoulder join.
- Cat: the same bunched legs and small face as in a.
- Bird: no problems seen.

## 3. Belonging
No difference. In both, the figures have the same dark outline and flat colour as the fence, house, swing and flowerbed, with the same soft ground shadows. I could not see any change in outline weight or shading between a and b.

## 4. Easier to make out
No difference overall. In both you can easily tell an older woman (grey bun, glasses, skirt) watering flowers and an older man (cap, grey hair) raking leaves. In b the man's raking is a little easier to read, because both arms visibly reach to the handle. In a his arm folded across his chest makes the grip less clear. The cat and bird are equally clear, or equally unclear in the cat's case, in both.

## 5. Choice
Either. The figures are drawn the same way in both as far as I can see. If one must be picked, b is slightly better because the man's grip on the rake is easier to read.
