## What I see
A sunny back garden: a cream house with a red roof and blue door on the left, a wooden fence with a gate across the back, trees and hedge behind, and a sun and two clouds in the sky. A girl or young woman in a yellow dress stands by the fence using a long-handled rake on a small pile of green and brown leaves. An older woman with grey hair in a bun, glasses, a teal top and a grey skirt stands at the edge of a paved path using a yellow-bristled broom to sweep a pile of orange, red and yellow leaves. Around them are a round flower bed with colourful flowers, a wooden bench, two potted plants, a birdhouse on a tall pole, some bushes, and an empty grey wheelbarrow and a teal watering can sitting on the grass in the foreground.

## Unsure or ambiguous
- The young figure could be read as a child or as a small adult, because she is drawn much smaller than the woman; this may just be distance. She could also be a girl or a young woman.
- Her rake head is small and sits at her feet, partly mixed in with her red shoes and the leaves, so the tool's head is hard to read at a glance; it could be taken for a plain stick at first.
- The older woman's broom head overlaps the leaf pile and the edge of the path, and she stands right on the line between grass and path. The pile of leaves sits half on the path.
- The wheelbarrow looks odd: its tray is a flat-sided grey shape, the wheel is on the left and the handles point right, and there is a short dark leg. The shadow under it is off to one side. It reads as a wheelbarrow, but it seems to be drawn small and low, with the parts not clearly joined.
- The birdhouse pole is very tall and thin and rises straight from the grass with a small green patch at the base; the house at the top is small. It could also be read as a lamp post or mailbox at first glance.
- The tree behind the house has its trunk going behind the fence, with the crown overlapping the house edge; that seems fine, but the crown is very close to the roof corner.
- Both figures' arms are bent with hands stacked on the handles, and each hand reads as a small round shape rather than a clear grip.
