# Picture 53

## Free description

This is a sunny back garden with a cream house with a red roof and blue door on the left, a long wooden fence behind, and a paved path and gate on the right. Three people are each doing a garden job. A woman with a dark ponytail, purple top and teal trousers holds a teal watering can over a flower bed, but the water looks wrong. Instead of falling onto the flowers, it rises from the spout in a curved arc, away from them and upwards, with drops at the very top, which looks impossible. At the back, a boy in a red T-shirt and blue jeans stands on the grass painting the wooden fence blue with a roller on a long pole, and a section of the fence is already blue. A tin of blue paint sits on the grass by his feet. In the front, a girl with short dark hair, a yellow dress and red shoes digs a hole with a spade, lifting a spadeful of soil next to a small mound of earth. A grey swing frame with a red seat stands empty on the right. Nobody seems to notice the odd water or to be talking to anyone else.

## Unsure or ambiguous

- The water could be read as water impossibly flowing upwards, which is my main reading, or as water squirting up and over before falling, like a fountain. But it starts low and only rises, and the drops at the top are not falling onto anything, so it does not look natural.
- The blue section of the fence could be read as just painted, or partly as a blue panel, because it looks like a flat block. It carries on behind the swing frame, further than the boy seems able to reach from where he is standing.
- The roller head reaches up above the fence top, so part of it seems to be in front of the sky and hedge rather than on the fence. He also stands some way out from the fence and seems to be painting with a long reach.
- The girl could be digging the hole or filling it in. The soil on the spade and the mound beside the hole could fit either, though her pose looks like digging.
- It is unclear whether the woman and the girl in the yellow dress are adults or older children, because they are drawn at similar sizes.
- Nothing passes through anything else apart from the fence and swing frame overlapping as expected.
