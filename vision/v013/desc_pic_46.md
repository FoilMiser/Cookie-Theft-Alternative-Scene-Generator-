# Picture 46

## Free description
This is a sunny back garden seen the other way round from usual: the house (red roof, blue door, window) is on the right, and the gate and stone path are on the left. The sun is top left, with two clouds. An older man with a grey beard, a grey cap, a white shirt, a brown waistcoat and dark trousers is painting the wooden fence blue with a long-handled brush. A few planks to his left are already blue, and a small tin of blue paint sits on the grass near him. At the front left, a woman with a dark bob, a yellow dress and red shoes is digging a hole in the lawn with a shovel, with a mound of earth beside it. On the right, a man in an orange cap, an orange high-visibility vest and blue jeans is holding a teal watering can over the flower bed by the house. Oddly, the water from the watering can shoots up in an arc, like a fountain, instead of pouring down onto the flowers, and drops fly upward at the top. A washing line between two posts in the middle holds a yellow-and-red striped towel and a pair of blue jeans, and there is a birdhouse on a pole behind the fence on the left.

## Unsure or ambiguous
- The water from the watering can goes upward, which is physically impossible for a watering can. Two readings: (a) it's a deliberate oddity (water flowing the wrong way), or (b) the water is meant to come from a hose and spray up. But the only hose is coiled on its reel with a short end, and the stream starts at the watering can's spout, so (a) is more likely.
- The older man's brush touches the top of the fence near the blue planks. It may be a paintbrush on a long handle or a roller. Either way, he is clearly painting.
- The birdhouse sits on a pole that appears to be behind the fence, possibly in a neighbouring garden.
- The man with the watering can has his face turned to the right towards the flowers, so I can't tell whether he has noticed the water going the wrong way.
