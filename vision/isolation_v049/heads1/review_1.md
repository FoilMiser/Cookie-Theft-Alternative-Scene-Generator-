# Head shape review, sheet_1 (rows A to E)

Reviewed from E:/Cognitive_Test_Scene_Creator/vision/isolation_v049/heads1/sheet_1.png only, at the size it is shown. Columns from left: woman front, woman turned, man front, man turned, man side, older woman, bearded man, child 1 (curly hair), child 2.

## 1. Row by row

**Row A.** The heads are round balls, widest near the eyes and closing to a round bottom with no jaw corner, so every face reads as a circle sitting on a stalk. The neck is a narrow, separate tube that meets the head at a pinch. Faults: on the woman front and woman turned the neck is drawn as a rounded lump under the face that reads like a double chin or swelling; on the older woman the neck is a thin stalk under a large head; on the man side the head is a ball with the neck stuck under the back of it.

**Row B.** The heads are broad ovals with soft, rounded jaws; the lower face is fuller than in A and the bottom of the head is a wide curve. The neck is short and fairly wide and meets the head along a plain curved line, so the join is tidier than A. Faults: on the man side the jaw slopes straight down into the neck with no chin point, and on the man turned the jaw line runs into the neck without a clear corner; the older woman's head is very wide at the cheeks.

**Row C.** The heads are boxier, with a broad, flattened lower edge and squared jaw corners. The neck is short and wide and sits under a nearly flat chin line. Faults: the squared jaw makes the man front and bearded man heavy and blocky; on the man turned the jaw corner is low and wide, so the face looks swollen at the bottom; the children get the same square lower face, which makes them look less childlike than in other rows.

**Row D.** The heads keep a rounded cranium but the jaw tapers clearly to a small rounded chin, giving a soft shield shape. The neck starts behind and below the chin with a separate line, so the head reads as sitting on the neck rather than merging into it. Faults: few; on the man side the neck is a little thin under the back of the skull, and the chin on the woman turned is small and close to the neck line.

**Row E.** Very close to D, but the jaw is longer and narrower and the chin comes to a sharper point. The necks are slightly longer and thinner. Faults: the sharper chin on the man front and man side makes the faces a little pinched and hard; the woman front's neck is a narrow column that looks thin for the width of her hair; the older woman's long neck and pointed chin make her face look gaunt.

## 2. Short answers

| Row | Grown figures look like grown-ups | Head has a chin |
|-----|-----------------------------------|-----------------|
| A   | No (round heads on thin necks read as toddlers or dolls) | No |
| B   | Partly | Partly |
| C   | Partly (heavy, but adult on the men; children also look older) | Partly (flat, wide) |
| D   | Yes | Yes |
| E   | Yes | Yes |

## 3. Ranking, best to worst

1. **D** - clear chin and jaw taper with a clean head-to-neck join, adult without losing the soft, friendly look.
2. **E** - same clear structure as D, but the sharper chin and thinner necks make several faces pinched or gaunt.
3. **C** - the jaw gives the men an adult weight, but the blocky square bottom is heavy and spills onto the children.
4. **B** - the neck join is tidy, but the soft oval jaw has no real chin, so adults look moonfaced and the side view is weak.
5. **A** - ball heads on stalk necks, no chin anywhere, and lump-like necks on the woman; adults read as children.

## 4. Choice

**Row D.** It is the only row where every grown figure has a visible jaw and chin while the curves stay rounded and soft, so the style stays friendly. The neck sits clearly under the head in front, turned and side views, which keeps the figures clear to read. Because the adults gain a defined chin and the children keep round, short faces, D gives the best separation between grown-ups and children; E is a close second but its sharper chins push it away from friendly.
