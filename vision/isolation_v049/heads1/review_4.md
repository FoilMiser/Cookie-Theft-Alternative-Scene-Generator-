# Review of heads1/sheet_4.png

Column order used below: 1 woman front, 2 woman turned, 3 man front, 4 man turned, 5 man side, 6 older woman, 7 bearded man, 8 girl child, 9 turned child.

Note: this review is based only on the sheet at its saved size. Small details such as line joins at the neck are near the limit of what can be seen, and the review says so where that matters.

## 1. Row by row

**Row A.** The heads are large, wide and almost round, with the lower face curving in a full, soft arc and no change of direction at the jaw. The heads sit almost straight on the shirt collar. Any neck is a very short stub, so the head looks set on the shoulders rather than carried. Faults: on the man front (3) and the bearded man (7), the round lower face and missing neck make them look like big toddlers. On the man side (5), the back of the head runs into a broad neck block with no clear nape. The children (8, 9) have the same head shape and neck as the adults, so nothing tells them apart except the clothes.

**Row B.** The heads narrow into a V, ending in a small pointed chin, and sit on a narrow, clearly drawn neck. This gives a light-bulb or lollipop look. The neck is much narrower than the jaw, and the head looks balanced on a stalk. Faults: the neck is too thin for the adult men (3, 4, 7). On the bearded man (7), the beard hangs from the point of the chin like a tassel. The children (8, 9) also get pointed chins and thin necks, so they look older and sharper than children should. The woman turned (2) shows a long, thin neck between the hair and the shirt.

**Row C.** The heads are broad and boxy. The jaw is wide and nearly flat across the bottom, with rounded corners. The neck is short and almost as wide as the jaw, so the head looks heavy and sits low. Faults: the bearded man (7) has a jaw so wide and flat that his beard reads as a band across the bottom of the face. The older woman (6) and the children (8, 9) get the same square jaw, which makes the children look stocky and the older woman look heavy-jawed. On the man turned (4), the jaw corner makes a hard angle close to the collar.

**Row D.** The heads are oval. The cheeks taper fairly gently to a rounded but clear chin, and there is a neck of medium width that is narrower than the jaw. The head meets the neck cleanly under the jaw line, and the neck meets the collar in a plain way. Faults are small. On the man side (5), the jaw line runs down at a steep slant into the neck, which is acceptable but a little abrupt. The children (8, 9) are only slightly rounder than the adults, so the age difference depends mostly on head size and clothes.

**Row E.** This row is close to D, but the lower face is a softer U shape and a little wider, so the chin is broader and less marked. The neck is medium to wide and joins under the jaw without trouble. Faults: on the man front (3) and the bearded man (7), the wide U-shaped jaw blurs the chin, so the chin reads less clearly than in D. The children (8, 9) look almost the same as in D.

## 3. Ranking, best drawn to worst drawn

1. **D**: oval heads with a clear rounded chin and a sensible neck, so the adults read as adults and the head-to-neck join is clean on every figure.
2. **E**: this has the same sound build as D, but the wider U-shaped jaw makes the chin less distinct on the men.
3. **C**: the adults clearly read as grown-ups, but the boxy, flat-bottomed jaw has no real chin and makes the older woman and the children look heavy.
4. **B**: there is a definite chin, but the V-shaped face on a thin stalk neck looks fragile on the men and makes the children look sharp and older.
5. **A**: round heads with no jaw, no chin and almost no neck make every figure, adults included, look like a baby or toddler.

## 4. Choice

**Row D.** It is the only row where the head has both a clear chin and a neck narrower than the jaw on every adult, so the men, the women and the older woman read as grown-ups at a glance. The rounded, gently tapered outline also stays soft and friendly rather than square (C) or pointed (B). One weakness: in D the children's heads are not much rounder than the adults', so a slightly rounder, shorter-necked child head would make children read as children more strongly.
