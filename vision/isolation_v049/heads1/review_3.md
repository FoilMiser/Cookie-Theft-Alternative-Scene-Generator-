# Review of heads1/sheet_3.png

Reviewed from the one image only, at the size it is saved. Rows C, D and E differ only a little at this size, so the fine points between them are less certain than the points about A and B.

## 1. Head shape and the join to the neck

**Row A.** Every head is a wide, rounded box that is almost as wide at the bottom as at the top. The bottom edge is broad and flat. Almost no neck shows: the head sits straight on the shirt. Faults: the front man and the bearded man look like heads with no neck. On the side-view man the goatee sticks out as a small bump at the lower front corner, and there is no jaw behind it. The older woman's head runs straight down into her collar.

**Row B.** The heads are egg shapes that narrow to a small rounded point at the bottom. Each one sits on a thin, separate neck tube, with a round bulge where the chin meets the neck. Faults: the necks are too thin for the heads, so every figure looks like a balloon on a stick. The turned woman's neck is long and thin. The bearded man's goatee sits on a pointed chin above a narrow neck. The two children get the same pointed chin as the grown-ups.

**Row C.** The heads are wide at the cheeks and taper gently to a broad, soft bottom. The neck is short and wide, and only a little of it shows under a line at the chin. Faults: the chin is hard to tell from the jaw, so the front man and the bearded man look a little square and heavy. On the turned and side-view men the neck is only a short stub below the jaw.

**Row D.** The heads taper from the cheeks to a soft but clear rounded chin. A neck of believable width shows below it, a bit narrower than in C. On the side-view man the back line of the neck runs down from behind the ear, so the head and the body join. Faults are small: on the turned man the jaw corner is faint. The children's heads look much like the grown-ups' heads, with only the size to tell them apart.

**Row E.** The heads have the most clearly drawn jaw: the sides run down at an angle to a rounded chin. A full-width neck joins under the jaw, and on the turned and side-view men a jaw line runs from the ear toward the chin. Faults: on the side-view man that jaw line and the neck line meet at a sharp corner, which looks a little hard for a friendly cartoon. The bearded man's beard hangs below the chin onto the neck, so his chin shape is hidden.

## 3. Ranking, best to worst

1. **E**: the clearest jaw, chin and neck of any row, so the grown-up figures read as grown-ups at a glance.
2. **D**: nearly as clear as E, with a softer, friendlier chin, but the jaw is weaker on the turned figures.
3. **C**: the head and neck join well enough, but the chin melts into a broad jaw and the men look heavy.
4. **B**: there is a chin, but the thin stalk necks and the bulge under the chin look odd on every figure.
5. **A**: no neck and no chin, so grown-ups and children have the same blocky doll head.

## 4. Choice

**Row E.** Of the five rows, its drawing shows grown-ups most clearly: each grown figure has a drawn jaw, a defined chin and a real neck, and the children's smaller, rounder heads stand apart from them. It stays simple enough for a friendly cartoon. One thing to fix: the sharp jaw-to-neck corner on the side-view man should be softened. If E then looks too hard, D is the fallback; it is friendlier but tells age a little less clearly.
