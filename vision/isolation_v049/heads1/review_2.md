# Review of sheet_2.png: head, jaw, chin and neck (rows A to E)

Based only on what is drawn in sheet_2.png. The children (columns 8 and 9) look almost the same in every row, so the differences between rows are mainly in the seven grown figures.

## 1. Row by row

**Row A.** The heads are soft, rounded ovals, and the lower face ends in a narrow rounded point. There is almost no neck: the chin sits straight on the collar line, so the heads look like they rest on the shirts. Faults: on the man seen from the side (column 5), the goatee and chin point forward with nothing under them, so the head looks like it floats on the shoulders. On the bearded man (column 7), the beard runs down into a dark brown block that reads as a beard and neck merged into one shape.

**Row B.** The skull is large and the face narrows sharply to a small pointed chin, which gives a light-bulb or inverted-pear shape. Under the chin is a thin, plain cylinder of a neck, clearly separated from the jaw. Faults: on the man from the front and turned (columns 3 and 4), the neck looks like a tube or mannequin peg. The big-skull, small-chin proportions make the woman (column 1) and the older woman (column 6) look young and bobble-headed.

**Row C.** The heads are wide and nearly round, with a broad rounded jaw and a soft, flattish bottom. A short, wide neck sits under a gentle jaw line. Faults: the jaw is so round and full that the chin does not stand out from the cheeks, most of all on the woman from the front (column 1) and the older woman (column 6). On the turned woman (column 2), the jaw curve merges into the hair.

**Row D.** The heads are oval and taper to a defined, slightly squared chin. The jaw line is clear, and a short neck, narrower than the jaw, leads down to the collar. This gives a readable head-on-neck join on every grown figure. Faults: on the man from the side (column 5), the chin and goatee jut forward a little sharply. The turned woman (column 2) has a fairly pointed chin.

**Row E.** The heads are broad and boxy, widest at a squared jaw with a flat chin. The neck is very short and nearly as wide as the jaw, so the heads look heavy and set low on the shoulders. Faults: the man from the front (column 3) and the bearded man (column 7) look blocky and stern. The square jaw on the turned man (column 4) and the man from the side (column 5) is heavy, and the ear and jaw crowd together.

## 3. Ranking, best drawn to worst drawn

1. **D**: Clear jaw, a defined chin and a believable short neck give the most solid head-on-neck construction across all views.
2. **C**: A neck is present and the heads are friendly, but the round jaw hides the chin.
3. **E**: The heads read as adult and the joins are tidy, but they are blocky and heavy, with almost no neck.
4. **B**: There is a real chin and neck, but the bulb-shaped skulls and peg necks make the adults look like children.
5. **A**: With no neck, the heads sit on the collars, the side view floats, and the beard merges into a neck block.

## 4. Choice

**Row D.** It is the only row where every grown figure has both a clear chin and a visible neck, which makes the adults read as adults. The heads keep a soft oval shape, so the style still feels friendly rather than stern as in row E. The children's round heads stay clearly different from the adults' tapered, chinned heads, so children still read as children.
