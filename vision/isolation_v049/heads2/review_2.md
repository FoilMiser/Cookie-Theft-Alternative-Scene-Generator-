# Head shape review, pair 2

Seen only in pair_2.png at the size it is given. The differences between the rows are small at this size, so several calls below are close.

## 1. How the rows differ

Row a draws the lower face narrower: the cheeks taper inward to a smaller, more defined chin, and the jaw has a visible angle on the turned and side views. Row b draws the lower face fuller and rounder, with a broader, softer chin and less of a jaw corner, so the heads read more like circles. Hair, eyes, mouths, beards and clothes look the same in both rows.

## 2. Figure by figure (left to right)

1. Woman, front: no difference. Both faces are a smooth oval under the hair; row b is a touch rounder at the chin, which I can barely see.
2. Woman, turned: a. Row a's chin comes to a clear point under the mouth, which makes the turn read; row b's rounder jaw makes the turned face look slightly puffy.
3. Man, front: no difference. Both are broad, rounded heads with the same neck width; the jaw outline is close to identical at this size.
4. Man, turned: a. Row a shows a jaw line running down to a defined chin, which reads as a grown man; row b's chin is softer and rounder and the face looks younger.
5. Man, side: b. In row a the long, sloping underside of the jaw makes the head look tipped back with a jutting chin; row b's rounder lower jaw sits more naturally on the neck, though both side views look a little awkward.
6. Older woman: a. Row a's narrower lower face gives her a more mature look under the glasses; row b's fuller, round cheeks make her look plumper and a little younger.
7. Bearded man: a. Row a's tapering jaw gives the beard a firm chin to sit on; row b's wider, rounder jaw makes the head look more like a ball with a beard on it.
8. Child: no difference. Both are a small round head with a short neck and read clearly as a child in either row.

## 3. Faults

Row a:
- Man, side: jaw underside slopes steeply, so the head looks tipped back and the chin juts.
- Man, turned: chin is quite sharp; at a smaller size it could look pinched.
- Woman, turned: pointed chin is close to too sharp for the friendly style.

Row b:
- Man, turned: soft round chin makes a grown man look young.
- Older woman: round, full cheeks reduce the sense of age.
- Bearded man: wide rounded jaw makes the head look ball-like.
- Woman, turned: fuller jaw looks slightly puffy on the turn.
- Adults overall: heads sit closer to the child's round shape, so the adult/child difference is weaker.

## 4. Choice

Row a. Its narrower jaws and defined chins keep the grown-ups reading as grown-ups while the child stays round, so the age difference is clearer, and it stays friendly. The one place row b is better is the man's side view. How sure: toss-up. The differences are small at this image size and could fall the other way on a closer look.

Counts: a 4, b 1, no difference 3.
