# Review of pair_5.png (head shape, jaw, chin, neck)

## 1. How the rows differ

Row a draws the lower face broad and rounded: the cheeks stay wide down to a blunt, rounded chin, and the jaw has some width where it meets the neck. Row b tapers the lower face to a narrower, more pointed chin, giving egg- or V-shaped heads. The child is drawn almost the same in both rows; the difference is clearest on the three views of the man and on the bearded man.

## 2. Figure by figure (left to right)

1. Woman, front: no difference. Both rows show the same soft oval face, and the slightly narrower chin in b doesn't change how she reads.
2. Woman, turned: b. In b the cheek runs in one smooth curve to the chin, while in a the jaw makes a small hard corner where it meets the neck.
3. Man, front: a. The broader, blunter jaw in a reads as a grown man, while the tapered chin in b narrows and softens the face.
4. Man, turned: a. The jaw in a has width and a clear turn toward the ear, while in b it slopes to a point and the face looks thinner and younger.
5. Man, side: a. In profile, a shows a rounded chin over a believable jawline, while b's chin juts forward to a sharper point.
6. Older woman: a. The fuller, rounder lower face in a suits the friendly granny look, while b makes her face narrower and more pinched below the glasses.
7. Bearded man: a. The beard sits on a broad chin in a, while in b the narrow chin turns the beard into a small goatee hanging off a pointed face.
8. Child: no difference. Both rows draw the same round head with a short chin, and I can see no change that matters.

## 3. Faults

Row a:
- Woman, turned: a small sharp corner where the jaw meets the neck.
- Man, front: the jaw is wide and slightly boxy, which looks a little heavy next to the soft features.
- Older woman: the face is very round, close to the child's proportions apart from the hair and glasses.

Row b:
- Man, front: the pointed chin makes him look softer and younger than the other grown men.
- Man, turned: the face thins toward a point, so the head reads less like an adult's.
- Man, side: the chin points sharply forward in profile.
- Older woman: the narrow lower face looks pinched under the wide glasses.
- Bearded man: the narrow chin shrinks the beard to a goatee and weakens the adult look.

## 4. Choice

I would choose row a. Its broader jaws keep the men and the older woman reading as grown-ups. With the child drawn the same in both rows, row a also gives the clearer gap in age between the adults and the child. Row b's tapered chins drift toward a younger, softer look. Row b only does better on the turned woman. I am fairly sure.
