# Review of pair_1.png (heads, row a vs row b)

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the heads are widest at the cheekbones and taper to a narrow, rounded chin, so the faces read as egg or bulb shapes sitting on a thin neck. In row b the jaw stays wider further down and ends in a broader, flatter chin, which gives the men in particular a squarer, heavier lower face. The difference is clear on the three views of the young man, slight on the bearded man, and hard to see at this size on the two women, the older woman and the child.

## 2. Figure by figure (left to right)

1. Woman, front: no difference. Both rows show the same soft oval face with a gently rounded chin; I can't see a meaningful change in jaw or neck.
2. Woman, turned: no difference. The cheek-to-chin curve and the neck look the same in both rows.
3. Man, front: b. Row a tapers to a small chin under a wide cranium, which looks boyish, while row b's broader, flatter jaw reads as an adult man.
4. Man, turned: b. In row b the jaw keeps its width to a defined chin under the moustache, while row a narrows to a soft point that looks weak and younger.
5. Man, side: b. Row b has a visible jaw line running from below the ear to a defined chin, while in row a the jaw curves up sharply into a small, rounded chin with a deep notch at the neck.
6. Older woman: no difference. The face under the glasses and the neck look the same in both rows at this size.
7. Bearded man: b. The difference is slight, but row b's jaw is a little wider at the beard, which suits a heavy-set grown man better than row a's narrower taper.
8. Child: no difference. Both rows show the same broad, round face, short neck and small chin.

## 3. Faults

Row a:
- Man, front: bulb-shaped head with a wide top and a small, narrow chin, which makes him look younger than a grown man.
- Man, turned: the jaw narrows to a soft point, so the lower face looks weak and the moustache sits on very little chin.
- Man, side: the jaw curves up steeply behind a small, rounded chin, and the deep notch where the jaw meets the neck looks pinched.
- Bearded man: the jaw tapers a little more than suits his broad build and big beard.

Row b:
- Man, side: the jaw is a long diagonal wedge that looks a little heavy, and the neck still joins the head awkwardly.
- Man, front: the square jaw sits on a fairly thin neck, so the head looks slightly top-heavy at the bottom.
- Women (front and turned) and the older woman: no visible change from row a, so they gain nothing from row b.
- Child: the head shape is not set apart from the adults any more than in row a, so the child still reads as a child mainly from size, hair and proportions rather than from the jaw.

## 4. Choice

Row b. For a friendly, clear cartoon style in which grown-ups must read as grown-ups, row b's broader jaws make the men read as adults. Row a's tapered chins push them toward looking boyish. Row b doesn't harm the women or the child, and it stays soft enough to look friendly. How sure: fairly sure. The gain shows mainly on the men, and the other four figures look the same in both rows.
