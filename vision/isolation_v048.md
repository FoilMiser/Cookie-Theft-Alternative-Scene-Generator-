# v0.48 isolation bench

The brief for v0.48 was Sam's: "go for v0.48 now. continue refining the character art, focusing on a couple of individual characters first in blender and really making them look good, then also fixing things like the quality of the animals and their limbs. I am not a fan of the squashed oval heads, and the faces need fixing. feel free to update the art style to make it easier to make the characters look better, and make sure to check for quality as you are working. I have blender installed so feel free to open that and do work there." The list beside it is `flaws_v047.md`: R1 `about_to_trip`, which failed v0.47's gate on a walker read as going the other way, and O1, which way a side-view figure faces.

This file has five parts: the study of two characters and the changes that came of it, the three blind pair benches that judged the people, the direction bench, the animals and their two pair benches, and the four rounds of single events cut out of scenes.

## The study of two characters

The two are the woman in the yellow dress (`person.mei`) and the man in the red shirt (`person.ben`). Each was built alone in Blender with a change and rendered at 800 px per metre, twice the release's size, as a sheet of heads with a row at scene size under it (`tpdb_work/v048/tryh.sh`; a build takes about 5 s and four renders 5 s). What v0.47's drawing showed on the first sheet: a head wider than tall (its half width, depth and height 1.04, 0.98 and 0.96 of its radius), a face small in the lower middle of that oval, a fringe straight across the brow and a square corner at each temple on every head of hair, a smile drawn as an angular red shape with a dark edge, and a nose ringed like a letter U with a blob. The changes, each behind a switch whose letter O gives v0.47's drawing:

- **The head** (`TPDB_HEAD48` B, `castlib/core.py`): an egg, taller than wide (0.92, 0.97, 1.06 of the radius), narrowing below the cheeks by 0.34 at the chin and carried forward 0.05, so there is a jaw from the front and a chin ahead of the neck from the side. One map (`head_pt`) places the head, its hair, its hats and every feature of the face.
- **The face** (`TPDB_FACE48` B, `castlib/face.py`): the eyes a little smaller and just under the middle of the head, the brows longer with a clear gap over the eyes and finer on the women and the children, the nose and the mouth lower, the mouth one plain line with no dark edge under it.
- **The hair** (`TPDB_HAIR48` B, `castlib/people.py`): the shell is cut along the zero line of a smooth field, so a hairline is a curve: a parting, a temple that rounds down, a short sideburn, the ear clear on a short cut, a fuller crown. The two women with long hair are parted differently, in the middle and at the side.
- **One flat tone** (`TPDB_FLAT48` B, `build_cast.py`): no shadow tone. Laid by a surface's normal, the second tone left a small island wherever a surface folds away, at a trouser's crotch, on a child's shorts, under a jaw.
- **The body, from the pair benches below:** a tee hemmed at the hips and not at the waist (`TPDB_TORSO48` B), standing feet turned out 16 degrees (`TPDB_STANCE48`), the chin a little lower and a child's neck fuller, the women's bare arms slimmer, glasses of a lighter wire with an arm to the ear.

Looked at on my own sheets before any reviewer: all ten people, front and three-quarter; all nineteen expressions on the woman from the front and on the man at three-quarter; one man in all 77 poses from the side.

## The people's pair benches

A pair is one figure of v0.47's cast (`assets/cast/out047`, the renders pixel-equal to v0.47's gallery) beside the same figure of a trial, as "a" and "b" with the new one drawn at random and the map kept outside the round folder until the reviews were in. The same sixteen figures went to four fresh reviewers each time: standing from the front and at three-quarter, sitting with a book, and eight side views. A reviewer said which is the better drawn and listed the faults of each (`tpdb_work/v048/figpairs48.py`; `isolation_v048/figs1`, `figs2`, `figs3`).

| Bench | Trial | New the better | v0.47's the better | No difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the head, face and hair | 10 | 3 | 3 |
| 2 | bench 1's mends, and the side view's head turned 15 degrees | 9 | 6 | 1 |
| 3 | bench 2's mends: the side view's own face | 12 | 3 | 1 |

**Bench 1.** Of the new drawing from the front: "The face reads clearly as a smiling woman with a simple nose and mouth, and the hair falls naturally, whereas a's looped nose and open mouth look odd and the straight fringe is helmet-like; the body is otherwise the same." The three for v0.47 were at the neck: "the neck is a short block with a dark outline running across under the jaw, so the head looks pasted onto it", and a child's "long and thin like a stalk, so the head looks perched on a stick". Named of both drawings alike, and mended after it: "the shirt is very short and the trouser waist very high, so the upper body looks short against long legs" (the tee's hem), "a faint lighter triangular mark on the far thigh, like a stray patch" (the shadow tone), a moustache "very large and curled, sitting over an open red mouth; slightly comic".

**Bench 2.** The direction bench (below) had turned a side view's head 15 degrees toward the viewer, and the reviewers of enlarged figures gave five of the six losses to that head or to a beard drawn as a tone: "no eye is visible, so the face looks empty and could be read as the back of the head", "the single eye sits on the very front edge of the face outline, partly cut off", "the beard is a soft darker-brown patch on the cheek and jaw with no edge, so it reads more like a shadow or dirt than a beard"; and of the man from the front, "no mouth under the moustache, so the face is a little blank". The mend is a face of its own for a side view (`TPDB_PROF48` B): as a draughtsman does in a profile, the near eye and its brow are set further round the cheek, the mouth and the moustache likewise, and nothing of the far eye is drawn; the head turns 22 degrees. The beard went back to a dark one with its line, on the chin, under a short moustache.

**Bench 3.** "It has ears, a neck and more balanced torso-to-leg proportions; a's capped hair, missing neck, very long legs and grimacing mouth look less right." Of a kicking child from the side: "the profile head looking down at the foot fits the kick and the face is placed consistently on the head; a's face looks pasted onto the side of the head". The three for v0.47 were side views still: a pose that turns the head itself had not been given the side view's face, and "the glasses hang at the very edge of the head and look partly off the face". After it: the side view's face for every pose whose head is within about 30 degrees of profile, the side view's own near rim and arm for glasses, smaller ears set further back, and the older woman's bun larger ("the bun on top is very small and hard to see, so the hair could read as a cap or helmet"). These last were looked at on my own sheets and not put to a fourth pair bench.

## The direction bench

`flaws_v047.md` asked for the walker alone at 0.41 of full size and one question, which way is he walking. Eight moving figures (two walkers with a book, two walkers, a runner, a kicker, a dog walker, a child with an ice cream) were each drawn three ways: A v0.47's drawing, B the new head turned 48 degrees toward the viewer as v0.47 turns it, C the new head turned 15. The 24 pictures, half of them mirrored, were each read by two fresh readers, who answered which way the figure goes, which way its face is turned and how sure they were (`tpdb_work/v048/dirbench.py`; `isolation_v048/dir1`).

Every one of the 48 answers has the way the figure goes, in all three drawings. What differs is how sure the reader was: "sure" of 4 of 16 for A, 6 of 16 for B and 11 of 16 for C. Of the turned heads: "the face is close to three-quarter so it is slightly less clear", "torso is drawn fairly square to the viewer, which weakens it a little". A figure alone at this size is an easier reading than the screen's, where the one misread stood in a scene, so the bench shows a direction and not a cure. The head's turn went to 15 degrees and, after pair bench 2, to 22 with the side view's own face.

## The animals

The animals were drawn again in `castlib/animals.py` behind `TPDB_ANIM48` (B; O builds v0.47's, and with `TPDB_FLAT48` O all 60 of v0.47's gallery animals rebuild to the pixel). Dogs: one body from a rounded rump through a narrow loin and a deep chest into a thick neck; a shoulder, an elbow, a wrist and a paw, and a hip, a stifle, a hock and a paw, each on a bone of its own; a smaller skull with a muzzle that tapers from it; a tapered, curved tail; a collar that is a band of the neck's own surface. A sitting dog sits on its haunches with the hind legs folded, a begging and a reading dog sit up on them, a lying dog lies on its belly. Cats: a longer, supple body, a broad skull, large three-sided ears, a long tail, and a bright iris with a slit pupil, which stays open in every face but the sleeping one. The goat: a deeper barrel, slim legs on split hooves, a wedge face. The birds and the duck keep their drawing, without the line where a head or a neck passes into the body.

Two pair benches, v0.47's drawing in the release's flat tone beside the new one (`isolation_v048/figsA`, `figsB`):

| Bench | Pairs | New the better | v0.47's the better |
|---|---|---|---|
| A, the first drawing | 20 | 17 | 3 |
| B, the dogs and cats after A | 15 | 14 | 1 |

**Bench A.** "it reads clearly as a sitting dog with a proper haunch, hind leg and separate tail, whereas a has a ramp-shaped body with no hind quarters and an over-long neck"; "it reads as a proper sitting cat with a haunch, folded hind leg, neck and separate tail, whereas b has a detached ball head, no hind quarters and a tail fused into a ramp-shaped body"; of the duck, "it is the same drawing but without the lines across the neck, so the duck looks like one body rather than parts stacked together". The three for v0.47 were all the tan dog standing or walking: "all four legs are very long and thin for the body, giving a stilt-like, spidery look", and its muzzle a "long, boxy pale snout reads like a muzzle guard or a held object rather than a dog's nose and jaw". Its legs were shortened and thickened, the hock opened, the muzzle shortened and tapered, the collar narrowed; the cats' legs were shortened and thickened too, after round 1 below.

**Bench B.** The three pairs lost in A all went to the new drawing: "Its legs and hip join cleanly and the body reads as solid and balanced, whereas b has a jagged hip line, thin stiff front legs and an awkward hind leg kink." The one for v0.47, the dog on the hose, now errs the other way: "all four legs are thick, straight columns, rather heavy and stumpy for the body; the hind leg has almost no hock bend". It was left there.

## Single events, cut out of scenes

A case is one event forced alone into a picture on one seed and cut out of it (`vision/isolation_bench.py`, the cases named `*wN_*`); a reader writes what it sees and what it is unsure of. Round 1 had the events with no dog in them, built before the dog's second drawing; rounds 2 and 3 the walker about to trip; round 4 the animals' events with the final cast.

**Round 1, seventeen cut-outs, nine readers.** The thief bird, the stalking cat (twice), the ducks, the fed birds, the goat, the ice cream, the kick, the reader on the bench and the runner were read as built. Of the cats: "The cat's back legs are long and spindly and the body is long and thin; the pose reads as stalking, but proportions are a bit stretched."; the cat on the fence, with the closed eyes of its happy face, "only one pointed ear and a blob of head show; no face is visible"; the orange cat asleep, "An orange cat (or possibly a fox)". Those three went back to the drawing. The walker about to trip, four cut-outs with v0.47's stride and gap: the way she goes read in three, and in the fourth "she is actually walking to the right, away from it, which makes the relation unclear" of a walker who faces left; the trip plain in one and hedged in two, "The box is some distance ahead and to the side of her path, so it is unclear whether she is actually heading toward it or will walk past it."

**Round 2, six trip cut-outs, six readers: a walk that was not taken.** The stride was redrawn as the step itself, the leading foot lifted with its toe up and the rear leg straight under the body, and the obstacle set at that toe. Five of the six readers had the walker standing: "The pose reads two ways: walking forward while reading, or standing still with legs apart and leaning slightly forward."; and the lifted foot at a ball was "resting his foot on it or about to kick it while he reads". The long stride is what says walking, and it was kept.

**Round 3, the same six seeds, six readers.** The long stride, two short motion lines behind the walker's back, as the runner has had three since revision 17, and the obstacle half a step from the leading toe (`templates.TRIP48`). All six readers have the walker walking the way he goes, and all six name the lines: "Motion lines behind her show she is moving." All six have the trip: "directly in his path, so he seems about to trip over or bump into it because he is not watching where he goes". What the lines cost: "The speed lines suggest she is moving faster, maybe almost jogging." Both readers of a ball offered a kick beside the trip, so a ball is no longer one of the obstacles.

**Round 4, eighteen cut-outs with the final cast, nine readers.** Read as built: the thief bird, the stalking cat (twice), the cat on the fence, the sleeping cat with no fox, the begging dog (twice: "sits up on its hind legs on the grass next to the bench, looking up toward her, as if begging for the ice cream"), the playing dog, the reading dog (twice), the dog that wants its walk, the dropped ice cream (twice) and the walked dog (twice). In one of the two walks the walker was again taken to go the other way: "The woman and dog walk in opposite directions (she faces right, the dog faces left), so the leash is pulled between them". The stopped hose was hedged as it was in v0.47, and the sleeping dog, small and far back, was not read: "A small white animal stands or crouches in the middle of a green lawn, seen from the front." Nothing was changed after this round.

## What the bench leaves open

- A walker who faces left was taken to go right in one cut-out of round 1 and one of round 4, and in v0.47's screen. The trip now has its motion lines; the other walkers have none.
- The side view's face is a draughtsman's cheat and was judged in enlarged pairs and in cut-outs, not yet in a screen.
- The dog on the hose lost its pair, and the hose's reading is hedged as before; the sleeping dog was not read at the size and angle the bench's seed gave it.
- The last mends to the people (glasses and moustache in a side view, the ears, the bun) and the cats' second drawing were looked at on my own sheets and, for the cats, read in round 4; they had no pair bench of their own.

## The blind rule

Every reviewer and reader was a fresh subagent given its prompt and its pictures and nothing else; the prompts say to write with the Write tool and not to list the folder. From pair bench 2 on a reader was given the path of its prompt file and told to open nothing but that file and the pictures it names. The maps that say which picture is which drawing are in `tpdb_work/v048/maps/` and were not in a round's folder while it was read.
