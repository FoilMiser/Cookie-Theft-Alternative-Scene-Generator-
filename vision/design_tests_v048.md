# v0.48 design tests

The cast of builder 0.48.0 (the egg head, the new face and hair, the side view's own face, one flat tone, the animals drawn anew) and the placement rules of revision 45 were read blind in whole test scenes (`tools/cycle/design_tests.py`, the v0.48 jobs), each picture by a fresh reader with the reader template, one picture to a reader, on Opus. The bench (`isolation_v048.md`) had read the figures one at a time and in pairs; this is the first time a reader had them at a picture's size, among other things. Between them the eight forced sets hold every person of the cast, every animal that was redrawn (both dogs, both cats, the goat, the birds) and the templates of the v0.47 list that this release worked on or carries: `about_to_trip` (R1), `blocked_hose` (R2), `lost_balloon` (R3), `dog_reading`, `bird_steals` and `cat_stalks_bird`. `design_tests_v048/map.txt` names each picture's record; it lay outside the folder until the readings were in.

## What was read

**Every forced event was read**: twenty-four of twenty-four in the eight pictures. That is a count of what a reader of mine wrote about a picture built to show it; it is not the screen, where a scorer who has not seen the picture marks each item against the description.

**The walker and the box** (R1, test 2). This is the template that failed two gates on a walker read as going the other way. "an older man in a grey cap, brown waistcoat and dark trousers is hurrying along to the right (motion lines behind him) with his eyes down on an open book he holds in both hands." And the box: "Directly in front of his feet, on the pavement, there is a cardboard box, and because he is reading he does not seem to see it, so he looks about to trip over it." The direction came with no hedge; the hedge was on what happens next: "It is not clear whether he will actually trip or just stop in time; he seems about to trip." The two motion lines of revision 45 were read as speed: "walking fast or hurrying, though his pose could also be read as already starting to stumble".

**The dogs' walkers** (the direction again, tests 1 and 2). v0.47's reader of its street picture had the dog's walker with a stride that "could also be read as walking right". Here both were told plainly, one each way: "She is walking left and holding the dog's lead." (1), and "walking to the right holding a red dog leash in both hands" (2). Neither has motion lines; both have the side view's own face.

**The dog on the hose** (R2, test 3; not worked on in this release, only drawn with the new dog). "a tan dog with a red collar is standing right on top of the hose (at what looks like a joint or bulge in it) next to the flower bed, so it seems the dog is pinching off the water." The hedge is v0.47's, the swelling and the cause: "there is a darker bulge or connector where the dog stands", and "it could also just be a dog standing near a hose that is turned off at the reel". The holder's face was read: "peers into or at the nozzle with a frowning, puzzled look; no water is coming out of it".

**The reading dog** (test 4). "a brown dog with a red collar sits on its haunches and holds an open newspaper in its front paws, looking at it as if reading, and it seems to be wearing glasses or eye markings." And: "That dog reading a newspaper is the oddest thing in the picture, since dogs cannot read." The glasses were the unsure part, as in v0.47: "could be glasses (a dog wearing reading glasses) or just darker fur patches around the eyes".

**The bird at the sandwich** (test 4). "a blue bird is perched on the bench right beside the sandwich, leaning toward it, as if it is about to peck at or steal the sandwich while the man is not looking." Under what was unsure: "it could be about to grab/peck the sandwich (stealing it while the man reads), or simply sitting next to it".

**The cat and the bird** (test 5). "an orange cat is crouched low, stalking toward the right with its tail stretched out behind it." And: "It seems the cat is about to pounce on the bird." The hedge: "The cat could be read as simply walking rather than stalking, but its low crouch and direction toward the bird strongly suggest hunting."

**The lost balloon** (R3, test 6; not worked on). "The yellow balloon's string hangs loose and ends in mid-air, a little above the child's raised hand, so it looks as if the child has just let go and is about to lose it." The second reading is v0.47's: "Either the child has just let go and it is drifting away, or the child is reaching up to catch it."

**The begging dog and the dropped ice cream** (test 8). "A brown puppy with a red collar sits up on its hind legs in front of him, begging and looking up at his ice cream." And: "Her pink ice cream with sprinkles has fallen onto the pavement beside her, and a white dog with brown spots is licking it up." The child "stands holding an empty ice-cream cone and rubbing her eye with her other hand, as if crying".

**The animals, drawn anew.** Every one was named for what it is and what it does, at a picture's size: "a white goat with curved horns and a red collar is grazing on a tuft of grass; it is tied by a rope to a small wooden stake in the ground" (5); "a white dog with brown spots crouches low with its front legs stretched out, like a play bow, next to a yellow-green tennis ball, and wag lines show its tail moving" (6); "A grey cat is walking along the top of the fence, balancing on the pointed fence boards." (7); "a small brown puppy with a red collar is lying on the grass, apparently asleep with its eyes closed" (7); the birds "seem to be pecking at the food" (1). No reader named a leg, a paw or a muzzle as wrong. Two remarks are on an animal's drawing. Of the sleeping dog, which the bench's fourth round could not read when it was small and far: "The puppy's body is drawn slightly compact/blobby, so its exact pose (lying on its belly vs curled up) is a bit hard to read." Of the goat's tether: "it appears to run from the collar to the stake, though at one point it also passes near/over the goat's leg".

**The cast.** The older woman's glasses and bun were named in both of her pictures, with no doubt on either: "an elderly woman with grey hair in a bun and glasses" (1), "an elderly woman with grey hair in a bun, glasses, a teal cardigan and a grey skirt" (2). v0.47's reader had the glasses "tiny" and was "not certain"; pair bench 3 had the bun as "a cap or helmet" before it was made larger. The older man: "an older man in a grey cap, brown waistcoat and dark trousers" (2), "an older man with grey hair and a grey cap, wearing a brown vest and dark trousers" (3), "an old man in a grey cap and brown vest" (8). None of the three wrote of his moustache, which v0.47's readers had named. The man in the red shirt, the study's second character, was a man in both of his pictures, "a man in a red T-shirt and blue trousers" (2) and "a man with brown hair in a red shirt and blue trousers" (4), and neither reader wrote of a mark at his mouth, where v0.47's had "ice cream/chocolate smeared on his face" (O1). The teenager: "a boy in a red cap, blue hoodie and dark trousers" (6). The children: "a child with dark skin and curly dark hair, wearing a yellow top and orange shorts" (4), "a small girl in a pink dress" (8), and in test 5 the child in pink with a doubt: "she could be a girl or a small adult woman; she looks like a child to me".

**Three grown people were read young.** The woman in the yellow dress, the study's first character, was "a woman in a yellow dress" carrying the box (3) and "a girl in a yellow dress" feeding the birds (1). The bearded man in green was "a man in a green shirt and beige trousers" running (8) and "a boy in a green shirt" reading on the bench (1); neither reader wrote of his beard. The woman in the purple top: "A woman (or older girl) with dark hair tied back, wearing a purple top and teal trousers" (7), and under what was unsure, "The person watering could be read as an adult woman or a teenage girl." v0.47's tests had the same of the man in the red shirt and of the woman in purple, so it is not new with the egg head, but it now shows on the first of the two figures the study was about. It is the first thing on the next list for the people.

**Faces.** The standing rule is that the pose carries the meaning and not the face. Where a reader did write of a face, the face agreed with the pose: "a frowning, puzzled look" (3), "The child's face looks worried or sad." (6), "happily eating a pink ice-cream cone of his own" (8).

No reader named a head's shape, a neck, a shoulder, a stiff leg or a shadow. Two remarks are on a person's drawing: "The woman's lead hand and the lead line look a little disconnected near her hands, but it reads as her holding it." (1), and "The running man's rear foot/leg pose is slightly awkward, but not clearly wrong." (8).

## For the watch list
