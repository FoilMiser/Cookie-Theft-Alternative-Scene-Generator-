# v0.24 isolation bench

Each visual was forced alone into a picture, cut out with little else around it and read blind by fresh readers (the method of `isolation_v023.md`). Round 1 was six readers and twelve cut-outs (`isolation_v024/round1`), round 2 two readers and four cut-outs (`isolation_v024/round2`); each `map.json` says which cut-out is which case. The cases are `teen*`, `see*` and `sli*` in `isolation_bench.py`. With one or two readings per candidate these are choices between drawings, not measurements; the whole scenes (`design_tests_v024.md`) read the winners again.

The bench casts were built with `TPDB_TEEN` (A, B) and `TPDB_SLIDE` (U, H); the rider offsets are `draw.RIDE24`.

## The teenager

In the v0.23 screen no reader called the teenager a teenager: "a woman with a brown ponytail", 13 items in all, credited because the accepted answers cover the action. Each candidate is shorter (0.88, was 0.92) with a larger head (1.09, was 1.05). Two more were tried and dropped by eye before any reader saw them: a mop of hair, which looked like curls, and a first backpack whose straps sank into the hoodie.

| Case | Drawing | What the readers said |
|---|---|---|
| `teenO_1`, `teenO_2` | v0.23: ponytail, hoodie | "A cartoon person with brown hair in a ponytail" twice; no age offered |
| `teenA_1` | **A**: short hair, a red cap worn backwards | "A boy or young man" |
| `teenA_2` | **A** | "Could be a teenager or a slim adult"; "a cap worn backwards, or ... facing the wrong way ... by mistake" |
| `teenB_1` | B: ponytail, an orange school backpack | "A cartoon girl or young woman"; the pack "could be a second ball or a disc ... or a shield-like object" |
| `teenB_2` | B | "Could be a girl, a teenager or a woman"; the pack "could also be a separate oval thing such as a float or a cushion" |

**Chosen: A.** It is the only candidate read as young without being read as a woman, and it adds no new object to misread. Cut out of its picture the v0.23 teenager was not called a woman either, so the whole scenes are the real test. Worn caps now keep the teenager out of the windy-hat template, which casts only someone bareheaded, and off that picture's flying cap colour; the bucket on the head already hid any cap.

## The slide's arms

Not on the v0.23 list, and found here: all three round 1 readers said the raised arm merges with the face, "holding their head or hair" or "covering the face or eyes (upset, scared ...)". The v0.23 readers had said the same in the pictures with this pose ("holding her head or hair"). In profile, arms raised out to the sides lie across the head. Round 2, with the seat at 0 px:

| Case | Arms | What the readers said |
|---|---|---|
| `sliU_1`, `sliU_2` | U: both up and ahead, clear of the face | "Going down it ... as if waving or cheering"; "a happy 'wheee' gesture"; the hand edge-on "a stick, pencil or twig"; "I can only see this one arm" |
| `sliH_1`, `sliH_2` | H: hands down, holding the chute | "Apparently sliding calmly"; "I cannot tell whether she is sliding down or just sitting still" |

**Chosen: U**, then refined for what its readers reported: the palms turned so the hands open toward the viewer, and the arms apart so both show. The first refinement, with the near arm steeper, lay across the face again and was rejected by eye; a child's head is large enough that the near arm clears the face only if it passes under the chin, so it now does, with the far arm higher behind the head. This final drawing was not read on the bench; the whole scenes read it first.
