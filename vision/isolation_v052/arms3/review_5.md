# Review of pair_5: upper arms, shoulders, sleeves (row a vs row b)

## 1. How the rows differ

Row b gives the upper arms a wider root where they meet the body, so the raised and reaching arms grow out of the shoulder rather than out of a narrow joint. In row b the older woman's raised sleeve ends in a visible cuff. Her other shoulder is squarer, with a seam line in place of a puffy cap. The side-view teenager's arms in row b angle slightly upward, and the far arm starts higher on the shoulder. The sweeping man changes least: his sleeves are a little longer and broader at the shoulder in b.

## 2. Figure by figure

1. **Teenager waving: b.** In row b the raised arm is wider at the top and joins the shoulder line smoothly. In row a it narrows into a lump that sits low on the chest beside the hood strings.
2. **Older woman reaching up: b.** Row b's raised arm is fuller where it leaves the shoulder and ends in a clear cuff. Her lowered arm starts at a clean square shoulder, while row a gives it a puffy, rounded cap.
3. **Older man sweeping: no difference.** In both rows the near shirt sleeve comes out of the vest armhole as a rounded tube and crosses the body in much the same way. Row b's version is a little longer and stiffer, and row a's a little thinner, but neither is clearly better.
4. **Teenager with both arms out, side view: b.** In row b the far arm starts higher, near the neck and shoulder, and both arms tilt slightly upward, so they read as reaching. In row a they are flat, parallel bars that look like they come out of the middle of the chest.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Teenager waving: the raised upper arm narrows to a rounded lump low on the chest, overlapping the hood strings. The arm looks attached to the chest, not the shoulder.
- Older woman: the raised sleeve is thin where it leaves the shoulder and has no cuff, so the sleeve and hand run together. The lowered arm has a puffy rounded shoulder cap that looks padded.
- Older man: the near sleeve starts at a small pill-shaped cap inside the vest armhole, which looks stuck on. The sleeve behind the broom bulges at the shoulder.
- Side-view teenager: both arms are flat, parallel bars. The near arm's rounded root sits well below the shoulder on the torso front, and the far arm seems to come out of the chest, not the shoulder. The pose reads stiff, like a sleepwalker.

### Row b
- Teenager waving: the rounded lump where the raised arm joins the torso is still visible near the hood strings. It is smaller than in a but still reads as a joint drawn on the chest.
- Older woman: the square shoulder and seam on her lowered arm are a little hard, but correct. No real fault.
- Older man: the near sleeve is a long, straight, stiff tube from shoulder to lower hand, with almost no elbow. Where it leaves the flat-topped vest armhole, the shoulder bulges.
- Side-view teenager: the near arm still starts at a rounded cap partway down the torso, not at the shoulder. The hood bump behind the neck is large and crowds the far arm's root.

### Other things that look wrong (both rows)
- Teenager waving: the lowered hand is very large next to the sleeve.
- Older woman: her lowered hand is a pale, flat shape that is barely drawn.
- Older man: both hands are brown mitten shapes with no clear grip on the broom handle.
- Side-view teenager: the hands are cupped claw shapes, and the white hood strings show at the front of the chest between the arms.

## 4. Choice

Row b, fairly sure. It is clearly better on the waving teenager, the older woman and the side-view teenager, and about the same on the sweeping man.
