# Review of pair_8: upper arms, shoulders and sleeves

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the shirts mostly stop at the body: the arms come out of a hard shoulder corner or from under the shirt edge, and only a few of them have a narrow, small sleeve. In row b every arm has a clear short sleeve with a visible hem, and the shoulders are rounder and fuller, so the shirts read as T-shirts with arms coming out of them.

## 2. Figure by figure

1. **Man waving: b.** In b the raised arm and the lowered arm both come out of a red sleeve with a hem, while in a both arms look bare and seem to be stuck onto a sleeveless shirt with a pointed shoulder corner.
2. **Woman reaching up: b.** In b the raised arm has a full rounded purple sleeve and the lowered arm has a clear hem line, while in a the raised sleeve is a thin purple stub and the lowered shoulder is a puffy bulge with only a faint crease where the sleeve should end.
3. **Child pointing up, from the side: b.** In b the hanging arm comes out of a yellow sleeve with a hem, while in a the arm is drawn flat on top of the shirt with no sleeve, like a cut-out pasted onto the side of the body.
4. **Man with both arms raised: b.** The difference is small, but in b the raised right arm (viewer's right) has a fuller sleeve that fits the shoulder, while in a that sleeve is narrow and tapers into the arm like a tube.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Man waving: no sleeves on either arm; the raised arm comes out of a sharp shoulder corner, and the lowered arm comes out from under the shirt edge, so the shirt looks sleeveless and the arms look stuck on.
- Woman reaching up: the raised arm's sleeve is a thin purple band, much narrower than the shoulder; the lowered arm has a large puffy shoulder with no clear sleeve end, only a short crease.
- Child: the hanging arm sits on top of the yellow shirt with no sleeve and no shoulder joint, so it reads as a flat cut-out.
- Man with both arms raised: the right sleeve is narrow and tapered; the left shoulder has stray crease lines and an unclear join where the arm leaves the shirt.

### Row b
- Man waving: the raised sleeve is long and stiff, running a good way up the upper arm; the small crease line at its base is a bit untidy.
- Woman reaching up: the raised sleeve is large and round, a little balloon-like compared with the thin forearm.
- Child: the sleeve is fine; the shoulder itself is still boxy.
- Man with both arms raised: the right sleeve is a thick red band that looks a little heavy, and the left shoulder bulges outward.

### Other faults, in both rows
- Child: the pointing arm seems to grow out of the cheek or jaw instead of a shoulder; only a sliver of yellow shows behind the neck. The pointing hand looks like a stick or baton rather than a hand with a finger.
- Man with both arms raised: the left arm crosses right over the face and hides an eye, so at small size he looks like he is shielding his face or saluting, not raising both arms.
- Man waving: at small size the raised hand reads more as a blob than an open waving hand.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. Its clear sleeves and rounder shoulders make the shirts and arms read cleanly and look friendlier. Row a's bare, stuck-on arms look unfinished. The gap is clear on the large drawings but small at picture size.
