# Review of pair_10: upper arms, shoulders and sleeves

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the sleeves are short, rounded puffs that bulge out over the shoulders like small balloons. The arm comes out from under the puff, narrower than the sleeve, so the shoulder line is sloped and pinched. In row b the sleeves are plain T-shirt sleeves with a straight, angled hem partway down the upper arm. The shoulders are broader and squarer, and the upper arm runs on from the sleeve at almost the same width.

## 2. Per figure (left to right)

1. **Man from the front: b.** Row b's sleeves read as an ordinary T-shirt with a clean hem, while row a's puffed caps look like balloon sleeves, with the arm tucked under them.
2. **Man turned: b.** The near sleeve in b has a crisp hem and a smooth shoulder; in a it is a lumpy puff with the arm sticking out from beneath it.
3. **Man from the side: b.** Row b draws a clear sleeve shape over the top of the arm with a visible hem. In row a the sleeve is only a couple of crease lines on the torso, and the arm's rounded top seems pasted onto the shirt.
4. **Woman from behind: b (narrowly).** Row b's sleeves and shoulders are clean and readable; row a's puffs balloon out and pinch the arm. But b makes her shoulders and upper arms very broad and heavy.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Man from the front: the sleeves are puffed balloons wider than the arm, and the arm starts underneath them, so the shoulder looks pinched and sloped.
- Man from the front: at the sleeve-to-arm join, small pale or pinkish patches show (the arm's top edge peeks out). It looks like a fill or overlap error.
- Man turned: the near sleeve is a lumpy puff, and the arm comes out of it at a kink.
- Man from the side: there is no real sleeve over the arm, only seam lines on the torso. The top of the arm is a rounded stub laid on the shirt, so the arm looks stuck on rather than attached at the shoulder.
- Woman from behind: the puffed sleeves with angled seam lines look like costume puff sleeves. The arms come out narrow beneath them.
- Small figures: the puffs still show as bumps at the shoulders, which makes the figures look hunched or padded.

### Row b
- Woman from behind: her shoulders are very wide and square, and her upper arms are thick. Next to her hips she looks top-heavy and more masculine than in a.
- Man from the front: the upper arms are fairly thick and the shoulders broad, so he looks a little bulky. This is acceptable for a cartoon.
- Man from the side: the sleeve is a flat rectangle drawn over the torso. It reads clearly, but it is somewhat stiff and slab-like.

### Other things that look wrong (both rows)
- Man turned: the far arm comes out from behind the torso with no far shoulder or far sleeve drawn, so that arm seems to start mid-back.
- Man from the side: the hand ends over the trouser front and overlaps the leg outline in an untidy way.
- Woman from behind: the hair is a heavy dark helmet-and-block shape that sits over the collar like a separate solid object.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. Its plain T-shirt sleeves and continuous shoulder-to-arm line are cleaner and friendlier, and they read better at picture size. The main thing against it is how bulky it makes the woman's shoulders and arms.
