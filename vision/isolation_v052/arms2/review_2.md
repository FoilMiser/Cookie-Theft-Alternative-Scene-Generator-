# Review: arms2 / pair_2

## 1. How the rows differ

In row a the sleeves look like separate flat pieces stuck on: a raised arm comes out of a long, narrow sleeve tube that runs up the arm, and a hanging arm comes out of a square flap with a hard hem line and a step in the torso outline. In row b the shoulders are rounded caps that are part of the shirt, the sleeves on raised arms are shorter, and the hanging arms come out from under a curved sleeve edge. Row b reads softer and fuller at the shoulders. Row a reads narrower and more angular.

## 2. Figure by figure (left to right)

1. **Man waving: b.** In b the raised arm comes out of a rounded sleeve cap and the hanging arm tucks under a curved shoulder. In a there is a flared sleeve and a notch in the left side of the torso where the sleeve meets the shirt.
2. **Woman reaching up: b.** In a the purple sleeve on the raised arm is a long tube that climbs beside the chin, so the shoulder looks hiked up, and the hanging arm's sleeve is a squared flap. In b the raised sleeve is short and the left shoulder is a natural round shape.
3. **Child pointing up, side view: no difference.** The b sleeve is a little shorter and rounder than the boxy sleeve in a, but the change is small, and the main problem is the same in both: the raised arm seems to grow out from behind the head.
4. **Man with both arms raised: b.** In a the red sleeve on the right arm is a long tube that reaches far up beside the head. In b the sleeve is shorter and the shoulders are rounded, so the arms seem to start from the body and not from the ears.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Man waving: the raised-arm sleeve flares out sideways like a cap stuck on, and the left torso outline has a visible step or notch below it. The right sleeve is a thin strip, and the hanging arm looks as if it starts beside the torso, not from a shoulder.
- Woman reaching up: the raised-arm sleeve is a long purple tube rising to chin height, which makes the shoulder look shrugged and too high. The left sleeve is a square flap with a hard hem line, which gives a boxy shoulder.
- Child: the sleeve is a long box with a straight hem, and the arm comes out low on the body.
- Man, both arms up: the right sleeve is a long red tube up to head level, so the shoulder looks dislocated upward. The left shoulder has folded, crumpled sleeve lines and a narrow, sloping torso top.

### Row b
- Man waving: the rounded shoulder bump on the raised side sits a little high, which gives a slight hunch.
- Woman reaching up: there is a small kink in the raised arm where the sleeve ends, as if a bend sits just above the shoulder. The raised sleeve still starts at about chin height.
- Child: the same "arm from behind the head" problem as row a. The rounded cap makes the sleeve a little puffy.
- Man, both arms up: the rounded shoulders make the shirt look barrel-shaped and puffy, and the right sleeve still starts near head height.

### Other things that look wrong (both rows)
- Man with both arms raised: the near arm crosses right over his face and covers an eye and part of the head. It reads as a face-block, not as raising both arms.
- Child: the raised arm has no visible shoulder or sleeve. Only a yellow sliver shows between the head and the shirt, so the arm seems to come out of the back of the neck. The pointing hand is a narrow tube with a stick finger and does not read as a hand.
- Man waving: the head is turned while the body faces front, and the neck is very thin next to the wide shoulders.
- At picture size (the small figures), the two rows are hard to tell apart. The child's raised arm and the man's face-covering arm are still the faults that stand out.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. Its rounded, shorter sleeves and shoulders that belong to the shirt look friendlier and clearer than the tube sleeves and square flaps of row a. Its puffiness is a smaller fault than row a's hiked-up shoulders.
