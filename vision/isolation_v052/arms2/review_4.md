# Review of pair_4: upper arms, shoulders and sleeves

## 1. How the rows differ

Row a gives every figure a boxy T-shirt: square shoulder corners that stand out past the arm, and sleeves that end in a straight, outlined hem with the arm coming out underneath. Row b rounds the shoulders off and makes the sleeves shorter and puffier. The sleeve has no outlined hem, and the rounded top of the arm is drawn over the bottom of the sleeve. At the small whole-figure size the two rows look almost alike. The square shoulders of row a and the rounded caps of row b are the only difference you can still make out.

## 2. Figure by figure

- **Man from the front: b.** Row a's square shoulder corners stick out like shoulder pads, while row b's rounded shoulders and slightly wider upper arms read as a natural, softer body.
- **Man turned: a.** Row a's near sleeve has a clear angled hem, but row b's near sleeve turns into a bulbous puff and the arm seems to be glued onto its underside.
- **Man from the side: a.** Row a shows a plain sleeve with a straight hem over the arm, while in row b the round top of the arm sits in the middle of the red shirt like a stump plugged into the body, with no sleeve edge.
- **Woman from behind: b.** Row b's rounded shoulders and short sleeves make a softer, friendlier back view, while row a's flat-topped, square-cornered shoulders look stiff and too wide.

Count: a = 2, b = 2, no difference = 0.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Man from the front: the shoulders end in hard square corners that sit wider than the arms, so the shirt looks padded or stiff.
- Man from the front: there is a small wedge of skin colour with no outline at each armpit, between the sleeve hem and the torso.
- Man turned: the far sleeve on the right has no visible hem, and the far arm just appears from behind the torso edge.
- Man turned: the near shoulder is a flat, squared slope.
- Woman from behind: the shoulder line is nearly flat with square corners, so her shoulders look broad and boxy for the figure.
- Woman from behind: the sleeves stick out sideways past the arms like little flaps.

### Row b
- Man from the front: the sleeve bottoms have no hem outline, so where the sleeve ends and the arm begins is less clear. There is still a small skin-coloured notch at the armpit.
- Man turned: the near sleeve is a large, lumpy puff, and the upper arm overlaps its lower edge, so it reads as a puffed sleeve rather than a T-shirt.
- Man turned: there is a pale, unoutlined patch where the near arm meets the sleeve.
- Man from the side: the arm ends in a rounded, outlined cap partway down the shirt. It looks like a doll's arm socketed into the torso, and nothing shows where the sleeve ends.
- Man from the side: the sleeve seam lines above the arm now lead nowhere.
- Woman from behind: the sleeves are puffy and somewhat bell-shaped, and the arms come out from under them with no hem line.

### Other things that look wrong (both rows)
- Man from the side: the line of the trouser leg runs through the hand, and the thumb sticks out over the trousers. The arm also hangs in front of the body rather than from the shoulder.
- Man turned: the near hand is a large blob over the trouser front, and the far hand has an odd spiral or curl line inside it.
- Man from the front: the hands are mittens with a small curl mark that does not read clearly as a thumb.
- Woman from behind: the hair is a dark helmet with a hard horizontal seam across it, and the lower block looks like a separate box rather than hair falling down.

## 4. Choice

**Row a, a toss-up.** Row b's rounded shoulders are friendlier on the front and back views. However, its sleeve-and-arm joins in the turned and side views (the puff and the socketed stump) are the clearest faults on the sheet. Row a's boxy sleeves are stiff, but they always read plainly as a T-shirt with arms coming out of it.
