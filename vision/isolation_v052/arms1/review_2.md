# Review of pair_2 (upper arms, shoulders, sleeves)

## 1. How the rows differ

Row a draws the upper arms as separate puffy tubes. Each shoulder is a round bump that sits up beside the neck, and the long sleeves balloon outward and gather into a cuff at the wrist. Row b joins the arm to the body with a sloping shoulder line, and its sleeves are straighter, plainer and open at the wrist (no cuff band). The worker's short sleeve is also longer and wider in b.

## 2. Figure by figure

1. **Teenager, hooded top (front): b.** In a the shoulders bulge up like shoulder pads on either side of the hood, which makes him look shrugged; in b they slope down naturally from the hood into the sleeves.
2. **Older woman, cardigan (turned): b.** In a the near sleeve swells into a balloon and pinches into a cuff, so it reads as a puffed costume sleeve; in b it is a clean, slightly widening cardigan sleeve with a visible shoulder line.
3. **Older man, waistcoat (side): no difference.** The cream shirt arm coming out of the waistcoat armhole is almost the same in both rows: a round shoulder cap and a long, even tube down to the hand.
4. **Worker, safety vest (turned): b.** In a the grey sleeve is a small cap with a round knob sticking out above the vest at the shoulder; in b it is a proper T-shirt sleeve that comes out from under the vest and slopes off the shoulder.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Teenager: both shoulders bump up above the line of the chest, like pads or a shrug, and leave a pinched notch where they meet the hood.
- Teenager: the sleeves are uniformly inflated, sausage-like tubes, and the cuffs are thick bands that make the wrists look bandaged.
- Older woman: the near sleeve balloons out well past the body and gathers sharply into a cuff, so it looks heavy and old-fashioned and hides the shape of the arm.
- Older woman: the far arm has no visible sleeve; only the hand shows, sticking out past the skirt.
- Older man: no elbow or bend in the long shirt arm; it is a stiff, even tube (shared with b).
- Worker: the sleeve is a very short cap and the shoulder makes a round knob that pokes out above the vest's armhole, so it reads as a lump rather than a shoulder.
- Worker: the vest's left armhole edge has a rough, scratchy outline.

### Row b
- Teenager: the sleeves widen slightly toward the wrist and end in a flat cut with no cuff, so they look a little stiff and boxy.
- Teenager: the arms hang straight down and are set wide, with a small gap from the body, so the pose is rigid.
- Older woman: the sleeve flares a little at the wrist like a bell, and its back edge runs almost straight from shoulder to hip.
- Older woman: the far arm still shows only a hand past the skirt, with no sleeve.
- Older man: the arm is the same stiff, elbowless tube as in a, and the shoulder cap is a large round ball inside the armhole.
- Worker: the sleeve flares out somewhat wide and makes the near arm hang away from the body; the shoulder looks broad.
- Worker: the vest's left armhole edge has the same rough outline as in a.

### Other things that look wrong (both rows)
- Teenager: there are two mouths, a small curved smile and a separate brown line under it.
- Older man: the arm is very long and pale against the dark waistcoat, so it reads as a tube rather than an arm; the hand sits low against the trouser leg.
- Worker: the hands are drawn as curled shapes with a single line inside, which look more like mitts than hands at full size.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. Its shoulders slope into the arms and its sleeves are plain and readable. That suits a friendly, clear cartoon style better than row a's puffed, padded shoulders and ballooned sleeves. The difference is small on the man in the waistcoat and modest on the worker.
