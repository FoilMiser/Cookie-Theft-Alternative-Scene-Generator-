# Review of pair_7: upper arms, shoulders, sleeves

## 1. How the rows differ

Row a draws short, puffed cap sleeves. They are rounded at the shoulder and gathered into a narrow cuff that hugs the top of the arm, so the shoulders look sloped and the arms seem to come out from under a little balloon. Row b draws plain T-shirt sleeves. They flare out from a wider, squarer shoulder to a straight hem that stands away from the arm, so the shirt reads as an ordinary tee and the upper arm shows clearly below it.

## 2. Figure by figure

1. **Man, front: b.** The straight, flared T-shirt hems read cleanly at both sizes, while a's puffed, cuffed sleeves look like a blouse and pinch the arm.
2. **Man, turned: b.** The near sleeve in b is a clear tee sleeve with a visible hem, while a's near sleeve is a rounded puff that hides where the shoulder is.
3. **Man, side: b.** In b the sleeve is a distinct block with a straight hem across the arm, while a's sleeve is only a curved line inside the torso and the arm looks pasted flat on the shirt.
4. **Woman, back: a.** a's puffed sleeves stay close to the body. b's sleeves flare well past the arms and make her shoulders very broad and boxy, wider than the man's.

## 3. Faults

### Row a
- Man, front: the puffed sleeves with narrow cuffs look like a blouse, not a man's shirt. The shoulders slope and look narrow, and the arms look pinched where the cuffs grip them.
- Man, front: where each cuff meets the arm (left and right, around armpit height) there is a pale pinkish patch with no outline, so the join looks unfinished.
- Man, turned: the near sleeve is a rounded balloon, so the shoulder line cannot be read. The far side shows no sleeve at all, only a bare arm coming straight out of the torso edge.
- Man, side: the sleeve is only an inner curve and a cuff line drawn on the torso, with no outline of its own. The arm's rounded top sits flat on the shirt like a sticker.
- Woman, back: the puffs are fine for a blouse, but the cuffs are tight and the arms look thin where they come out.
- At picture size the puffs blur into rounded shoulder bumps, and the sleeve shape is hard to read.

### Row b
- Woman, back: the sleeves flare far out past the arms, so her shoulders become very wide and square, broader than the man's. That looks heavy for her build.
- Man, front: the shoulders are now quite wide and flat on top, which makes the torso a little boxy. A faint light patch may still show at the hem-to-arm join.
- Man, turned: as in row a, the far shoulder has no sleeve, only a bare arm from the torso edge.
- Man, side: the back edge of the sleeve merges with the torso outline, so the sleeve looks like a rectangle stuck on the front of the chest rather than wrapping the arm.

### Other things that look wrong (both rows)
- Man, turned: the far hand is drawn as an open ring outline that looks unfinished.
- Man, side: the hand lies on top of the trouser leg, and the thumb outline looks odd. The trouser front has a stray double line.
- Woman, back: the hair is a large dark helmet with a squared-off block below it. It reads as a hood or a bag more than as hair.
- Man, front: the trousers start very low under a long shirt, so the waist looks low.

## 4. Choice

**Row b**, fairly sure. Its T-shirt sleeves are clearer and friendlier, they read as a shirt at picture size, and they show the shoulder and upper arm well. The woman's sleeves in row b should be narrowed so her shoulders are not so broad.
