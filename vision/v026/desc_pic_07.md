# Picture 07

## Free description

This is a sunny back garden with a house with a red roof and blue door on the left, a wooden fence across the back, a gate and a paved path on the right. Near the centre at the back, a boy in a red cap, blue hoodie and dark trousers is standing with his back mostly to us, painting the fence blue with a long-handled roller, and he has finished a block of three boards or so. A paint tin and a paint tray sit on the grass by his feet. A white dog with brown patches and a blue collar is walking away to the right, with blue on its paws, and a line of blue paw prints runs across the grass from the paint tray to the dog, so it has clearly stepped in the paint and is spreading it around. The boy does not seem to have noticed. The rest of the garden is quiet and empty of people: a flower bed by the house, an empty bench at bottom left, a wheelbarrow and a green watering can on the grass at the front, a bush, and a tall bird box on a pole. Nothing is impossible here, but the dog's trail of prints looks like the small accident the picture is about.

## Unsure or ambiguous

- The blue section of the fence is painted with clean, square edges, and the wooden boards have been completely covered, including the rails. The block is so neat that it could be read as painted boards or as a separate blue panel fitted into the fence.
- The boy's cap and face: his head is turned to the left and the cap's peak points left, so I could not fully tell how much of his back or side we see.
- The paw prints start at the tray and go right to the dog. The first print is immediately under the tray, so it looks a bit as though it comes out from under it. It reads clearly, though, that the dog walked through the paint.
- The dog could be walking away unaware, or it could be running away from the boy after making the mess; it looks calm, so I would say the first reading.
- The roller head looks slightly offset from the handle and stands upright, and the tip of the roller seems to sit right at the edge of the painted area, so it looks like it is painting but is not clear whether it is at the moment touching the wood.
