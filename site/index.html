Inspection and blind screen

What v0.48 changed

Fresh readers, who never saw the scoring key, describe every picture the way a test participant would, and separate scorers mark each scored item as mentioned, half-mentioned, missed or misread. The screen has now run on forty-five releases, v0.4 to v0.48, and once more on the unchanged v0.9 pictures with fresh readers, to measure how much the readers themselves vary. v0.48 began from a study of two figures, the woman in the yellow dress and the man in the red shirt, built alone in Blender one change at a time. An egg head with a jaw and a chin, a plain line for a mouth, hair cut along a curve, a face of its own for a side view and one flat tone came of it for every person, and every animal has a new body and new limbs. Each was judged blind in pairs beside the drawing of v0.47 before it went into a scene. The walker about to trip, which failed the last two gates, is read in full in all five of its pictures, and no item of the screen was misread. The set's overall score is 0.993, from 0.991, and 63 of the 64 templates pass the gate, as in v0.47. The one that fails is the coming rain, where one reader named the storm clouds and did not go on to the rain; it heads the list for v0.49. The finer faces cost two readings: no reader named a moustache or a beard, and grown figures were called a girl or a boy more often. Nothing was mended, swapped or read again after the screen. With the 33 full marks that stand beside another reading halved, 55 of the 64 pass. All 19 pairs of a template and an angle an animal is drawn at pass. Probe pictures put every template in front of the readers at least three times, and every angle at least twice.

Combinatorics

How many different scenes the generator can make

Structures are template sets that fit a tier's plan and can reach its content band. Signatures are what the difficulty model sees (item types, chain lengths, scored setting entities); structures sharing a signature are candidate isomorphs. Totals multiply structures by cast assignments, expressions, facing, mirroring and optional features; positions are continuous and not counted.

Action library

actions, each with its own props and anchors

Lower-body postures combined with upper-body activities. Props are built for the pose and parented to the hand or head, so scenes can attach water, strings, leashes and ropes to the right point. These are the actions the templates use; more stay in the cast for future templates and are left out until one uses them. Earlier drawings that a refined pose replaced have been retired from the cast.
